What is the spectral theorem for symmetric matrices?	A symmetric matrix has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Writing them as columns of an orthogonal matrix S diagonalizes the matrix as S-transpose A S equal to a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues.<br><br>\[ A=S\Lambda S^{\top},\qquad S^{\top}S=I,\qquad \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n) \]	Numerik linear-algebra eigenvalues
How is the spectrum of a matrix computed?	As the roots of the characteristic polynomial, the determinant of A minus lambda times the identity. Each root is an eigenvalue.<br><br>\[ \det(A-\lambda I)=0 \]	Numerik linear-algebra eigenvalues
How do you find the eigenvector for a known eigenvalue?	Solve the homogeneous system where the eigenvalue times the identity is subtracted from the matrix. Every non-trivial solution is an eigenvector.	Numerik linear-algebra eigenvalues
What are the trace and determinant in terms of eigenvalues?	The trace is the sum of the eigenvalues and the determinant is their product. Both are invariant under similarity transformations.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{tr}(A)=\sum_i\lambda_i,\qquad \det(A)=\prod_i\lambda_i \]	Numerik linear-algebra eigenvalues
What does positive definite mean?	The quadratic form x-transpose A x is strictly positive for every non-zero vector x. Geometrically, the matrix never flips a vector past a right angle to itself.<br><br>\[ x^{\top}Ax\gt 0\qquad\forall\,x\neq 0 \]	Numerik linear-algebra positive-definite
What is the eigenvalue characterization of positive definiteness for a symmetric matrix?	All eigenvalues are strictly positive. The proof expresses an arbitrary vector in the orthonormal eigenbasis, which turns the quadratic form into a weighted sum of eigenvalues.	Numerik linear-algebra positive-definite
Why are the diagonal entries of a symmetric positive definite matrix positive?	Because plugging in a standard basis vector into the quadratic form returns exactly that diagonal entry, which must therefore be positive.	Numerik linear-algebra positive-definite
Why is A-transpose-A always symmetric and positive semi-definite?	Symmetric because transposing it gives itself back; positive semi-definite because the quadratic form equals the squared Euclidean norm of A times x, which is never negative.	Numerik linear-algebra
When is A-transpose-A positive definite?	When A has trivial kernel, equivalently full column rank. Then A times x is non-zero for every non-zero x, so the squared norm is strictly positive.	Numerik linear-algebra
What are the three axioms of a norm?	It is zero only for the zero vector, it is absolutely homogeneous under scalar multiplication, and it satisfies the triangle inequality.<br><br>\[ \|x\|=0\Leftrightarrow x=0,\quad \|\alpha x\|=|\alpha|\,\|x\|,\quad \|x+y\|\le\|x\|+\|y\| \]	Numerik norms
What is the p-norm and its important special cases?	The p-th root of the sum of the p-th powers of the absolute components. p equal to 1 gives the sum of absolute values, p equal to 2 the Euclidean norm, and the limit gives the maximum absolute component.<br><br>\[ \|x\|_p=\Big(\sum_i|x_i|^p\Big)^{1/p},\qquad \|x\|_\infty=\max_i|x_i| \]	Numerik norms
What is the norm equivalence theorem in finite dimensions?	Any two norms on a finite-dimensional space bound each other up to positive constants. Convergence therefore does not depend on which norm you use.	Numerik norms
What is an induced (operator) matrix norm?	The maximum of the norm of A times x over all unit vectors x. It measures the largest possible stretching factor of the matrix.<br><br>\[ \|A\|=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Ax\| \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the key compatibility property of an induced matrix norm?	The norm of A times x is bounded by the matrix norm times the vector norm. Every induced norm is compatible with the vector norm generating it.<br><br>\[ \|Ax\|\ \le\ \|A\|\,\|x\| \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is submultiplicativity of a matrix norm?	The norm of a product is at most the product of the norms. It follows directly from compatibility.<br><br>\[ \|AB\|\ \le\ \|A\|\,\|B\| \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the matrix norm induced by the maximum vector norm?	The maximum absolute row sum. Each row is summed in absolute value and the largest sum is taken.<br><br>\[ \|A\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j |a_{ij}| \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the spectral norm?	The matrix norm induced by the Euclidean vector norm. It equals the square root of the largest eigenvalue of A-transpose-A, that is, the largest singular value.<br><br>\[ \|A\|_2=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}\big(A^{\top}A\big)}=\sigma_{\max}(A) \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the spectral radius?	The largest absolute eigenvalue of a matrix. It controls the asymptotic behaviour of matrix powers and hence the convergence of iterative methods.<br><br>\[ \rho(A)=\max_i|\lambda_i|\ \le\ \|A\| \]	Numerik linear-algebra spectral-radius
What is the Frobenius norm and is it induced?	The square root of the sum of all squared entries. It is not an induced norm, because the identity matrix has Frobenius norm the square root of n rather than 1.<br><br>\[ \|A\|_F=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^2},\qquad \|I\|_F=\sqrt{n}\neq 1 \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
Why must every induced matrix norm assign 1 to the identity?	Because the identity leaves every vector unchanged, so the maximum stretching factor is exactly 1.	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the relation between the squared Frobenius norm and the trace?	The squared Frobenius norm equals the trace of A-transpose-A, which is also the sum of the eigenvalues of that product.<br><br>\[ \|A\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}\big(A^{\top}A\big) \]	Numerik norms matrix-norms
What is the condition number of a matrix?	The product of the norm of the matrix and the norm of its inverse. It bounds how much a relative error in the data can be amplified in the solution.<br><br>\[ \kappa(A)=\|A\|\cdot\|A^{-1}\|\ \ge\ 1 \]	Numerik conditioning
What does a large condition number tell you?	The problem is ill-conditioned: small perturbations in the input can cause large changes in the solution, regardless of the algorithm used.	Numerik conditioning
What is the condition number of a regular symmetric matrix in the spectral norm?	The ratio of the largest to the smallest absolute eigenvalue. A wide spread of eigenvalues means poor conditioning.<br><br>\[ \kappa_2(A)=\frac{|\lambda_{\max}|}{|\lambda_{\min}|} \]	Numerik conditioning
Is conditioning a property of the algorithm or of the problem?	Of the problem. Even a perfect algorithm cannot beat the inherent error amplification measured by the condition number.	Numerik conditioning
What is the power method (Potenzverfahren)?	Repeatedly apply the matrix to a starting vector and normalize after each step. The iterate aligns with the eigenvector of the largest absolute eigenvalue.<br><br>\[ x^{(k+1)}=\frac{Ax^{(k)}}{\|Ax^{(k)}\|}\ \longrightarrow\ v_{\max} \]	Numerik eigenvalues power-method
Why does the power method converge to the dominant eigenvector?	Expanding the start vector in the eigenbasis, each component is scaled by its eigenvalue to the power k. After factoring out the dominant eigenvalue, all other ratios are less than 1 and decay to zero.	Numerik eigenvalues power-method
How do you obtain the dominant eigenvalue from the power method?	Take the inner product of successive normalized iterates, or use the Rayleigh quotient. The scaling factor between iterates converges to the eigenvalue.<br><br>\[ \lambda_{\max}\approx\frac{x^{\top}Ax}{x^{\top}x}\qquad\text{(Rayleigh quotient)} \]	Numerik eigenvalues power-method
How is the smallest absolute eigenvalue computed?	Apply the power method to the inverse matrix. Its largest eigenvalue is the reciprocal of the smallest eigenvalue of the original.	Numerik eigenvalues power-method
What is the LU decomposition and what does it require?	A factorization into a lower triangular matrix with unit diagonal and an upper triangular matrix. It requires that all leading pivots are non-zero.<br><br>\[ A=LU,\qquad L\ \text{unit lower},\ U\ \text{upper triangular} \]	Numerik linear-systems lu
How does LU decomposition relate to Gaussian elimination?	It is Gaussian elimination bookkept: U is the resulting upper triangular matrix, and L records the elimination multipliers used at each step.	Numerik linear-systems lu
How is a linear system solved once the LU factors are known?	First solve the lower triangular system by forward substitution, then the upper triangular system by backward substitution. Both are cheap.	Numerik linear-systems lu
What is the computational cost of LU decomposition versus substitution?	The decomposition costs on the order of n cubed over 3 operations, while each substitution costs only on the order of n squared. The factorization dominates.<br><br>\[ \tfrac{n^3}{3}\ \text{(factorization)}\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad \mathcal{O}(n^2)\ \text{(substitution)} \]	Numerik linear-systems complexity
Why is LU decomposition efficient for repeated right-hand sides?	Because the expensive factorization is done once, and each new right-hand side only needs the two cheap substitutions.	Numerik linear-systems lu
What is LU with row pivoting and why is it needed?	Rows are permuted so that a non-zero (ideally the largest) pivot is used at each step. It is needed when a pivot vanishes and it also improves numerical stability.	Numerik linear-systems pivoting
What is the key theorem justifying pivoting?	If a pivot is zero in a regular matrix, some entry below it in that column must be non-zero. Otherwise the column would be a linear combination of previous ones and the matrix would be singular.	Numerik linear-systems pivoting
Why is the largest available entry usually chosen as the pivot?	To avoid dividing by a small number, which would amplify rounding errors and destabilize the elimination.	Numerik linear-systems pivoting
What is the Cholesky decomposition?	A factorization of a symmetric positive definite matrix into a lower triangular matrix times its own transpose. It is the symmetric analogue of LU.<br><br>\[ A=LL^{\top},\qquad A\ \text{symmetric positive definite} \]	Numerik linear-systems cholesky
What is the advantage of Cholesky over LU?	It costs about half as much, roughly n cubed over 6 operations, because symmetry means only one triangular factor has to be computed and stored.<br><br>\[ \tfrac{n^3}{6}\ \text{operations, about half of LU} \]	Numerik linear-systems cholesky complexity
What is the rational Cholesky decomposition?	A factorization into a unit lower triangular matrix, a positive diagonal matrix, and the transpose of the triangular matrix. It avoids taking square roots.<br><br>\[ A=LDL^{\top},\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_n),\ d_i\gt 0 \]	Numerik linear-systems cholesky
How does the Cholesky factor relate to the rational version?	The Cholesky factor is the unit lower triangular matrix multiplied by the square root of the diagonal matrix.<br><br>\[ L_{\text{Chol}}=L\,D^{1/2} \]	Numerik linear-systems cholesky
Why does each Cholesky elimination step preserve positive definiteness?	Because the reduced matrix is congruent to the original via an invertible transformation, and congruence preserves the sign of the quadratic form.	Numerik linear-systems cholesky
What is the general idea of an iterative method for a linear system?	Split the matrix into two parts, rewrite the system as a fixed point equation, and iterate. Each step must be cheap, so the part that gets inverted has to be simple.	Numerik linear-systems iterative
What is the Banach fixed point theorem?	A contraction mapping a complete space into itself has exactly one fixed point, and the fixed point iteration converges to it from any starting point.<br><br>\[ \|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|\le q\|x-y\|,\ q\lt 1\ \Rightarrow\ \exists!\,x^*=\Phi(x^*) \]	Numerik fixed-point banach
What is a contraction?	A self-map that shrinks distances by a factor strictly less than 1. In the differentiable one-dimensional case it suffices that the absolute derivative is bounded below 1 everywhere.<br><br>\[ |\Phi'(x)|\le q\lt 1\qquad\forall x \]	Numerik fixed-point contraction
What are the two things to verify when applying the Banach fixed point theorem?	That the map sends the set into itself (self-mapping), and that it is a contraction on that set. Both are needed, not just the contraction property.	Numerik fixed-point banach
What is the a priori error estimate of the fixed point iteration?	The error after k steps is bounded by the contraction factor to the power k times the initial error. This lets you predict how many iterations are needed in advance.<br><br>\[ \|x^{(k)}-x^*\|\ \le\ \frac{q^{k}}{1-q}\,\|x^{(1)}-x^{(0)}\| \]	Numerik fixed-point convergence
What is the Jacobi (total step) method?	Solve each equation for its own diagonal variable using only values from the previous iteration. All components are updated simultaneously from old values.<br><br>\[ x_i^{(k+1)}=\frac{1}{a_{ii}}\Big(b_i-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}x_j^{(k)}\Big) \]	Numerik linear-systems iterative jacobi
What is the Gauss-Seidel (single step) method?	Like Jacobi, but always use the most recently computed values within the same sweep. Updates are used immediately as they become available.<br><br>\[ x_i^{(k+1)}=\frac{1}{a_{ii}}\Big(b_i-\sum_{j\lt i}a_{ij}x_j^{(k+1)}-\sum_{j\gt i}a_{ij}x_j^{(k)}\Big) \]	Numerik linear-systems iterative gauss-seidel
What is the exact convergence criterion for a linear iterative method?	The spectral radius of the iteration matrix must be strictly less than 1. This is necessary and sufficient and holds for every starting vector.<br><br>\[ \rho(M)\lt 1\qquad\text{(necessary and sufficient)} \]	Numerik linear-systems iterative convergence
What is the sufficient norm criterion for convergence?	If any induced matrix norm of the iteration matrix is less than 1, the method converges. It is only sufficient, since the spectral radius is at most any induced norm.	Numerik linear-systems iterative convergence
What is strict diagonal dominance?	Each diagonal entry exceeds in absolute value the sum of the absolute values of the other entries in its row. The diagonal dominates the rest of the row.<br><br>\[ |a_{ii}|\ \gt\ \sum_{j\neq i}|a_{ij}|\qquad\forall i \]	Numerik linear-systems diagonal-dominance
What does strict diagonal dominance guarantee?	Both the Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel methods converge for any starting vector. It also implies all diagonal entries are non-zero.	Numerik linear-systems convergence
What determines the asymptotic speed of a convergent iterative method?	The spectral radius of the iteration matrix. The error decays roughly like that radius raised to the iteration count, so a smaller radius converges faster.	Numerik linear-systems convergence
What is the linear least squares problem?	Find the parameters minimizing the squared Euclidean norm of the residual vector between model and data. Squares are used because the absolute value is not differentiable at zero.<br><br>\[ \hat x=\arg\min_x\ \|Ax-b\|_2^2 \]	Numerik least-squares
What are the Gaussian normal equations?	The system A-transpose A x equal to A-transpose b. Solving it is equivalent to solving the least squares problem.<br><br>\[ A^{\top}A\,x=A^{\top}b \]	Numerik least-squares normal-equations
What is the geometric meaning of the normal equations?	The residual must be orthogonal to the column space of the design matrix. The best approximation is the orthogonal projection of the data onto that space.<br><br>\[ A^{\top}(b-Ax)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\perp \operatorname{im}(A) \]	Numerik least-squares geometry
When do the normal equations have a unique solution?	When A has full column rank, so A-transpose A is positive definite and hence invertible.	Numerik least-squares
How is a polynomial fit expressed as a least squares problem?	Build a Vandermonde design matrix whose columns are the powers of the data points, then solve the normal equations for the coefficients.	Numerik least-squares polynomial-fit
When is a polynomial least squares fit unique?	When the number of data points is at least the polynomial degree plus 1. Fewer points leave the coefficients underdetermined.	Numerik least-squares polynomial-fit
What is the singular value decomposition?	A factorization of any matrix into an orthogonal matrix, a diagonal matrix of non-negative singular values, and another orthogonal matrix transposed. It exists for every matrix, square or not.<br><br>\[ A=U\Sigma V^{\top},\qquad U^{\top}U=I,\ V^{\top}V=I,\ \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i) \]	Numerik svd
What is the outer product form of the SVD?	The matrix is the sum over the singular values of that value times the outer product of the corresponding left and right singular vectors. Truncating the sum gives the best low-rank approximation.<br><br>\[ A=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i\,u_iv_i^{\top} \]	Numerik svd
What is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse via the SVD?	Swap the two orthogonal factors and invert the non-zero singular values, transposing the diagonal shape. It generalizes matrix inversion to singular and rectangular matrices.<br><br>\[ A^{+}=V\Sigma^{+}U^{\top},\qquad \Sigma^{+}=\operatorname{diag}\big(\sigma_i^{-1}\big)\ \text{for }\sigma_i\neq 0 \]	Numerik svd pseudoinverse
What does the pseudoinverse deliver when the least squares solution is not unique?	The solution with the smallest Euclidean norm among all least squares solutions. It picks the minimum-norm representative.	Numerik svd pseudoinverse least-squares
Why is the minimum-norm solution a sensible choice?	Because the solution set is an affine subspace, and the minimum-norm element is the unique one orthogonal to the kernel, giving a canonical and stable answer.	Numerik svd pseudoinverse
What is the convergence order of an iterative method?	The exponent p such that the new error is bounded by a constant times the old error to the power p. Order 1 is linear, order 2 is quadratic.<br><br>\[ \|x^{(k+1)}-x^*\|\ \le\ C\,\|x^{(k)}-x^*\|^{\,p} \]	Numerik convergence
What is the difference between local and global convergence?	Local convergence requires the starting point to lie in some neighbourhood of the solution; global convergence holds for every admissible starting point.	Numerik convergence
When is a fixed point iteration locally linearly convergent?	When the derivative (or the compatible matrix norm of the Jacobian) at the fixed point has magnitude less than 1. The map is then a contraction near the fixed point.	Numerik fixed-point convergence
When is a fixed point iteration locally quadratically convergent?	When the derivative at the fixed point is exactly zero and the map is twice continuously differentiable. The linear term in the Taylor expansion vanishes and the error is squared each step.	Numerik fixed-point convergence
What is Newton's method for a scalar equation?	Iterate by subtracting the function value divided by its derivative. It replaces the function by its tangent line and takes the zero of that tangent.<br><br>\[ x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)}-\frac{f\big(x^{(k)}\big)}{f'\big(x^{(k)}\big)} \]	Numerik root-finding newton
Why is Newton's method called continued linearization?	Because each step replaces the function by its first-order Taylor expansion around the current iterate and solves that linear equation exactly.	Numerik root-finding newton
When does Newton's method converge quadratically?	When the function is three times continuously differentiable and the root is simple, that is, the derivative at the root is non-zero. Then the iteration derivative vanishes at the root.	Numerik root-finding newton
Why does Newton's method lose quadratic convergence at a multiple root?	Because the iteration derivative at the root becomes 1 minus the reciprocal of the multiplicity, which is non-zero. Convergence degrades to linear.<br><br>\[ \Phi'(x^*)=1-\frac{1}{m}\ \neq\ 0 \]	Numerik root-finding newton
What is the modified Newton method for multiple roots?	Multiply the correction step by the multiplicity m. This restores a zero iteration derivative and hence quadratic convergence.<br><br>\[ x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)}-m\,\frac{f(x^{(k)})}{f'(x^{(k)})} \]	Numerik root-finding newton
What is the secant method?	Newton's method with the derivative replaced by a difference quotient of the last two iterates. It avoids computing derivatives at the cost of needing two starting points.<br><br>\[ x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)}-f(x^{(k)})\,\frac{x^{(k)}-x^{(k-1)}}{f(x^{(k)})-f(x^{(k-1)})} \]	Numerik root-finding secant
What is the convergence order of the secant method?	The golden ratio, roughly 1.618. It is slower than Newton but each step is cheaper because no derivative is evaluated.<br><br>\[ p=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\approx 1.618 \]	Numerik root-finding secant
What is the bisection method?	Repeatedly halve an interval whose endpoints have opposite function signs, keeping the half that still brackets a sign change.	Numerik root-finding bisection
What are the trade-offs of bisection?	It is extremely robust and guaranteed to converge for a continuous function with a sign change, but it converges only linearly and is therefore slow.	Numerik root-finding bisection
What is Newton's method for systems of equations?	Solve the linear system with the Jacobian matrix against the negative function value to get the correction step, then add that step to the current iterate.<br><br>\[ J_f\big(x^{(k)}\big)\,\Delta x=-f\big(x^{(k)}\big),\qquad x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)}+\Delta x \]	Numerik root-finding newton systems
Why is the Jacobian never explicitly inverted in Newton's method for systems?	Because solving the linear system is much cheaper and numerically more stable than forming the inverse. Inversion costs more and amplifies rounding error.	Numerik root-finding newton systems
What is the cost per step of multidimensional Newton?	On the order of n cubed over 3, dominated by solving the linear system with the Jacobian at each iteration.	Numerik root-finding complexity
What is the Jacobian matrix?	The matrix of all first partial derivatives of a vector-valued function, with one row per component function and one column per variable.<br><br>\[ J_f(x)=\Big(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\Big)_{i,j} \]	Numerik calculus jacobian
What is damping in Newton's method and when is it used?	Scaling the correction step by a factor between 0 and 1. It is used when a full Newton step fails to reduce the residual, which can happen far from the solution.	Numerik root-finding newton damping
What is the steepest descent method?	Move iteratively in the direction of the negative gradient with a suitable step size, so that the objective decreases at each step.<br><br>\[ x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)}-\alpha_k\,\nabla f\big(x^{(k)}\big) \]	Numerik optimization gradient-descent
What is the Gauss-Newton method?	A method for non-linear least squares that linearizes the residual function at the current iterate and solves the resulting linear least squares problem via the normal equations.<br><br>\[ \big(J^{\top}J\big)\,\Delta x=-J^{\top}r\big(x^{(k)}\big) \]	Numerik least-squares gauss-newton
Why is Gauss-Newton preferred over plain Newton for least squares?	Because it exploits the least squares structure and only needs the Jacobian of the residuals, avoiding the expensive second derivatives of the full Hessian.	Numerik least-squares gauss-newton
What is the polynomial interpolation problem?	Find the polynomial of degree at most m passing exactly through m+1 given data points.	Numerik interpolation
Is the interpolating polynomial unique?	Yes. If two polynomials of degree at most m agreed at m+1 points, their difference would have more roots than its degree allows, so it must be identically zero.	Numerik interpolation
What are Lagrange basis polynomials?	Polynomials that equal 1 at their own node and 0 at every other node. They make the interpolation coefficients simply the data values themselves.<br><br>\[ L_i(x)=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j},\qquad L_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij} \]	Numerik interpolation lagrange
What is the danger of high-degree polynomial interpolation?	Strong oscillation between the nodes, especially near the interval edges. A high degree does not mean a better approximation.	Numerik interpolation runge
What is a quadrature formula?	An approximation of an integral by a weighted sum of function values at chosen nodes. The weights and nodes determine its accuracy.<br><br>\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx\ \approx\ \sum_{i=0}^{m}w_i\,f(x_i) \]	Numerik integration quadrature
What is the order of exactness of a quadrature rule?	The largest polynomial degree that the rule integrates exactly. A rule with order of exactness m is exact for every polynomial up to that degree.	Numerik integration quadrature
What defines a Newton-Cotes formula?	Quadrature on an equidistant grid where the weights are the integrals of the Lagrange basis polynomials. It integrates the interpolating polynomial exactly.	Numerik integration newton-cotes
What is the trapezoidal rule?	Approximate the integral on a subinterval by the average of the two endpoint values times the interval width, that is, by the area of a trapezoid.<br><br>\[ \int_{a}^{b}f\,dx\approx\frac{b-a}{2}\big(f(a)+f(b)\big) \]	Numerik integration trapezoid
What is the consistency order of the composite trapezoidal rule?	Two: the error is bounded by a constant times the second derivative bound times the step size squared. Halving the step size quarters the error.<br><br>\[ |E|\ \le\ C\,h^2\,\max|f''| \]	Numerik integration trapezoid
What is Simpson's rule?	Weight the two endpoints by 1 and the midpoint by 4, then scale by the interval width over 6. It integrates the quadratic interpolating polynomial exactly.<br><br>\[ \int_a^b f\,dx\approx\frac{b-a}{6}\Big(f(a)+4f\big(\tfrac{a+b}{2}\big)+f(b)\Big) \]	Numerik integration simpson
What is the consistency order of the composite Simpson rule?	Four: the error scales with the fourth power of the step size and involves the fourth derivative. Halving the step size reduces the error by a factor of 16.<br><br>\[ |E|\ \le\ C\,h^4\,\max|f^{(4)}| \]	Numerik integration simpson
What is the general definition of consistency order for a quadrature rule?	The exponent s such that the quadrature error is bounded by a constant times the step size to the power s. Higher order means faster error decay under refinement.	Numerik integration quadrature
How does the error of an unstable linear system solution behave?	The relative error in the solution is at most the condition number times the relative error in the data. The condition number is exactly the worst-case amplification factor.<br><br>\[ \frac{\|\Delta x\|}{\|x\|}\ \le\ \kappa(A)\,\frac{\|\Delta b\|}{\|b\|} \]	Numerik conditioning
What can be said about the spectrum of a nilpotent matrix?	It contains only zero, so the determinant and the spectral radius are both zero. A nilpotent matrix is either zero or not diagonalizable.	Numerik linear-algebra
When does a linear system have a unique solution?	Exactly when the kernel of the matrix is trivial, equivalently when the matrix is regular and has full rank.	Numerik linear-algebra
Can a linear iteration diverge even if most starting vectors work?	Yes. If the spectral radius is at least 1, starting exactly along the dominant eigenvector direction produces an error that never decays.	Numerik linear-systems convergence
What is the relation between the spectrum of A squared and that of A?	The eigenvalues of A squared are exactly the squares of the eigenvalues of A. Squaring the matrix squares its spectrum.<br><br>\[ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda^2\in\sigma(A^2) \]	Numerik linear-algebra eigenvalues
