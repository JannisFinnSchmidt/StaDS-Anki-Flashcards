What is the survival function?	The probability that the event time exceeds a given time t, that is one minus the CDF. It is the probability of surviving beyond t.<br><br>\[ S(t)=P(T\gt t)=1-F(t) \]	Survival basics
What is the hazard rate?	The instantaneous risk of experiencing the event right now, conditional on having survived until now. It is a rate, not a probability, and can exceed 1.<br><br>\[ h(t)=\lim_{\Delta\to 0}\frac{P\big(t\le T\lt t+\Delta\mid T\ge t\big)}{\Delta} \]	Survival basics hazard
What is the cumulative hazard?	The integral of the hazard rate from zero up to time t. It accumulates all the risk experienced so far.<br><br>\[ H(t)=\int_0^t h(u)\,du \]	Survival basics hazard
What is the relationship between hazard, density and survival function?	The hazard equals the density divided by the survival function. Risk right now is the chance of the event happening divided by the chance of still being at risk.<br><br>\[ h(t)=\frac{f(t)}{S(t)} \]	Survival basics relationships
How is the survival function recovered from the cumulative hazard?	As the exponential of the negative cumulative hazard. This is the central identity linking the two representations.<br><br>\[ S(t)=\exp\big(-H(t)\big) \]	Survival basics relationships
How is the density recovered from the survival function?	As the negative derivative of the survival function.<br><br>\[ f(t)=-\frac{d}{dt}S(t) \]	Survival basics relationships
What is the conditional survival probability beyond a later time given survival to t?	The ratio of the two survival function values, equivalently the exponential of the negative difference of the cumulative hazards.<br><br>\[ P(T\gt s\mid T\gt t)=\frac{S(s)}{S(t)}=\exp\big(-[H(s)-H(t)]\big) \]	Survival basics
How is the expected survival time expressed via the survival function?	As the integral of the survival function over all positive times. It follows from writing the event time as an integral of indicators and swapping expectation and integral.<br><br>\[ E(T)=\int_0^{\infty}S(t)\,dt \]	Survival basics
Why does the exponential distribution model non-aging objects?	Because its hazard is constant over time, so the risk never changes with age. It is the memoryless lifetime distribution.<br><br>\[ h(t)=\lambda,\qquad S(t)=e^{-\lambda t} \]	Survival distributions exponential
What is the relationship between exponential lifetimes and the Poisson process?	Exponential lifetimes are the waiting times between events of a Poisson process.	Survival distributions exponential
What is the hazard shape of a Weibull distribution?	Proportional to a power of time. A shape parameter above 1 gives a strictly increasing hazard, below 1 a strictly decreasing one, and exactly 1 recovers the exponential.<br><br>\[ h(t)=\lambda\,\alpha\,t^{\alpha-1},\qquad S(t)=\exp\big(-\lambda t^{\alpha}\big) \]	Survival distributions weibull
What is the hazard shape of a lognormal survival distribution?	Hump-shaped: risk first rises and then falls. It arises when the logarithm of the survival time is normally distributed.	Survival distributions lognormal
What is right censoring?	The event has not been observed by the end of the observation period, so only a lower bound on the survival time is known.	Survival censoring
What is left censoring?	The event happened before the first observation time, but by how much is unknown.	Survival censoring
What is interval censoring?	The event is known to have occurred within some interval, but the exact time within it is unknown.	Survival censoring
What is left truncation?	Individuals are only in the study if they survived up to a certain entry time. Those who experienced the event earlier never appear in the data at all.	Survival truncation
What is the crucial difference between censoring and truncation?	With censoring you observe the individual but not their complete event time; with truncation the individual is missing from the data entirely.	Survival censoring truncation
What are the three types of censoring mechanisms?	Type I with a fixed end time for everyone, type II where the study runs until a fixed number of events, and type III with a random censoring time per individual.	Survival censoring
What is the key assumption for valid likelihood inference under random censoring?	Independence of the event time and the censoring time. If they are dependent you have competing risks instead.	Survival censoring assumptions
What is the likelihood contribution of an observed event?	The density at the observed time. For a right-censored observation it is the survival function at the censoring time instead.<br><br>\[ L_i=f(t_i)^{d_i}\;S(t_i)^{1-d_i} \]	Survival likelihood
What is the likelihood contribution of a left-censored observation?	The CDF at the censoring time, since all that is known is that the event happened before it.	Survival likelihood
What is the likelihood contribution of an interval-censored observation?	The difference of the CDF at the two interval boundaries, that is, the probability of falling inside the interval.	Survival likelihood
How does left truncation change the likelihood contribution?	The contribution is divided by the survival function at the truncation time, conditioning on having survived long enough to enter the study.<br><br>\[ L_i=\frac{f(t_i)^{d_i}\,S(t_i)^{1-d_i}}{S(\tau_i)} \]	Survival likelihood truncation
Why can the censoring distribution be dropped from the likelihood?	Because it does not depend on the parameters of interest, so it enters only as a multiplicative constant.	Survival likelihood
What is the life-table estimator?	A discrete-time estimator that partitions the time axis into intervals and estimates the hazard in each as events divided by the number at risk, adjusted for censoring within the interval.	Survival non-parametric life-table
What is the risk set at a time point?	The set of individuals still under observation and event-free just before that time. Its size is the denominator of hazard estimates.	Survival non-parametric
What is the Kaplan-Meier estimator?	A non-parametric estimator of the survival function formed as a product over all observed event times of one minus the estimated hazard at each.<br><br>\[ \hat S(t)=\prod_{t_{(j)}\le t}\left(1-\frac{d_j}{n_j}\right) \]	Survival non-parametric kaplan-meier
Why is Kaplan-Meier a step function that only drops at event times?	Because the estimated hazard is zero at times with no events, so those factors equal 1. Censored observations only reduce the risk set.	Survival non-parametric kaplan-meier
What is Greenwood's formula used for?	Estimating the variance of the Kaplan-Meier estimator, which then gives pointwise confidence intervals for the survival curve.<br><br>\[ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}\big(\hat S(t)\big)=\hat S(t)^2\sum_{t_{(j)}\le t}\frac{d_j}{n_j\,(n_j-d_j)} \]	Survival non-parametric kaplan-meier
What is the Nelson-Aalen estimator?	A non-parametric estimator of the cumulative hazard, formed as a sum over event times of events divided by the number at risk.<br><br>\[ \hat H(t)=\sum_{t_{(j)}\le t}\frac{d_j}{n_j} \]	Survival non-parametric nelson-aalen
What is the Breslow estimator of the survival function?	The exponential of the negative Nelson-Aalen cumulative hazard. It is an alternative to Kaplan-Meier.<br><br>\[ \hat S(t)=\exp\big(-\hat H_{NA}(t)\big) \]	Survival non-parametric breslow
What is the log-rank test?	A non-parametric test comparing the survival distributions of two groups by aggregating, over all event times, the difference between observed and expected events in one group.<br><br>\[ U=\sum_{j}\big(d_{1j}-e_{1j}\big),\qquad \chi^2=\frac{U^2}{\operatorname{Var}(U)}\ \sim\ \chi^2_1 \]	Survival testing log-rank
What is the null hypothesis of the log-rank test?	That the two survival functions are identical at every time point.	Survival testing log-rank
What distribution underlies the expected counts in the log-rank test?	The hypergeometric distribution, since under the null the events at each time are like drawing from an urn without regard to group membership.	Survival testing log-rank
Why does the log-rank statistic sum signed rather than absolute differences?	Because the test targets a consistent difference in one direction; random fluctuations of opposite sign should cancel across time steps.	Survival testing log-rank
How does the Wilcoxon test differ from the log-rank test?	It weights each time point by the size of the risk set, which puts more weight on early differences when many subjects are still at risk.	Survival testing wilcoxon
What are the advantages of parametric over non-parametric estimation?	Non-parametric estimation needs a lot of data per group, requires arbitrary splitting for numeric covariates, and provides no significance testing between groups.	Survival parametric
What is an accelerated failure time model?	A model in which covariates stretch or compress the time scale, typically formulated as a linear model for the log survival time.<br><br>\[ \log T=x^{\top}\beta+\sigma\varepsilon \]	Survival aft
How is an AFT coefficient interpreted?	Through the acceleration factor, the exponential of the coefficient. It is the ratio of expected survival times between covariate levels.<br><br>\[ T=\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big)\cdot\exp(\sigma\varepsilon),\qquad x_1\to x_1+1:\ T\times e^{\beta_1} \]	Survival aft interpretation
What does a positive AFT coefficient mean?	Deceleration: the event is pushed further into the future, so survival time is longer. A negative coefficient accelerates the event.	Survival aft interpretation
What error distribution corresponds to a Weibull AFT model?	The Gumbel (extreme value) distribution for the error on the log time scale.	Survival aft weibull
What is the shape parameter interpretation in a Weibull model?	A shape of 1 means constant hazard (the exponential case), and a shape above 1 means the hazard grows over time.	Survival weibull
What is special about the Weibull among AFT models?	It is the only distribution that is both an accelerated failure time model and a proportional hazards model.	Survival weibull aft
What is the Cox proportional hazards model?	A model where the hazard is a common unspecified baseline hazard multiplied by the exponential of a linear predictor of the covariates.<br><br>\[ h(t\mid x)=h_0(t)\,\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big) \]	Survival cox
What is the baseline hazard in a Cox model?	The hazard for an individual with all covariates equal to zero. It is left completely unspecified, which makes the model semi-parametric.	Survival cox
What is the proportional hazards assumption?	The hazard ratio between any two individuals is constant over time. Covariate effects multiply the baseline without changing its shape.	Survival cox assumptions
How is a Cox coefficient interpreted?	Its exponential is the hazard ratio: the multiplicative change in hazard for a one-unit increase in the covariate, holding everything else fixed.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{HR}=\frac{h(t\mid x+1)}{h(t\mid x)}=e^{\beta} \]	Survival cox interpretation
What is the partial likelihood?	A likelihood built only from the conditional probabilities of who experiences each event given the risk set at that time. The baseline hazard cancels out entirely.<br><br>\[ PL(\beta)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\exp\big(x_{(i)}^{\top}\beta\big)}{\sum_{j\in R_i}\exp\big(x_j^{\top}\beta\big)} \]	Survival cox partial-likelihood
Why does the partial likelihood eliminate the baseline hazard?	Because the baseline appears identically in the numerator and denominator of each conditional probability and therefore cancels.	Survival cox partial-likelihood
What does the partial likelihood depend on?	Only the ordering of the event times and the covariates of those at risk, not the actual time values.	Survival cox partial-likelihood
How is the Cox partial likelihood derived from the profile likelihood?	By maximizing the full likelihood over the baseline hazard values for fixed coefficients and plugging the resulting maximizers back in.	Survival cox partial-likelihood
How are ties handled in the Cox partial likelihood?	Via approximations such as the Breslow method, which puts all subjects with an event at the same time into a single combined term.	Survival cox ties
How is the baseline hazard estimated after fitting a Cox model?	Via the Breslow estimator, which distributes the observed events over the exponentiated linear predictors of those at risk.<br><br>\[ \hat H_0(t)=\sum_{t_{(i)}\le t}\frac{1}{\sum_{j\in R_i}\exp\big(x_j^{\top}\hat\beta\big)} \]	Survival cox breslow
How is a linear hypothesis on Cox coefficients tested?	With a likelihood ratio statistic of twice the difference of the log partial likelihoods, which is asymptotically chi-squared with degrees of freedom equal to the number of restrictions.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{lq}=2\big(\log PL(\hat\beta)-\log PL(\tilde\beta)\big)\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ \chi^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)} \]	Survival cox testing
What is a stratified Cox model?	A Cox model that allows a separate baseline hazard for each level of a stratification variable while sharing the coefficients.	Survival cox stratification
When should you use a stratified Cox model?	When the proportional hazards assumption is violated for a categorical variable. Stratifying removes the need for proportionality in that variable.	Survival cox stratification
What is the drawback of stratifying on a variable?	No coefficient is estimated for it, so its effect can no longer be interpreted or tested.	Survival cox stratification
What are Cox-Snell residuals?	The estimated cumulative hazard evaluated at each observed time. Under a correct model they should behave like a standard exponential sample.<br><br>\[ r_i=\hat H\big(t_i\mid x_i\big)\ \sim\ \mathrm{Exp}(1)\ \text{ under a correct model} \]	Survival cox diagnostics residuals
Why should Cox-Snell residuals be exponentially distributed?	Because applying the true cumulative hazard transform to the event time produces a standard exponential variable, analogous to the probability integral transform.	Survival cox diagnostics
What are martingale residuals?	The event indicator minus the Cox-Snell residual. They have mean zero and are useful for assessing the functional form of covariates.<br><br>\[ m_i=d_i-r_i=d_i-\hat H(t_i\mid x_i) \]	Survival cox diagnostics residuals
What are deviance residuals used for?	Detecting outliers and checking whether a numeric variable should enter linearly. They are a symmetrized transformation of the martingale residuals.	Survival cox diagnostics residuals
What are Schoenfeld residuals and what are they for?	Per-covariate residuals comparing the covariate value of the individual who had the event against the risk-set weighted average. They test the proportional hazards assumption.	Survival cox diagnostics schoenfeld
How do you check the PH assumption with Schoenfeld residuals?	Plot them against time; under proportional hazards they should show no trend. A significant time trend indicates the effect changes over time.	Survival cox diagnostics schoenfeld
What are time-dependent covariates?	Covariates whose value changes during follow-up. They break proportionality since the hazard ratio then varies with time.	Survival cox time-dependent
What are time-varying effects?	Coefficients that themselves depend on time, typically expanded in basis functions. They relax the proportional hazards assumption directly.<br><br>\[ h(t\mid x)=h_0(t)\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta(t)\big) \]	Survival cox time-varying
What are the two options when the PH assumption fails?	Model the coefficient as a function of time, or stratify so that each group gets its own baseline hazard.	Survival cox assumptions
What is additive Cox regression?	A Cox model where covariate effects enter as smooth functions, typically splines, rather than linear terms.	Survival cox additive
What is a penalized partial likelihood?	The partial likelihood minus a roughness penalty on the spline coefficients, which controls the smoothness of the estimated effect functions.	Survival cox penalized
What is the piecewise exponential model?	A model assuming the baseline hazard is constant within each of a set of time intervals, which turns survival analysis into standard Poisson regression.<br><br>\[ h(t\mid x_i)=\exp\big(\beta_{0j}+x_i^{\top}\beta\big)=h_{ij},\qquad t\in I_j \]	Survival pem
Why does the piecewise exponential model reduce to Poisson regression?	Because with a piecewise constant hazard the likelihood has the same form as a Poisson likelihood, with the time spent in each interval acting as an offset.	Survival pem poisson
What is the role of the offset in a piecewise exponential model?	It is the log of the exposure time spent in each interval, so the model estimates a rate rather than a raw count.<br><br>\[ o_{ij}=\log t_{ij},\qquad d_{ij}\sim\mathrm{Poi}\big(h_{ij}\,t_{ij}\big) \]	Survival pem poisson
How is data restructured for a piecewise exponential model?	Into one row per subject per interval in which that subject is at risk, with an event indicator and the time spent in that interval.	Survival pem data
What is the trade-off in choosing the number of intervals for a PEM?	Few intervals give stable but crude estimates; many intervals give accurate but unstable ones. Use finer intervals where the hazard changes fastest.	Survival pem
When is the piecewise exponential model equivalent to the Cox model?	When there are no ties in the data. The PEM then estimates the same effects but with the baseline hazard estimated via the Poisson likelihood.	Survival pem cox
What is a piecewise additive mixed model (PAMM)?	A PEM where the baseline hazard is modelled as a smooth function of time via basis functions instead of a step function.	Survival pamm
How are time-varying effects expressed in a PAMM?	As interactions between time and the covariate. If the functional form is unknown it is estimated as a smooth two-dimensional term.	Survival pamm
What is a frailty model?	A survival model with a random effect capturing unobserved risk factors, so that individuals or groups can have systematically higher or lower hazards.	Survival frailty
How is a frailty term interpreted?	A positive random effect means a higher hazard for that individual or group, a negative one a lower hazard. A small variance means little unexplained heterogeneity.	Survival frailty
How does a frailty term change the interpretation of covariate effects?	Effects become conditional on the random effect, so they are interpreted per individual or group rather than at the population level.	Survival frailty interpretation
What is discrete-time survival analysis?	Modelling event times on a discretized time scale, where the discrete hazard is the conditional probability of the event in an interval given survival to it.<br><br>\[ \lambda_j=P\big(T=j\mid T\ge j\big) \]	Survival discrete-time
What is the discrete-time survival function?	The product over all preceding intervals of one minus the discrete hazard in that interval.<br><br>\[ S(j)=\prod_{k=1}^{j}\big(1-\lambda_k\big) \]	Survival discrete-time
Why can any binary model be used for discrete-time survival?	Because within each interval the outcome is simply whether the event occurs, so the data become a sequence of Bernoulli trials.	Survival discrete-time
What is the discrete-time logistic model?	A model applying the logit link to the discrete hazard, with an interval-specific intercept as the baseline hazard.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{logit}\big(\lambda_j(x)\big)=\beta_{0j}+x^{\top}\beta \]	Survival discrete-time logistic
How is a coefficient in the discrete logistic survival model interpreted?	As an odds ratio: it multiplies the odds of the event occurring at that time rather than later, for a one-unit covariate increase.	Survival discrete-time interpretation
What is the Gompertz discrete-time model?	A discrete-time model with the complementary log-log link. It is the discrete analogue of the Cox proportional hazards model, hence the grouped PH model.<br><br>\[ \log\!\big(-\log(1-\lambda_j)\big)=\beta_{0j}+x^{\top}\beta \]	Survival discrete-time gompertz
Why does the complementary log-log link give proportional hazards?	Because it arises directly from the relationship between the survival function and the integrated hazard, which preserves the multiplicative hazard structure.	Survival discrete-time gompertz
How are left truncation and right censoring handled in discrete-time models?	By adjusting the risk set: subjects only contribute rows for the intervals in which they are actually under observation.	Survival discrete-time
What are competing risks?	A setting with multiple mutually exclusive event types, where the occurrence of one prevents the others from being observed.	Survival competing-risks
What is a cause-specific hazard?	The instantaneous rate of experiencing one particular event type, given no event of any type has yet occurred.	Survival competing-risks
What is the all-cause hazard?	The sum of the cause-specific hazards over all event types. It gives the overall rate of leaving the event-free state.<br><br>\[ h(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}h_k(t) \]	Survival competing-risks
What is the cumulative incidence function?	The probability of having experienced a specific event type by time t. It accounts for the fact that other events can preempt it.<br><br>\[ F_k(t)=\int_0^{t}h_k(u)\,S(u)\,du \]	Survival competing-risks
How is competing risks data handled in a piecewise exponential framework?	Create one row per subject per interval per event type, with the event indicator set to 1 only for the event type that actually occurred.	Survival competing-risks pem
What is the multinomial logit model for competing risks in discrete time?	A model with one cause- and time-specific hazard per event type, normalized so the probabilities of all events plus survival sum to 1.	Survival competing-risks multinomial
How is a coefficient in the competing risks multinomial model interpreted?	As the multiplicative change in the odds of that specific event type relative to remaining event-free.	Survival competing-risks interpretation
What is a multi-state model?	A model of transitions between several states over time, characterized by transition hazards from each state to each other state.	Survival multi-state
What is the Markov assumption in a multi-state model?	The transition hazard depends only on the current state and not on the history of how it was reached.	Survival multi-state markov
What is an absorbing state? (Survival Analysis)	A state that can never be left once entered, such as death. Recurrent event and competing risks models are special cases of the multi-state framework.	Survival multi-state
What is the transition probability matrix in a multi-state model?	The matrix of probabilities of being in each state at a later time given the state at an earlier time. Its rows sum to 1.	Survival multi-state
What is the Aalen-Johansen estimator?	A non-parametric estimator of the transition probability matrix in a multi-state model, built as a product integral of the estimated transition hazards.	Survival multi-state aalen-johansen
What are state occupation probabilities?	The probabilities of being in each state at a given time, obtained by multiplying the initial state vector by the transition probability matrix.	Survival multi-state
What is event history analysis?	The general framework covering single events, recurrent events, competing risks and multi-state processes as increasingly general transition structures.	Survival event-history
What is a recurrent event?	An event that can happen repeatedly to the same subject, such as repeated hospitalizations.	Survival recurrent-events
What is the difference between gap and calendar timescale for recurrent events?	The gap timescale resets to zero after each event, while the calendar timescale runs continuously from the start of the study.	Survival recurrent-events timescale
Why do subjects become left truncated on the gap timescale?	Because to be at risk for the k-th event they must already have experienced the previous one, so they enter the risk set for that event later.	Survival recurrent-events truncation
What are the three modelling choices for recurrent events?	Assuming independence of events within a subject gives a standard model; different baselines per event number requires stratification; intra-subject correlation calls for a frailty model.	Survival recurrent-events
What is a survival tree?	A decision tree whose splits are chosen to maximize the separation of survival curves, typically using the log-rank statistic as the splitting criterion.	Survival machine-learning trees
How are predictions made from a survival tree?	By computing a Kaplan-Meier or Nelson-Aalen estimate within each terminal node.	Survival machine-learning trees
What is a random survival forest?	An ensemble of survival trees grown on bootstrap samples with random feature subsets, whose predictions are averaged.	Survival machine-learning random-forest
What are the pros and cons of random survival forests?	They perform well, handle high dimensionality and do variable selection, and need no PH assumption; but they cannot handle multi-state settings.	Survival machine-learning random-forest
What is the concordance index?	The proportion of comparable observation pairs whose predicted ordering matches the observed ordering of survival times. A value of 0.5 means random prediction.<br><br>\[ C=\frac{\#\{\text{concordant pairs}\}}{\#\{\text{comparable pairs}\}} \]	Survival evaluation c-index
Which pairs are not comparable when computing the C-index?	Pairs where both observations are censored, and generally any pair whose true ordering cannot be determined from the data.	Survival evaluation c-index
What is the Brier score in survival analysis?	The mean squared error of the predicted survival probability against the observed status, with right-censored observations reweighted by inverse probability of censoring.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{BS}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat w_i(t)\,\Big(\mathbf{1}\{t_i\gt t\}-\hat S(t\mid x_i)\Big)^2 \]	Survival evaluation brier-score
What is a prediction error curve?	A plot of the Brier score at different time points. Integrating it over time gives the integrated Brier score as a single summary.	Survival evaluation brier-score
What does the AIC look like for a survival model?	Minus twice the maximized log-likelihood plus twice the model degrees of freedom, exactly as in other likelihood models.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{AIC}=-2\,\ell(\hat\theta)+2\,\mathrm{df} \]	Survival model-selection aic
What is the observed Fisher information in survival estimation?	The second derivative of the log-likelihood, used both in the Newton-Raphson iteration and for standard errors of the estimates.	Survival estimation
How do you check graphically whether an exponential model fits?	Plot the negative log of the survival function against time; for an exponential distribution this should be a straight line through the origin.<br><br>\[ -\log \hat S(t)\ \text{vs.}\ t\ \Rightarrow\ \text{straight line through origin} \]	Survival diagnostics
How do you check graphically whether a Weibull model fits?	Plot the log of the negative log of the survival function against log time; a Weibull distribution gives a straight line.<br><br>\[ \log\!\big(-\log\hat S(t)\big)\ \text{vs.}\ \log t\ \Rightarrow\ \text{straight line} \]	Survival diagnostics
What does it suggest if stratified Kaplan-Meier curves cross or diverge strongly?	Non-proportional hazards. The proportional hazards assumption is then not appropriate for that stratifying variable.	Survival cox assumptions
What are the assumptions of the Cox model?	Proportional hazards over time, multiplicative covariate effects on the baseline, and no distributional assumption on the baseline hazard itself.	Survival cox assumptions
What are the assumptions of a Weibull survival model?	A specified functional form for the hazard, proportional multiplicative covariate effects, and in the AFT reading, covariates that accelerate or decelerate time to the event.	Survival weibull assumptions
How do you simulate a survival time from a given survival function?	Draw a uniform variable, set it equal to the survival function and solve for t. This is inversion using the fact that one minus a uniform is again uniform.<br><br>\[ U\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1),\qquad T=S^{-1}(U) \]	Survival simulation
What is the mean residual lifetime?	The expected remaining lifetime given survival to time x: the integral of the survival function beyond x divided by the survival function at x.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{mrl}(x)=\frac{\int_x^{\infty}S(t)\,dt}{S(x)} \]	Survival basics
