What is the conditional probability measure given an event B?	The probability of the intersection with B divided by P(B). It renormalizes the whole probability space so that B becomes the new certain event.<br><br>\[ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)} \]	Stat1 probability conditioning
What is conditional expectation given an event B?	The expectation computed under the conditional measure: the integral of X over B divided by P(B). It is the average of X restricted to the world where B happened.<br><br>\[ E(X\mid B)=\frac{1}{P(B)}\int_B X\,dP \]	Stat1 probability conditional-expectation
How is E(X | Y) defined when Y is discrete?	It is the random variable that takes the value E(X | Y = y_i) on the event {Y = y_i}. It is a function of Y, hence a random variable, not a number.	Stat1 conditional-expectation
Why is E(X | Y) a random variable while E(X | Y = y) is a number?	Because E(X | Y) is a function of the random Y: it takes different values depending on which value Y happens to realize. Once you fix Y = y, you get an ordinary number.	Stat1 conditional-expectation
How is conditional expectation computed in the continuous case?	As the integral of x against the conditional density f(x | y), where the conditional density is the joint density divided by the marginal density of Y.<br><br>\[ E(X\mid Y=y)=\int x\,f(x\mid y)\,dx \]	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is a conditional density?	The joint density divided by the marginal density of the conditioning variable, defined wherever that marginal density is positive. It is the density of X in the slice where Y = y.<br><br>\[ f(x\mid y)=\frac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)} \]	Stat1 densities conditioning
What is the factorization lemma?	A random variable X is measurable with respect to the sigma-algebra generated by Y exactly when X can be written as a measurable function of Y. Being determined by Y and being a function of Y are the same thing.	Stat1 measure-theory
What is the defining property of conditional expectation in measure-theoretic terms?	E(X | Y) is the (almost surely unique) Y-measurable random variable whose integral agrees with that of X over every event in the sigma-algebra generated by Y.	Stat1 conditional-expectation measure-theory
What is the linearity property of conditional expectation?	E(aX + bZ | Y) = a E(X | Y) + b E(Z | Y). Conditioning preserves linear combinations exactly as ordinary expectation does.<br><br>\[ E(aX+bZ\mid Y)=aE(X\mid Y)+bE(Z\mid Y) \]	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is the monotonicity property of conditional expectation?	If X is at most Z pointwise, then E(X | Y) is at most E(Z | Y). Order is preserved under conditioning.	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is the pull-out property (taking out what is known)?	If X is measurable with respect to Y, then E(X Z | Y) = X E(Z | Y). Anything already determined by the conditioning information behaves like a constant.<br><br>\[ E(XZ\mid Y)=X\,E(Z\mid Y)\quad\text{if }X\text{ is }\sigma(Y)\text{-measurable} \]	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is E(X | Y) when X and Y are independent?	Just E(X). Independence means Y carries no information about X, so conditioning changes nothing.	Stat1 conditional-expectation independence
What is E(X | Y) when Y is constant?	E(X). A constant carries no information, so the conditional expectation reduces to the unconditional one.	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is the law of iterated expectations (tower property)?	E(E(X | Y)) = E(X). Averaging the conditional averages recovers the overall average.<br><br>\[ E\big(E(X\mid Y)\big)=E(X) \]	Stat1 conditional-expectation tower-property
What is E(X | X)?	X itself. Conditioning on a variable given full knowledge of it returns it unchanged.	Stat1 conditional-expectation
What is the law of total expectation?	E(X) is the integral of E(X | Y = y) against the distribution of Y. In the discrete case it is a weighted sum of conditional means weighted by the probabilities of the conditioning events.<br><br>\[ E(X)=\int E(X\mid Y=y)\,dF_Y(y) \]	Stat1 conditional-expectation
How can a probability be computed via conditioning?	Write the probability as the expectation of an indicator, then apply the tower property: P(A) equals the average of P(A | Y = y) over the distribution of Y.	Stat1 probability conditioning
What is conditional variance?	The expected squared deviation from the conditional mean, computed under the conditional distribution. It measures the remaining spread once the conditioning information is known.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y)=E\big(X^2\mid Y\big)-E(X\mid Y)^2 \]	Stat1 conditional-variance
State the variance decomposition (law of total variance).	Var(X) equals the expected conditional variance plus the variance of the conditional expectation. Total uncertainty splits into within-group and between-group variability.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(X)=E\big(\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y)\big)+\operatorname{Var}\big(E(X\mid Y)\big) \]	Stat1 conditional-variance decomposition
State the covariance decomposition.	Cov(X,Y) equals the expected conditional covariance plus the covariance of the two conditional expectations. It is the covariance analogue of the variance decomposition.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Z)=E\big(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Z\mid Y)\big)+\operatorname{Cov}\big(E(X\mid Y),E(Z\mid Y)\big) \]	Stat1 conditional-variance decomposition
If X and Z are independent, what is Var(E(X | Z))?	Zero, because E(X | Z) is then the constant E(X), and a constant has no variance.	Stat1 conditional-variance independence
What is conditional independence?	Two variables are conditionally independent given Y if, once Y is known, their joint conditional behaviour factorizes. Intuitively: risks are independent apart from one common external influence.	Stat1 independence conditioning
Why is conditional independence weaker than independence?	Because two variables can be strongly dependent unconditionally purely through a shared driver Y, and become independent once that driver is held fixed.	Stat1 independence conditioning
What is a convolution and what does it compute?	The integral of the product of one density evaluated at s and the other at t minus s. It gives the density of the sum of two independent random variables.<br><br>\[ f_{X+Y}(s)=\int f_X(t)\,f_Y(s-t)\,dt \]	Stat1 probability convolution
What is the sum of two independent Poisson variables?	Poisson with the sum of the intensities. The Poisson family is closed under independent addition.<br><br>\[ X+Y\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2) \]	Stat1 distributions poisson
Given X + Y = j for independent Poissons, what is the conditional distribution of X?	Binomial with j trials and success probability equal to the first intensity divided by the sum of both intensities. The total is split binomially.<br><br>\[ X\mid X+Y=j\ \sim\ \mathrm{Bin}\!\left(j,\ \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}\right) \]	Stat1 distributions poisson conditioning
What is a statistical model?	A triple consisting of a sample space, a sigma-algebra, and a family of candidate probability measures indexed by an unknown parameter. Inference means choosing among that family.	Stat1 inference model
What is a parametric statistical model?	One where the family of distributions is indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter, for example the normal family indexed by mean and variance.	Stat1 inference model
What is a non-parametric model?	A model whose family of distributions is not indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter, for example all distributions with a finite expectation.	Stat1 inference model
What is a point estimator?	A measurable map from the sample space to the parameter space that does not depend on the unknown parameter. It must be computable purely from the data.	Stat1 estimation
Why must an estimator not depend on the unknown parameter?	Because it has to be evaluable from observed data alone. A rule involving the unknown truth would be unusable in practice.	Stat1 estimation
What does it mean for an estimator to be unbiased?	Its expected value equals the true parameter for every possible parameter value. On average it hits the target with no systematic error.<br><br>\[ E_\theta(\hat\theta)=\theta\quad\forall\,\theta\in\Theta \]	Stat1 estimation bias
What is the bias of an estimator?	The difference between its expected value and the true parameter. It is the systematic part of the estimation error.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{Bias}(\hat\theta)=E_\theta(\hat\theta)-\theta \]	Stat1 estimation bias
What is asymptotic unbiasedness?	The bias vanishes as the sample size grows to infinity, even if the estimator is biased for any finite sample.	Stat1 estimation bias asymptotics
What does consistency of an estimator mean?	The estimator converges in probability to the true parameter: for any tolerance, the probability of missing by more than that tolerance goes to zero as n grows.<br><br>\[ \forall\varepsilon\gt 0:\quad P\big(|\hat\theta_n-\theta|\gt \varepsilon\big)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0 \]	Stat1 estimation consistency
What is a sufficient condition for consistency?	Asymptotic unbiasedness together with variance tending to zero. If the estimator centers correctly and its spread collapses, it must converge to the truth.	Stat1 estimation consistency
What is the mean squared error of an estimator?	The expected squared deviation from the true parameter. It measures total accuracy, combining both systematic and random error.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta)=E_\theta\big[(\hat\theta-\theta)^2\big] \]	Stat1 estimation mse
State the bias-variance decomposition of the MSE.	MSE equals the variance plus the squared bias. Total error splits into a random scatter component and a systematic offset component.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta)=\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)+\mathrm{Bias}(\hat\theta)^2 \]	Stat1 estimation bias-variance
Why does the cross term vanish in the bias-variance decomposition?	Because it contains the expectation of the deviation of the estimator from its own mean, which is zero by construction.	Stat1 estimation bias-variance
What is the practical lesson of the bias-variance decomposition?	A slightly biased estimator with much smaller variance can beat an unbiased one in total error. Unbiasedness is not automatically the best criterion.	Stat1 estimation bias-variance
Why is the sample variance divided by n-1 rather than n?	Because using the estimated mean instead of the true mean removes one degree of freedom. Dividing by n-1 corrects the resulting downward bias.<br><br>\[ S^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i-\bar X)^2,\qquad E(S^2)=\sigma^2 \]	Stat1 estimation variance
If the true mean mu is known, what is the unbiased estimator of the variance?	The average squared deviation from mu, divided by n rather than n-1. No degree of freedom is lost when the mean is not estimated.	Stat1 estimation variance
What is a uniformly best estimator?	An unbiased estimator whose variance is at most that of every other unbiased estimator, for every parameter value. It is optimal across the whole parameter space, not just at one point.	Stat1 estimation optimality
Are unbiasedness, the ML principle and minimal MSE always compatible?	No. They are all reasonable criteria but they can point to different estimators, for example different scaling constants in a variance estimator.	Stat1 estimation optimality
What is the likelihood function?	The density or probability mass of the observed data viewed as a function of the parameter with the data held fixed. It measures how well each parameter explains what was seen.<br><br>\[ L(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(x_i\mid\theta),\qquad \ell(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f(x_i\mid\theta) \]	Stat1 likelihood
Why do we work with the log-likelihood instead of the likelihood?	Because the log turns products over independent observations into sums, which are far easier to differentiate, and it is monotone so it has the same maximizer.	Stat1 likelihood
What is the maximum likelihood estimator?	The parameter value that maximizes the likelihood, that is, the parameter under which the observed data are most probable.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta_{ML}=\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}L(\theta) \]	Stat1 likelihood mle
What are the steps for computing an ML estimator analytically?	Write the log-likelihood, set its gradient to zero, verify the second derivative is negative or the Hessian negative definite, and check the boundary behaviour of the parameter space.	Stat1 likelihood mle
Why must the boundary of the parameter space be checked for ML estimation?	Because the maximum may lie at the edge rather than at a stationary point, in which case the derivative condition never detects it.	Stat1 likelihood mle
What is the ML estimator for the success probability of a Bernoulli sample?	The sample proportion of successes. The most likely p is simply the observed relative frequency.<br><br>\[ \hat p=\bar X=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i \]	Stat1 likelihood mle
What are the ML estimators for a normal sample?	The sample mean for mu, and the average squared deviation from the sample mean (divided by n) for the variance. The ML variance estimator is biased downward.<br><br>\[ \hat\mu=\bar X,\qquad \hat\sigma^2=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i-\bar X)^2 \]	Stat1 likelihood mle normal
What is the ML estimator for the upper limit of a Uniform(0, theta) sample?	The sample maximum. It is biased downward since it can never exceed the true limit, but it is consistent and asymptotically unbiased.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta=\max_i X_i=X_{(n)} \]	Stat1 likelihood mle uniform
How is the biased maximum estimator for a uniform limit corrected?	Multiply the sample maximum by (n+1)/n. This scales it up just enough to remove the bias.<br><br>\[ \tilde\theta=\frac{n+1}{n}X_{(n)} \]	Stat1 estimation bias uniform
What is the invariance principle of ML estimation?	If theta-hat is the ML estimator of theta, then g(theta-hat) is the ML estimator of g(theta) for any injective measurable g. ML estimation commutes with reparameterization.	Stat1 likelihood mle invariance
What is the plug-in method?	Estimating a transformed parameter by inserting the estimator into the transformation. It is the practical form of the ML invariance principle.	Stat1 estimation plug-in
What is the score function?	The derivative of the log-likelihood with respect to the parameter, equivalently the relative rate of change of the density in the parameter. Setting it to zero gives the ML equation.<br><br>\[ S(\theta)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log L(\theta) \]	Stat1 likelihood score
What is the expected value of the score function?	Zero, at the true parameter. This follows from differentiating the constant total probability mass of 1 under the integral sign.<br><br>\[ E_\theta\big[S(\theta)\big]=0 \]	Stat1 likelihood score
What is the Fisher information?	The variance of the score, equivalently the negative expected second derivative of the log-likelihood. It quantifies how much information the data carry about the parameter.<br><br>\[ I(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}\big(S(\theta)\big)=-E\!\left[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta^2}\log L(\theta)\right] \]	Stat1 likelihood fisher-information
What does large Fisher information mean?	The log-likelihood is sharply peaked, so the parameter can be pinned down precisely. Large information corresponds to small estimator variance.	Stat1 likelihood fisher-information
How does Fisher information behave for an i.i.d. sample of size n?	It is n times the information from a single observation. Information adds up across independent observations.<br><br>\[ I_n(\theta)=n\,I_1(\theta) \]	Stat1 likelihood fisher-information
What is the asymptotic distribution of the ML estimator?	Approximately normal, centered at the true parameter, with variance the inverse of the total Fisher information. ML estimators are asymptotically normal and efficient.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta_{ML}\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ N\!\left(\theta,\ \frac{1}{n I_1(\theta)}\right) \]	Stat1 likelihood asymptotics
Why is the ML estimator called asymptotically efficient?	Because its asymptotic variance attains the inverse Fisher information, which is the lower bound on the variance of an unbiased estimator.	Stat1 likelihood efficiency
What is the delta method?	A rule for the asymptotic distribution of a smooth transformation g of an asymptotically normal estimator: the variance gets multiplied by the squared derivative of g at the true parameter.<br><br>\[ \sqrt{n}\big(g(\hat\theta)-g(\theta)\big)\ \stackrel{d}{\to}\ N\!\big(0,\ g'(\theta)^2\sigma^2\big) \]	Stat1 asymptotics delta-method
Why does the derivative appear squared in the delta method?	Because the transformation is locally linearized, and a linear rescaling multiplies the variance by the square of the scaling factor.	Stat1 asymptotics delta-method
What is a confidence region?	A data-dependent set that does not involve the unknown parameter, is measurable, and covers the true parameter with probability at least 1 minus alpha.	Stat1 confidence-intervals
Why is a confidence interval random while the parameter is fixed?	Because the endpoints are computed from the random sample. The coverage statement is about the procedure, not about the parameter moving.	Stat1 confidence-intervals interpretation
What trade-off governs the choice of a confidence level?	Higher confidence requires a wider interval. You want the interval as short as possible while still guaranteeing the required coverage.	Stat1 confidence-intervals
How is Chebyshev's inequality used to build a confidence interval?	It bounds the probability of a deviation using only the variance, so if an upper bound on the variance is known, a valid interval follows without any distributional assumption.<br><br>\[ P\big(|X-\mu|\ge k\big)\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{k^2} \]	Stat1 confidence-intervals chebyshev
What is the drawback of a Chebyshev-based confidence interval?	It is very conservative and therefore unnecessarily wide, because Chebyshev's bound holds for all distributions and is far from tight for any specific one.	Stat1 confidence-intervals chebyshev
How does the central limit theorem improve on Chebyshev intervals?	It gives the approximate normal distribution of the standardized sample mean, so normal quantiles can be used instead of the crude Chebyshev bound, producing much shorter intervals.<br><br>\[ \frac{\sqrt{n}\,(\bar X-\mu)}{\sigma}\ \stackrel{d}{\to}\ N(0,1) \]	Stat1 confidence-intervals clt
How is a confidence interval built from ML asymptotics?	Use the asymptotic normality of the ML estimator with the estimated Fisher information, giving an interval of the estimator plus and minus a normal quantile times the estimated standard error.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta\ \pm\ z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\,\hat I_1(\hat\theta)}} \]	Stat1 confidence-intervals likelihood
What is Student's theorem (the four classical facts)?	For an i.i.d. normal sample: the sample mean is normal; the scaled sample variance is chi-squared with n-1 degrees of freedom; mean and variance are independent; and their standardized ratio is t-distributed with n-1 degrees of freedom.<br><br>\[ \bar X\sim N\!\Big(\mu,\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\Big),\quad \frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi^2_{n-1},\quad \frac{\sqrt{n}(\bar X-\mu)}{S}\sim t_{n-1} \]	Stat1 normal-theory student
Why is the independence of sample mean and sample variance so important?	Because it is exactly what allows their ratio to have a t-distribution, which is the basis of the t-test and t-interval.	Stat1 normal-theory student
When do you use a normal quantile versus a t quantile for a mean interval?	Use the normal quantile when the variance is known, and the t quantile when it is estimated from the data. The t distribution accounts for the extra uncertainty in the variance.<br><br>\[ \bar X\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad \bar X\pm t_{n-1;1-\alpha/2}\tfrac{S}{\sqrt n} \]	Stat1 confidence-intervals t-distribution
How is an exact confidence interval for a normal variance built?	From the chi-squared distribution of the scaled sample variance, using its lower and upper quantiles as the two endpoints of the interval.<br><br>\[ \left[\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\chi^2_{n-1;1-\alpha/2}},\ \frac{(n-1)S^2}{\chi^2_{n-1;\alpha/2}}\right] \]	Stat1 confidence-intervals chi-squared
Why is the confidence interval for a variance asymmetric?	Because the chi-squared distribution is skewed, so its lower and upper quantiles are not symmetric around the estimate.	Stat1 confidence-intervals chi-squared
What is the covariance matrix of a random vector?	The matrix of all pairwise covariances between components, with the individual variances on the diagonal.	Stat1 multivariate
What are the two structural properties of every covariance matrix?	It is symmetric, and it is positive semi-definite. Positive semi-definiteness holds because any linear combination has non-negative variance.	Stat1 multivariate
How does a covariance matrix transform under an affine map?	Cov(AX + b) = A Cov(X) A-transpose. The shift b drops out and the linear map sandwiches the matrix.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(AX+b)=A\operatorname{Cov}(X)A^{\top} \]	Stat1 multivariate
Why is an off-diagonal covariance entry bounded by the diagonal entries?	By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, each covariance is at most the geometric mean of the two corresponding variances.	Stat1 multivariate cauchy-schwarz
What characterizes the multivariate normal distribution?	Its density involves the inverse covariance matrix in a quadratic form, and it requires the covariance matrix to be positive definite so that the determinant is non-zero.	Stat1 multivariate normal
What is the standard multivariate normal?	The vector with zero mean and identity covariance, equivalently a vector of independent standard normal components.	Stat1 multivariate normal
How is a general multivariate normal built from a standard one?	Take X = A Z + mu where A A-transpose equals the target covariance. Affine maps generate the whole family from the standard case.<br><br>\[ X=AZ+\mu\ \sim\ N_d\big(\mu,\ AA^{\top}\big) \]	Stat1 multivariate normal
What is the equivalent characterization of multivariate normality via linear combinations?	A vector is multivariate normal exactly when every linear combination of its components is univariate normal. This is often the easiest way to verify it.	Stat1 multivariate normal
Are the marginals of a multivariate normal always normal?	Yes, each component is univariate normal. The converse fails: normal marginals do not imply joint normality.	Stat1 multivariate normal
For jointly normal variables, what is the relation between uncorrelatedness and independence?	They are equivalent. This equivalence is special to the joint normal case and does not hold in general.	Stat1 multivariate normal independence
Why is the standard multivariate normal invariant under rotations?	Because an orthogonal matrix O satisfies O O-transpose equal to the identity, so the covariance is unchanged. The distribution is spherically symmetric.	Stat1 multivariate normal
What is the chi-squared distribution in terms of normals?	The sum of squares of independent standard normals. The number of terms is the degrees of freedom.<br><br>\[ \chi^2_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_i^2,\qquad Z_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}N(0,1) \]	Stat1 distributions chi-squared
What are the mean and variance of a chi-squared distribution with n degrees of freedom?	Mean n and variance 2n. Both grow linearly in the degrees of freedom.<br><br>\[ E(\chi^2_n)=n,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\chi^2_n)=2n \]	Stat1 distributions chi-squared
What is a statistical decision problem in test theory?	A split of the parameter space into a null region and an alternative region, with the task of deciding from data which one contains the truth.	Stat1 testing
What is a simple hypothesis?	One that specifies exactly one distribution, so the corresponding parameter set is a single point. A composite hypothesis contains more than one.	Stat1 testing
What is a non-randomized test?	A measurable map from the sample space to {0,1}: 1 means reject the null in favour of the alternative, 0 means do not reject.	Stat1 testing
What is a type 1 error and why is it treated as the more serious one?	Rejecting a true null hypothesis. It is controlled at a fixed small level because the null typically represents the status quo, and falsely overturning it is the costlier mistake.	Stat1 testing errors
What is a type 2 error?	Failing to reject the null when the alternative is actually true. It is not directly controlled; it is only minimized subject to the level constraint.	Stat1 testing errors
What is the power function of a test?	The probability of rejecting the null as a function of the true parameter. It gives, for every parameter value, the chance of deciding for the alternative.<br><br>\[ \beta(\theta)=P_\theta(\text{reject }H_0) \]	Stat1 testing power
What is the power (Scharfe) of a test at an alternative?	One minus the type 2 error probability at that parameter, that is, the value of the power function inside the alternative region.	Stat1 testing power
What is the level condition (Niveaugleichung) of a test?	The supremum of the power function over the null region must be at most alpha. The worst-case rejection probability under the null is capped.<br><br>\[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_0}\ \beta(\theta)\ \le\ \alpha \]	Stat1 testing level
Why is there a fundamental conflict between the two error types?	Enlarging the acceptance region reduces type 1 error but increases type 2 error, and vice versa. Both cannot be reduced at once for a fixed sample size.	Stat1 testing errors
What is a uniformly most powerful test?	A level-alpha test whose power function dominates that of every other level-alpha test at every parameter value in the alternative.	Stat1 testing optimality
What is an unbiased test?	A test whose rejection probability is at most alpha under the null and at least alpha under every alternative. It is never less likely to reject when the alternative is true.<br><br>\[ \beta(\theta)\le\alpha\ \ \forall\theta\in\Theta_0,\qquad \beta(\theta)\ge\alpha\ \ \forall\theta\in\Theta_1 \]	Stat1 testing unbiasedness
Why is unbiasedness a natural requirement for a test?	Because a test that rejects less often when the alternative holds than when the null holds is worse than useless: it points in the wrong direction.	Stat1 testing unbiasedness
What is a randomized test?	A map from the sample space into the interval from 0 to 1, giving the probability of rejecting for each observation. It allows exact level attainment with discrete data.	Stat1 testing randomization
Why are randomized tests needed for discrete distributions?	Because the achievable rejection probabilities jump in discrete steps, so a non-randomized test usually cannot hit the level exactly. Randomization fills the gaps.	Stat1 testing randomization
How is the randomization probability gamma constructed?	As the leftover level not used by the strict rejection region, divided by the probability mass sitting exactly at the critical value.<br><br>\[ \gamma=\frac{\alpha-P_{\theta_0}(T\gt c)}{P_{\theta_0}(T=c)} \]	Stat1 testing randomization
What does the Neyman-Pearson lemma give?	The uniformly most powerful test for a simple null against a simple alternative: reject when the likelihood ratio exceeds a critical value, randomize when it equals it.<br><br>\[ \varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&amp; \frac{f_1(x)}{f_0(x)}\gt k\\ \gamma,&amp; =k\\ 0,&amp; \lt k\end{cases} \]	Stat1 testing neyman-pearson
What is the intuition behind the likelihood ratio test statistic?	Include in the rejection region exactly those observations that are relatively most probable under the alternative compared with the null, since these buy the most power per unit of level.	Stat1 testing neyman-pearson
Is the Neyman-Pearson test unique?	It is essentially unique: any uniformly most powerful test must agree with it apart from sets of probability zero.	Stat1 testing neyman-pearson
What does the exhaustion property of an optimal test state?	A uniformly most powerful test either uses the level fully (its null rejection probability equals alpha) or has power exactly 1. Otherwise it could be improved.	Stat1 testing optimality
Why is every uniformly most powerful test automatically unbiased?	Because the constant test that always rejects with probability alpha is a valid level-alpha test, and the optimal test must have power at least as large as that constant.	Stat1 testing unbiasedness
What is the one-sided Gauss test?	A test for the mean of a normal sample with known variance, using the standardized sample mean compared to a standard normal quantile.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{\sqrt{n}\,(\bar X-\mu_0)}{\sigma}\ \gt\ z_{1-\alpha} \]	Stat1 testing gauss-test
Why is the one-sided Gauss test uniformly most powerful?	Because the density ratio is monotone in the test statistic, so the Neyman-Pearson rejection region is the same for every alternative in the one-sided region.	Stat1 testing gauss-test
Why is there no uniformly most powerful two-sided Gauss test?	Because it would have to be simultaneously optimal against both directions, and the optimal one-sided tests contradict each other. Optimality is only recovered within the class of unbiased tests.	Stat1 testing gauss-test
What is the two-sided Gauss test optimal for?	It is the uniformly best unbiased level-alpha test. Restricting to unbiased tests resolves the contradiction between the two directions.	Stat1 testing gauss-test
What is the t-test and when is it used?	The analogue of the Gauss test for a normal mean when the variance is unknown: the sample standard deviation replaces sigma, and t quantiles replace normal quantiles.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{\sqrt{n}\,(\bar X-\mu_0)}{S}\ \sim\ t_{n-1}\ \text{ under } H_0 \]	Stat1 testing t-test
What is the chi-squared variance test?	A test for the variance of a normal sample based on the scaled sample variance, compared to chi-squared quantiles. The two-sided version uses both tails.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma_0^2}\ \sim\ \chi^2_{n-1}\ \text{ under } H_0 \]	Stat1 testing chi-squared
What is the two-sample t-test?	A test for the equality of two normal means with equal unknown variances, based on the difference of the sample means standardized by a pooled variance estimate.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{\bar X-\bar Y}{S_p\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}+\tfrac{1}{m}}}\ \sim\ t_{n+m-2} \]	Stat1 testing two-sample
What is the pooled variance estimator?	A weighted average of the two sample variances with weights based on their degrees of freedom. It combines both samples into a single estimate of the common variance.<br><br>\[ S_p^2=\frac{(n-1)S_X^2+(m-1)S_Y^2}{n+m-2} \]	Stat1 testing two-sample
How many degrees of freedom does the two-sample t-test have?	The sum of the two sample sizes minus 2, since two means have been estimated from the data.	Stat1 testing two-sample
What is the p-value?	The probability, computed under the null, of observing a test statistic at least as extreme in the direction of the alternative as the one actually observed.<br><br>\[ p=P_{H_0}\big(T\ge t_{\text{obs}}\big) \]	Stat1 testing p-value
What is the alternative interpretation of a p-value as a level?	It is the smallest significance level at which the null would still have been rejected given this observation. Compare it to alpha to decide.	Stat1 testing p-value
Does the p-value depend on the direction of the test problem?	Yes. Extremeness is measured in the direction of the alternative, so a one-sided and a two-sided problem give different p-values for the same data.	Stat1 testing p-value
How is a large p-value interpreted?	The observation is compatible with the null hypothesis, so there is no evidence to reject it. This is not proof that the null is true.	Stat1 testing p-value interpretation
What is a quantile function?	The generalized inverse of the CDF: the infimum of all values whose CDF is at least the given level. It is monotone increasing and left-continuous.<br><br>\[ q_\alpha=F^{-}(\alpha)=\inf\{x: F(x)\ge\alpha\} \]	Stat1 quantiles
What are the three defining properties of a CDF?	It is monotone increasing, right-continuous, and tends to 0 at minus infinity and 1 at plus infinity.	Stat1 probability cdf
What is the alpha-quantile characterization by probability?	A value q is an alpha-quantile if the probability of being at most q is at least alpha and the probability of being at least q is at least 1 minus alpha.<br><br>\[ P(X\le q)\ge\alpha\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(X\ge q)\ge 1-\alpha \]	Stat1 quantiles
Why can a discrete distribution have a whole interval of alpha-quantiles?	Because its CDF is a step function that jumps over the level alpha, so a range of values all satisfy the quantile condition. The quantile function returns the smallest one.	Stat1 quantiles
What is the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test?	A goodness-of-fit test based on the maximum absolute distance between the empirical CDF and a fully specified theoretical CDF.<br><br>\[ D_n=\sup_{x}\big|\hat F_n(x)-F_0(x)\big| \]	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
What is the key distribution-free property of the KS statistic?	Under the null, the distribution of the KS statistic does not depend on the hypothesized F at all. One universal table of critical values works for every continuous F.	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
Why is the KS statistic distribution-free?	Because applying the true CDF to the data turns them into uniform variables via the probability integral transform, reducing every case to the uniform one.	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
How is the KS statistic computed in practice?	Sort the data and take the maximum, over all observations, of the two distances at each jump: the gap above the step and the gap below it.<br><br>\[ D_n=\max_{1\le i\le n}\max\!\left\{\tfrac{i}{n}-F_0(x_{(i)}),\ F_0(x_{(i)})-\tfrac{i-1}{n}\right\} \]	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
What happens to the KS statistic as the sample size grows under the null?	It converges to zero almost surely, since the empirical CDF converges uniformly to the true CDF (Glivenko-Cantelli).	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
What is Kolmogorov's theorem about the scaled KS statistic?	Multiplied by the square root of n, the KS statistic converges to a universal limiting distribution, which supplies the approximate critical values for large samples.<br><br>\[ \sqrt{n}\,D_n\ \stackrel{d}{\to}\ K\quad\text{(Kolmogorov distribution)} \]	Stat1 testing goodness-of-fit
What is the multinomial distribution?	The joint distribution of the counts in each of s categories over n independent trials with fixed category probabilities. It generalizes the binomial to more than two outcomes.<br><br>\[ P(N_1=n_1,\dots,N_s=n_s)=\frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_s!}\,p_1^{n_1}\cdots p_s^{n_s} \]	Stat1 distributions multinomial
What is the covariance between two different multinomial counts?	Negative, equal to minus n times the product of the two category probabilities. More observations in one category necessarily mean fewer elsewhere.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(N_i,N_j)=-n\,p_ip_j,\qquad i\neq j \]	Stat1 distributions multinomial
What is the chi-squared goodness-of-fit test statistic?	The sum over categories of the squared difference between observed and expected counts divided by the expected count. It measures total relative discrepancy.<br><br>\[ T=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{(N_j-np_j)^2}{np_j}\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ \chi^2_{s-1} \]	Stat1 testing chi-squared goodness-of-fit
What is the asymptotic distribution of the chi-squared goodness-of-fit statistic?	Chi-squared with s-1 degrees of freedom, where s is the number of categories. One degree of freedom is lost because the counts must sum to n.	Stat1 testing chi-squared
What is the rule of thumb for the validity of the chi-squared goodness-of-fit test?	Every expected cell count should be at least 5. Otherwise the chi-squared approximation to the discrete statistic is unreliable.<br><br>\[ np_j\ \ge\ 5\quad\text{for all }j \]	Stat1 testing chi-squared
How is the chi-squared test applied to a continuous distribution?	Partition the range into disjoint intervals, compute the theoretical probability of each interval, and compare observed with expected counts as in the discrete case.	Stat1 testing chi-squared
What is the limitation of binning a continuous distribution for a chi-squared test?	It only checks agreement at the level of the chosen intervals. Two different distributions with identical interval probabilities are indistinguishable to the test.	Stat1 testing chi-squared
What is the transformation formula for densities?	If T is bijective with differentiable inverse, the density of T(X) is the original density at the inverse point multiplied by the absolute determinant of the Jacobian of the inverse.<br><br>\[ f_{T(X)}(y)=f_X\big(T^{-1}(y)\big)\,\big|\det J_{T^{-1}}(y)\big| \]	Stat1 probability transformation
Why does the Jacobian determinant appear in the density transformation?	Because it measures how the transformation stretches or compresses volume, and the density must be rescaled so that total probability mass remains 1.	Stat1 probability transformation
What is the characteristic function of a standard normal?	The exponential of minus one half of the squared argument. Its multivariate version has the same form with the squared norm of the argument vector.<br><br>\[ \varphi_Z(t)=e^{-t^2/2},\qquad \varphi_Z(t)=e^{-\|t\|^2/2}\ \text{(multivariate)} \]	Stat1 probability characteristic-function
