What is the general form of an exponential family?	A base function of the data times a normalizing function of the parameter times the exponential of an inner product between parameter functions and sufficient statistics.<br><br>\[ f(x\mid\theta)=h(x)\,c(\theta)\exp\!\Big\{\textstyle\sum_j w_j(\theta)\,T_j(x)\Big\} \]	StatInf exponential-family
What are the four key properties of exponential families?	The support does not depend on the parameters, the statistic T(x) is sufficient, the likelihood is concave so local maxima are global, and conjugate priors exist.	StatInf exponential-family
Why does the support have to be parameter-free in an exponential family?	Because the exponential structure factorizes the parameter dependence out of the data support. A parameter-dependent support like the uniform breaks this.	StatInf exponential-family
What is a location-scale family?	A family generated from one base distribution by shifting with a location parameter and stretching with a scale parameter. The normal and uniform families are examples.	StatInf distributions
What is a mixture distribution?	A weighted combination of several component distributions with weights summing to 1. It models a heterogeneous population made of distinct subgroups.	StatInf distributions mixture
What are the assumptions on the response in a linear model with OLS?	The responses are conditionally independent given the covariates but not identically distributed, since each has its own mean. The errors are i.i.d. normal.	StatInf linear-model
How does a GLM generalize the linear model's distributional assumption?	The conditional response follows an exponential family distribution instead of a normal, and the mean is linked to the linear predictor through a response function.	StatInf glm
What is an autoregressive model for longitudinal data?	A model where each observation depends on the previous one within the same subject plus covariates. It is a Markov model: the conditional mean depends only on the immediately preceding value.	StatInf longitudinal
What is a statistic?	A measurable function of the data, usually mapping into a lower-dimensional space. It performs dimension reduction while ideally preserving the relevant information.	StatInf sufficiency
What does it mean for a statistic to be sufficient?	It contains all the information about the parameter in the data: conditional on the statistic, the distribution of the data no longer depends on the parameter.	StatInf sufficiency
Why is the full sample trivially sufficient?	Because conditioning on the entire dataset leaves nothing random that depends on the parameter. The point of sufficiency is to reduce the dimension further.	StatInf sufficiency
Is a bijective function of a sufficient statistic still sufficient?	Yes. A one-to-one transformation neither adds nor destroys information.	StatInf sufficiency
What does the factorization theorem state?	A statistic is sufficient exactly when the density factors into a function of the data alone times a function depending on the parameter only through that statistic.<br><br>\[ f(x\mid\theta)=g\big(T(x),\theta\big)\cdot h(x) \]	StatInf sufficiency factorization
What is the sufficient statistic in an exponential family?	The T(x) appearing in the exponent. Exponential families always have a natural low-dimensional sufficient statistic.<br><br>\[ T(x)\ \text{in}\ \ f(x\mid\eta)=h(x)\exp\big\{\eta^{\top}T(x)-A(\eta)\big\} \]	StatInf sufficiency exponential-family
What is minimal sufficiency?	A statistic that is sufficient and is a function of every other sufficient statistic. It achieves the greatest possible data reduction without losing information.	StatInf sufficiency minimal
How is minimal sufficiency shown in practice?	Show that the likelihood ratio between two parameter values is free of the parameter exactly when the statistic takes the same value at both data points.<br><br>\[ \frac{f(x\mid\theta_1)}{f(x\mid\theta_2)}\ \text{free of }\theta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(x)=T(y) \]	StatInf sufficiency minimal
What is a complete statistic?	One for which the only function with zero expectation for every parameter value is the function that is almost surely zero. Completeness rules out non-trivial unbiased estimators of zero.<br><br>\[ E_\theta\big[g(T)\big]=0\ \forall\theta\quad\Rightarrow\quad g(T)=0\ \text{a.s.} \]	StatInf completeness
What does the Lehmann-Scheffe theorem give you?	If a statistic is both sufficient and complete, it is minimal sufficient, and conditioning an unbiased estimator on it produces the uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator.	StatInf lehmann-scheffe
What is an ancillary statistic?	One whose distribution does not depend on the parameter at all. It carries no information about the parameter on its own.	StatInf ancillarity
What is the bias of an estimator? (Statistical Inference)	The difference between its expectation and the true parameter value. A zero bias means the estimator is unbiased.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{Bias}(\hat\theta)=E_\theta(\hat\theta)-\theta \]	StatInf estimation bias
How does MSE decompose?	Into the variance plus the squared bias. Total error combines random scatter with systematic offset.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta)=\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)+\mathrm{Bias}(\hat\theta)^2 \]	StatInf estimation mse
What is an efficient estimator?	An unbiased estimator with the smallest possible variance, that is, one attaining the Cramer-Rao lower bound.	StatInf estimation efficiency
What is an admissible estimator?	One that is not strictly dominated in MSE by any other estimator across the whole parameter space. An inadmissible estimator is beaten everywhere by some competitor.	StatInf estimation admissibility
What are Fisher-regular distributions and why do they matter?	Distributions where differentiation and integration can be interchanged. Regularity is what makes the score have zero mean and the Cramer-Rao bound valid.	StatInf regularity
What is the score function? (Statistical Inference)	The derivative of the log-likelihood with respect to the parameter. Setting it to zero gives the maximum likelihood equations.<br><br>\[ S(\theta)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log L(\theta\mid x) \]	StatInf likelihood score
What is observed information?	The negative second derivative of the log-likelihood, evaluated at the observed data. It measures the curvature of the actual likelihood surface.<br><br>\[ J(\theta)=-\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta\partial\theta^{\top}}\log L(\theta) \]	StatInf likelihood information
What is Fisher information?	The expected value of the observed information, equivalently the variance of the score under regularity conditions.<br><br>\[ I(\theta)=E\big[J(\theta)\big]=\operatorname{Var}\big(S(\theta)\big) \]	StatInf likelihood fisher-information
What are the two key Fisher-regularity identities?	The score has expectation zero, and its covariance equals the Fisher information.<br><br>\[ E\big[S(\theta)\big]=0,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}\big(S(\theta)\big)=I(\theta) \]	StatInf likelihood fisher-information
What is the relation between the Fisher information of a statistic and of the full data?	The information in any statistic is at most that of the full data, with equality exactly when the statistic is sufficient.	StatInf sufficiency fisher-information
What does the Cramer-Rao inequality state?	For a Fisher-regular model, the variance of any unbiased estimator is at least the reciprocal of the Fisher information.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}\big(\hat\theta\big)\ \ge\ \frac{1}{I(\theta)} \]	StatInf estimation cramer-rao
How does the Cramer-Rao bound generalize to a transformed parameter?	The bound becomes the squared derivative of the transformation divided by the Fisher information, via the delta method.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}\big(g(\hat\theta)\big)\ \ge\ \frac{g'(\theta)^2}{I(\theta)} \]	StatInf estimation cramer-rao
What is a UMVU estimator?	The uniformly minimum variance unbiased estimator: unbiased with the smallest variance for every parameter value.	StatInf estimation umvu
Is every UMVU estimator efficient?	Not necessarily. The sample variance with n-1 in the denominator is UMVU but does not attain the Cramer-Rao bound.	StatInf estimation umvu
What does the Rao-Blackwell theorem do?	It improves an unbiased estimator by conditioning it on a sufficient statistic. The result stays unbiased and has variance no larger than the original.<br><br>\[ \tilde\theta=E\big(\hat\theta\mid T\big),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\tilde\theta)\le\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta) \]	StatInf estimation rao-blackwell
Why does conditioning on a sufficient statistic never increase variance?	By the variance decomposition: the variance of the conditional expectation is the total variance minus the expected conditional variance, which is non-negative.	StatInf estimation rao-blackwell
What are the three notions of consistency?	Weak consistency is convergence in probability, MSE consistency means the MSE tends to zero, and strong consistency is almost sure convergence.	StatInf asymptotics consistency
What is a sufficient condition for weak consistency via Chebyshev?	MSE consistency implies weak consistency, since Chebyshev bounds the deviation probability by the MSE over the squared tolerance.	StatInf asymptotics consistency
What does the continuous mapping theorem state?	If an estimator is consistent for a parameter, then a continuous function of it is consistent for the same function of the parameter.	StatInf asymptotics
What is the asymptotic normality result for ML and least squares estimators?	Scaled by the square root of n, the estimation error converges in distribution to a multivariate normal with the inverse Fisher information as covariance.<br><br>\[ \sqrt{n}\big(\hat\theta_n-\theta\big)\ \stackrel{d}{\to}\ N\!\big(0,\ I_1(\theta)^{-1}\big) \]	StatInf asymptotics
What is a BAN estimator?	Best asymptotically normal: consistent, asymptotically normal, and attaining the asymptotic Cramer-Rao bound. ML estimators are BAN under regularity.	StatInf asymptotics
What is the delta method? (Statistical Inference)	A rule for the asymptotic distribution of a smooth transformation of an asymptotically normal estimator: the asymptotic variance is multiplied by the squared derivative.<br><br>\[ \sqrt{n}\big(g(\hat\theta)-g(\theta)\big)\ \stackrel{d}{\to}\ N\!\big(0,\ g'(\theta)^2\,\sigma^2\big) \]	StatInf asymptotics delta-method
What is the multivariate version of the delta method?	The asymptotic covariance is sandwiched between the Jacobian of the transformation and its transpose.<br><br>\[ \Sigma_{g}=\nabla g(\theta)^{\top}\,\Sigma\,\nabla g(\theta) \]	StatInf asymptotics delta-method
What is the difference between simple and composite hypotheses?	A simple hypothesis specifies a single parameter value, while a composite one specifies a set of values.	StatInf testing
What is a type 1 versus a type 2 error?	Type 1 is rejecting a true null, type 2 is failing to reject a false null. The type 1 error rate is capped at alpha and the type 2 rate is then minimized.	StatInf testing errors
What is the power of a test?	One minus the type 2 error probability: the probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis.	StatInf testing power
What is the power function?	The rejection probability as a function of the true parameter. On the null region it gives the type 1 error rate, on the alternative region it gives the power.<br><br>\[ \beta(\theta)=P_\theta(\text{reject }H_0) \]	StatInf testing power
What is randomization in testing and why is it needed?	Rejecting with an intermediate probability at the critical value. It is needed for discrete test statistics, where the level cannot be attained exactly otherwise.	StatInf testing randomization
How is the randomization probability determined?	It is chosen so that the strict rejection probability plus gamma times the probability mass at the critical value equals exactly alpha.<br><br>\[ P_{\theta_0}(T\gt c)+\gamma\,P_{\theta_0}(T=c)=\alpha \]	StatInf testing randomization
What is a likelihood ratio test?	A test rejecting when the ratio of the alternative to the null likelihood exceeds a critical value, with randomization at equality.<br><br>\[ \Lambda(x)=\frac{L(\theta_1\mid x)}{L(\theta_0\mid x)}\ \gtrless\ k \]	StatInf testing likelihood-ratio
What does the Neyman-Pearson lemma state?	For a simple null against a simple alternative, the likelihood ratio test has maximal power among all level-alpha tests. It is the optimal test in that setting.	StatInf testing neyman-pearson
What is a uniformly most powerful test? (Statistical Inference)	A level-alpha test whose power is at least that of any other level-alpha test at every parameter in the alternative region.	StatInf testing ump
When does a UMP test exist for a one-sided problem?	When the likelihood ratio is monotone in a statistic. Then the same rejection region is optimal for every alternative simultaneously.	StatInf testing ump
Which family always has a monotone likelihood ratio?	The exponential family. This is why one-sided UMP tests exist for essentially all standard models.	StatInf testing ump exponential-family
What is an unbiased test? (Statistical Inference)	One whose rejection probability is at most alpha under the null and at least alpha under every alternative. It is never anti-correlated with the truth.<br><br>\[ \beta(\theta)\le\alpha\ \forall\theta\in\Theta_0,\qquad \beta(\theta)\ge\alpha\ \forall\theta\in\Theta_1 \]	StatInf testing unbiasedness
Why is the class of unbiased tests needed for two-sided problems?	Because no UMP test exists two-sided: the two directions demand contradictory rejection regions. Restricting to unbiased tests restores an optimum.	StatInf testing umpu
What is a UMPU test and what does it require?	The uniformly most powerful unbiased test. For two-sided problems it requires a one-parameter exponential family and rejects in both tails.	StatInf testing umpu
What are the two conditions determining the critical values of a UMPU test?	The rejection probability under the null must equal alpha, and the derivative of the power function must vanish at the null parameter.<br><br>\[ \beta(\theta_0)=\alpha,\qquad \beta'(\theta_0)=0 \]	StatInf testing umpu
What is the duality between tests and confidence intervals?	A test fixes the parameter and asks which data are consistent with it; a confidence interval fixes the data and asks which parameters are consistent. The interval is the acceptance region inverted.	StatInf confidence-intervals duality
What is false coverage?	The probability that a confidence interval covers a wrong parameter value. Good intervals minimize it.	StatInf confidence-intervals
What is a uniformly most accurate confidence interval?	One minimizing the probability of false coverage among all intervals of the given level. It is obtained by inverting UMP tests and is generally one-sided.	StatInf confidence-intervals
What is the likelihood function? (Statistical Inference)	The density of the data viewed as a function of the parameter with the data held fixed. It measures how well each parameter explains the observations.<br><br>\[ L(\theta\mid x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(x_i\mid\theta) \]	StatInf likelihood
How does the joint density factorize for i.i.d., independent, and dependent data?	For i.i.d. data it is the product of identical densities, for merely independent data a product of different densities, and for dependent data a product of conditional densities via the chain rule.	StatInf likelihood
What is the first-order Markov property in a likelihood?	Each conditional density depends only on the immediately preceding observation, so the likelihood is a product of one-step transitions times the initial density.<br><br>\[ L=f(x_1)\prod_{i=2}^{n}f\big(x_i\mid x_{i-1}\big) \]	StatInf likelihood markov
Why is the likelihood itself minimal sufficient?	Because the likelihood is a function of every sufficient statistic, which is exactly the defining property of minimal sufficiency.	StatInf likelihood sufficiency
What is the KL divergence between a true density g and a model?	The expectation under g of the log ratio of g to the model density. It is non-negative and zero only when the two coincide.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{KL}(g\,\|\,f_\theta)=\int g(x)\log\frac{g(x)}{f_\theta(x)}\,dx\ \ge\ 0 \]	StatInf kl-divergence
What is the relationship between minimizing KL divergence and maximizing likelihood?	They are equivalent: the term involving g alone does not depend on the parameter, so minimizing KL reduces to maximizing the expected log-likelihood.	StatInf kl-divergence likelihood
What does the ML estimator converge to under model misspecification?	The parameter minimizing the KL divergence to the true distribution, that is, the best approximation available within the assumed model family.	StatInf asymptotics misspecification
How is Newton-Raphson applied to ML estimation?	Linearize the score function and set it to zero, giving an update that adds the inverse observed information times the score to the current estimate.<br><br>\[ \theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+\big[J(\theta^{(t)})\big]^{-1}S\big(\theta^{(t)}\big) \]	StatInf numerics newton-raphson
How does Fisher scoring differ from Newton-Raphson?	It replaces the observed information with the expected (Fisher) information, which is always positive definite and often cheaper to compute.<br><br>\[ \theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+\big[I(\theta^{(t)})\big]^{-1}S\big(\theta^{(t)}\big) \]	StatInf numerics fisher-scoring
What problem does the EM algorithm solve?	Maximum likelihood estimation with incomplete data, where some variables are unobserved or latent.	StatInf em-algorithm
What are the two steps of the EM algorithm?	The E step computes the expected complete-data log-likelihood over the latent variables given the current parameters; the M step maximizes that expectation to update the parameters.<br><br>\[ Q\big(\theta\mid\theta^{(t)}\big)=E_{Z\mid x,\theta^{(t)}}\big[\log L(\theta\mid x,Z)\big],\qquad \theta^{(t+1)}=\arg\max_\theta Q \]	StatInf em-algorithm
What is the key guarantee of the EM algorithm?	The observed-data log-likelihood never decreases from one iteration to the next, so the sequence converges to a stationary point.	StatInf em-algorithm
What is the intuition behind EM?	If the latent variables were known, the parameters could be estimated by ordinary maximum likelihood; if the parameters were known, the latent variables could be inferred by Bayes. EM alternates between the two.	StatInf em-algorithm
How does EM apply to a mixture model?	The latent variable is the component membership of each observation. The E step computes posterior membership probabilities and the M step re-estimates the component parameters and mixture weights.	StatInf em-algorithm mixture
How does EM handle missing values in a bivariate normal?	Missing coordinates are replaced by their conditional expectations given the observed coordinate and the current parameter estimates, then the parameters are re-estimated.	StatInf em-algorithm missing-data
What are the two ingredients of model selection criteria?	Goodness of fit through the maximized log-likelihood, and complexity through the number of parameters.	StatInf model-selection
What is the AIC and what does it estimate?	Minus twice the log-likelihood plus twice the number of parameters. It estimates the expected KL divergence between the true density and the fitted model.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{AIC}=-2\,\ell(\hat\theta)+2p \]	StatInf model-selection aic
Why is the absolute value of an AIC meaningless?	Because it contains an unknown constant from the true density. Only differences between AIC values of competing models are interpretable.	StatInf model-selection aic
When should the corrected AIC be used?	For small samples, specifically when the ratio of sample size to number of parameters is below about 40. It adds a finite-sample bias correction.	StatInf model-selection aic
How does BIC differ from AIC?	BIC penalizes with the log of the sample size times the number of parameters instead of 2. It penalizes complexity more heavily and favours smaller models as n grows.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{BIC}=-2\,\ell(\hat\theta)+\log(n)\cdot p \]	StatInf model-selection bic
What is Bayes' theorem for inference?	The posterior is proportional to the likelihood times the prior, normalized by the marginal likelihood of the data.<br><br>\[ p(\theta\mid x)=\frac{f(x\mid\theta)\,p(\theta)}{\int f(x\mid\theta)p(\theta)\,d\theta}\ \propto\ f(x\mid\theta)p(\theta) \]	StatInf bayes
Why can the normalizing constant often be ignored in Bayesian computation?	Because it does not depend on the parameter, so the posterior shape is fully determined by the likelihood times the prior.	StatInf bayes
What are the three standard Bayesian point estimates?	The posterior mean, the posterior median (the 50 percent quantile), and the posterior mode, also called the maximum a posteriori estimate.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta_{\text{mean}}=E(\theta\mid x),\quad \hat\theta_{\text{med}}=q_{0.5},\quad \hat\theta_{MAP}=\arg\max_\theta p(\theta\mid x) \]	StatInf bayes estimation
What is an improper prior?	A prior that does not integrate to a finite value, so it is not a real probability distribution. It can still yield a proper posterior.	StatInf bayes prior
What does a uniform prior imply for the MAP estimate?	The MAP estimate equals the ML estimate, since a flat prior contributes nothing to the maximization.	StatInf bayes prior
What is a conjugate prior?	A prior whose family is preserved by the update, so the posterior belongs to the same family. It makes the posterior available in closed form.	StatInf bayes conjugate-prior
What are the conjugate priors for the Bernoulli, Poisson and exponential likelihoods?	Beta for Bernoulli, and Gamma for both Poisson and exponential. The Dirichlet is conjugate to the multinomial.	StatInf bayes conjugate-prior
How does the Beta posterior update for Bernoulli data?	The first parameter gains the number of successes and the second gains the number of failures. The prior parameters act like pseudo-observations.<br><br>\[ \theta\mid x\ \sim\ \mathrm{Beta}\Big(a+\textstyle\sum_i x_i,\ \ b+n-\sum_i x_i\Big) \]	StatInf bayes conjugate-prior
What is the Jeffreys prior?	A prior proportional to the square root of the Fisher information. It is invariant under reparameterization.<br><br>\[ p(\theta)\ \propto\ \sqrt{I(\theta)} \]	StatInf bayes jeffreys-prior
Why is a flat prior not a satisfactory non-informative prior?	Because flatness is not transformation invariant: reparameterizing turns a flat prior into a non-flat one through the Jacobian factor.	StatInf bayes jeffreys-prior
What does the Jeffreys prior maximize?	The expected information gain from the data, measured as the KL divergence between posterior and prior averaged over datasets.	StatInf bayes jeffreys-prior
What is a credible interval and how does it differ from a confidence interval?	A credible interval is a fixed region containing the random parameter with a given posterior probability. In a confidence interval the parameter is fixed and the interval is random.	StatInf bayes credible-interval
How is a credible interval constructed?	From quantiles of the posterior distribution.	StatInf bayes credible-interval
What is the posterior odds ratio used for?	Comparing two parameter values when the posterior is not normalized, since the unknown normalizing constant cancels in the ratio.	StatInf bayes
What is the predictive distribution in Bayesian inference?	The distribution of a new unobserved value, obtained by integrating the data distribution against the posterior. It accounts for parameter uncertainty.<br><br>\[ p\big(x_{\text{new}}\mid x\big)=\int f\big(x_{\text{new}}\mid\theta\big)\,p(\theta\mid x)\,d\theta \]	StatInf bayes prediction
What is a loss function in Bayesian decision theory?	A function measuring the cost of a decision given the true parameter. Its expectation over the parameter is the risk.	StatInf decision-theory
What is the minimax decision rule?	The rule minimizing the worst-case risk over all parameter values. It protects against the least favourable scenario.<br><br>\[ \delta^*=\arg\min_\delta\ \sup_\theta\ R(\theta,\delta) \]	StatInf decision-theory minimax
What is the Bayes risk and the Bayes optimal decision?	Bayes risk is the risk averaged over the prior; the Bayes optimal decision minimizes it. It can be found pointwise for each observed dataset.<br><br>\[ r(\delta)=\int R(\theta,\delta)\,p(\theta)\,d\theta,\qquad \delta^*=\arg\min_\delta r(\delta) \]	StatInf decision-theory bayes
Which Bayesian estimator is optimal under squared error loss?	The posterior mean. Differentiating the expected squared loss and setting it to zero yields exactly the posterior expectation.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta=E\big(\theta\mid x\big) \]	StatInf decision-theory bayes
Which Bayesian estimator is optimal under absolute error loss?	The posterior median.	StatInf decision-theory bayes
Which Bayesian estimator is optimal under 0-1 loss?	The posterior mode, obtained as the limit of a loss penalizing deviations beyond a shrinking tolerance.	StatInf decision-theory bayes
What is the relationship between admissibility and Bayes optimality?	A Bayes optimal decision rule is admissible exactly when it is not dominated by another rule.	StatInf decision-theory admissibility
What is the idea behind Markov Chain Monte Carlo?	Construct a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is the target posterior, then use its simulated path as approximate samples from that posterior.	StatInf mcmc
What three properties must a Markov chain have for MCMC to work?	Homogeneity (fixed transition probabilities), irreducibility (every state reachable), and aperiodicity (no cycles).	StatInf mcmc
What is burn-in?	Discarding the first portion of a chain, since the early samples still reflect the starting value rather than the stationary distribution.	StatInf mcmc
How are expectations estimated from an MCMC run?	By averaging the function of interest over the retained samples after burn-in.<br><br>\[ E\big[g(\theta)\mid x\big]\ \approx\ \frac{1}{M-b}\sum_{t=b+1}^{M}g\big(\theta^{(t)}\big) \]	StatInf mcmc
What is the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm?	Propose a candidate from a proposal distribution, accept it with a probability based on the ratio of target and proposal densities, otherwise keep the current state.<br><br>\[ \alpha=\min\!\left(1,\ \frac{p(\theta^*)\,q(\theta^{(t)}\mid\theta^*)}{p(\theta^{(t)})\,q(\theta^*\mid\theta^{(t)})}\right) \]	StatInf mcmc metropolis-hastings
Why does Metropolis-Hastings not require the normalizing constant?	Because the acceptance probability involves a ratio of target densities, in which the unknown normalizing constant cancels.	StatInf mcmc metropolis-hastings
What is Gibbs sampling?	An MCMC method that updates one coordinate at a time by drawing from its full conditional distribution, cycling through all coordinates.	StatInf mcmc gibbs
What is a full conditional distribution?	The conditional posterior of one parameter given all the others and the data. It is proportional to the joint distribution with all other arguments held fixed.<br><br>\[ p\big(\theta_j\mid \theta_{-j},x\big)\ \propto\ p\big(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_p\mid x\big) \]	StatInf mcmc gibbs
When is Gibbs sampling preferable to Metropolis-Hastings?	When all full conditionals are known in closed form and easy to sample from, since then every proposal is accepted.	StatInf mcmc gibbs
What controls the acceptance rate in multivariate Metropolis-Hastings?	The covariance matrix of the multivariate proposal distribution. It sets the typical proposal step size.	StatInf mcmc metropolis-hastings
What is the Bayesian linear model with a Jeffreys prior?	A linear model where the joint posterior factorizes into an inverse chi-squared distribution for the variance and a normal distribution for the coefficients given that variance.	StatInf bayes linear-model
What is the orthogonality result underlying the Bayesian linear model derivation?	The residual vector is orthogonal to both the design matrix columns and the fitted values, which lets the quadratic form split cleanly into a residual part and a coefficient part.	StatInf bayes linear-model
What is a marginal posterior?	The posterior of one parameter obtained by integrating out all the others. It reflects the uncertainty in the nuisance parameters.	StatInf bayes multi-parameter
Why is the incorrect specification of a linear model a real risk?	The normality assumption on the errors may be wrong, and the true conditional mean may be non-linear, so the linear form misrepresents the actual relationship.	StatInf model-checking
What is the ML estimator's relation to the least squares estimator in the linear model?	They coincide, and this common estimator is UMVU for the coefficients.	StatInf linear-model estimation
Why is the sample variance with n-1 in the denominator UMVU but not efficient?	It does not attain the Cramer-Rao bound, but Lehmann-Scheffe still establishes minimum variance among unbiased estimators via sufficiency and completeness.	StatInf estimation umvu
