What is longitudinal data?	Repeated measurements on the same subject at different time points. When the time points become very dense it turns into functional data.	RCD longitudinal basics
What is the difference between a cohort study and a randomized trial?	A cohort or panel study follows a group of people with shared characteristics over time; a randomized trial assigns subjects randomly to treatment groups.	RCD study-design
What does balanced longitudinal data mean?	Every subject has the same number of observations. Equally spaced means the gap between successive observation times is constant.	RCD longitudinal terminology
What is a time-invariant covariate?	A covariate that does not change over the observation period, such as gender.	RCD longitudinal terminology
What are the two sources of variation in longitudinal data?	Inter-subject variation between different subjects, and intra-subject variation within the same subject across measurements.	RCD longitudinal variation
Why is longitudinal data more informative than cross-sectional data?	Because individual changes over time can be observed, rather than only differences between individuals at one moment.	RCD longitudinal
How does longitudinal data protect against confounding?	Time-constant confounders are eliminated by taking within-subject differences, since the constant term cancels out of the difference.<br><br>\[ E\big(Y_{ij}-Y_{ik}\big)=\beta_1\big(x_{ij}-x_{ik}\big) \]	RCD longitudinal confounding
What role does a random intercept play with respect to confounding?	It captures the time-constant subject-specific confounder, so that differences within a subject are free of it.	RCD longitudinal confounding
What are the main challenges of longitudinal data?	The data are correlated with correlation typically decaying over time, dropouts create missing data, and time-varying covariates raise lag-structure and endogeneity questions.	RCD longitudinal challenges
What is covariate endogeneity in longitudinal data?	When the response influences the later value of a covariate, so the covariate is no longer external to the process being modelled.	RCD longitudinal endogeneity
What goes wrong if you fit ordinary linear regression to longitudinal data?	The residuals are not independent, which violates a core assumption and invalidates the standard errors.	RCD longitudinal naive-methods
What is the drawback of a purely cross-sectional analysis?	It only captures the population trend and implicitly assumes the same trend for everyone, and it requires measurements at common time points.	RCD longitudinal naive-methods
What is the drawback of fitting a separate regression per individual?	Each model has very little data, so the individual estimates are extremely imprecise.	RCD longitudinal naive-methods
What is a marginal model in longitudinal analysis?	A model of the population mean together with a model for the variances and covariances, with correlation accounted for through robust standard errors.	RCD marginal-model
What is a mixed model in longitudinal analysis?	A conditional model with subject-specific means, where parameters are estimated from the marginal likelihood after integrating out the random effects.	RCD mixed-model
What is the asymmetry between mixed and marginal models?	A mixed model always implies a specific marginal model, but a marginal model does not determine a mixed model.	RCD mixed-model marginal-model
What are the goals of graphically displaying longitudinal data?	Show the original data, make trends visible, and highlight both cross-sectional and longitudinal patterns.	RCD visualization
What is a spaghetti plot?	A plot drawing one connected line per subject over time, showing all individual trajectories at once.	RCD visualization
What is a lasagna plot?	A heatmap-style display with time on the columns and subjects on the rows. The row ordering strongly controls the visual impression.	RCD visualization
Why are boxplots per time point useful?	They give a cross-sectional view of the distribution at each measurement occasion.	RCD visualization
Why are smooth means used for irregular longitudinal data?	Because subjects are measured at different time points, so no common grid exists for simple averaging. Note that smoothing methods assume i.i.d. errors.	RCD visualization smoothing
How is the correlation structure explored empirically?	By examining the correlation of measurements as a function of the time difference between them, since correlation typically shrinks over time.	RCD correlation-structure
What is the two-stage analysis motivation for mixed models?	Stage one fits a separate linear model per subject; stage two models the resulting subject coefficients with a linear model, for example split by gender.	RCD mixed-model two-stage
What are the drawbacks of two-stage analysis?	It fails when there are few observations per subject, information is lost through the two-step split, and stage two uses estimated rather than true coefficients, which adds uncertainty.	RCD mixed-model two-stage
What is the longitudinal linear mixed model?	The response for subject i equals a fixed-effects design times beta, plus a random-effects design times a subject-specific random vector, plus an error, with both random parts normal and independent.<br><br>\[ Y_i=X_i\beta+Z_ib_i+\varepsilon_i,\qquad b_i\sim N_q(0,D),\ \varepsilon_i\sim N_{n_i}(0,\Sigma_i) \]	RCD lmm
What can the fixed-effect design matrix contain in an LMM?	Both time-constant and time-varying covariates, including time itself.	RCD lmm
What is the independence structure of the random effects across and within subjects?	Random effects of different subjects are independent, but the components within one subject's random vector are correlated through the covariance matrix D.	RCD lmm
What assumption makes an LMM specifically longitudinal?	That the response can be split into independent blocks, one per subject.	RCD lmm
What is the conditional mean and covariance in an LMM?	The conditional mean given the random effects is the fixed part plus the random part; the conditional covariance is just the error covariance Sigma_i.<br><br>\[ E\big(Y_i\mid b_i\big)=X_i\beta+Z_ib_i,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}\big(Y_i\mid b_i\big)=\Sigma_i \]	RCD lmm conditional
What is the marginal mean and covariance in an LMM?	The marginal mean is the fixed part alone; the marginal covariance is Z D Z-transpose plus Sigma_i.<br><br>\[ E(Y_i)=X_i\beta,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(Y_i)=Z_iDZ_i^{\top}+\Sigma_i \]	RCD lmm marginal
How are the two parts of the marginal covariance interpreted?	The Z D Z-transpose part is the subject's deviation from the population, and Sigma_i is the deviation from that subject's own mean.	RCD lmm marginal
What is conditional independence in an LMM?	When the error covariance is a scalar multiple of the identity, so all within-subject correlation is generated purely by the random effects.	RCD lmm conditional
Why is it costly to treat subject effects as fixed rather than random?	Because every subject effect must then be estimated as its own parameter, which consumes many degrees of freedom.	RCD lmm fixed-vs-random
Do fixed effects have the same interpretation in the marginal and conditional views of an LMM?	Yes, in the linear mixed model the fixed effects mean the same thing in both. This is not true for non-linear links.	RCD lmm interpretation
What does an interaction between time and a covariate model?	A group-specific difference in the growth rate, whereas the covariate main effect models a difference in the intercept.<br><br>\[ E\big(Y_{ij}\big)=\beta_0+\beta_1t_{ij}+\beta_2g_i+\beta_3\,g_it_{ij} \]	RCD lmm interactions
How can a non-linear time trend be modelled?	With polynomial terms in time, a piecewise-linear specification with a break point, or by treating time as a categorical factor with a dummy per time point.	RCD lmm time-trend
What correlation structure does a random intercept induce?	A constant correlation between any two measurements of the same subject, equal to the random intercept variance divided by the sum of that variance and the error variance.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Corr}\big(Y_{ij},Y_{ik}\big)=\frac{d_{11}}{d_{11}+\sigma^2}=\rho \]	RCD lmm random-intercept
What is the key limitation of the random intercept correlation structure?	The correlation is the same for all subjects and constant over time, which often contradicts the observed decay of correlation with time distance.	RCD lmm random-intercept
What is the random intercept and random slope model?	A model where each subject gets both their own intercept deviation and their own slope deviation, with a two-by-two covariance matrix D linking them.<br><br>\[ Y_{ij}=\beta_0+\beta_1t_{ij}+b_{i1}+b_{i2}t_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad b_i\sim N_2(0,D) \]	RCD lmm random-slope
Why can a covariance entry in the marginal model be negative when a variance cannot?	Because in the normal LMM the diagonal entry of D is a variance and must be positive, but in the marginal model it acts as a covariance between measurements, which may be negative.	RCD lmm marginal
What is the ML estimator of the fixed effects for known covariance?	The generalized least squares estimator, which weights by the inverse marginal covariance. It is the best linear unbiased estimator.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_{ML}(\alpha)=\big(X^{\top}V(\alpha)^{-1}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}V(\alpha)^{-1}Y \]	RCD estimation gls
Why is the fixed-effect estimate dependent on the covariance specification?	Because the covariance enters as the weighting matrix, so a different assumed correlation structure produces different weights and therefore different estimates.	RCD estimation gls
What is the profile likelihood in mixed model estimation?	The marginal likelihood with the fixed effects replaced by their conditional maximizer, leaving a function of the variance parameters alone.	RCD estimation profile-likelihood
Why does ML give downward-biased variance estimates?	Because the mean has to be estimated first, and that estimation is not accounted for. It is the same reason the naive variance estimator divides by n instead of n-1.<br><br>\[ \hat\sigma^2=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(Y_i-\hat\mu\big)^2\quad\text{(REML analogue)} \]	RCD estimation reml
What is REML in one sentence?	The idea behind dividing by n-1 rather than n, applied to the linear mixed model: estimate the variance parameters from data transformed to be free of the fixed effects.	RCD estimation reml
How does REML technically eliminate the fixed effects?	It finds a transformation matrix A with A times X equal to zero, so the transformed data have expectation zero and their likelihood carries no information about beta.<br><br>\[ AX=0\ \Rightarrow\ AY\sim N\big(0,\ AVA^{\top}\big) \]	RCD estimation reml
How are mixed models fitted numerically?	With the EM algorithm or Newton-Raphson, since the likelihood has no closed-form maximizer in the variance parameters.	RCD estimation numerics
How are random effects predicted?	By the Bayesian posterior mean: the conditional expectation of the random effect given the observed data for that subject.<br><br>\[ \hat b_i=E\big(b_i\mid Y_i=y_i\big),\qquad E\big(\hat b_i\big)=0 \]	RCD prediction random-effects
Is the predictor of a random effect unbiased?	Yes, its expectation is zero, matching the expectation of the random effect itself.	RCD prediction random-effects
What is shrinkage in random effect prediction?	Predicted random effects have less variability than the corresponding fixed-effect estimates would. Subjects with little data are pulled harder toward the population mean.	RCD prediction shrinkage
What are the Henderson mixed model equations?	The joint system whose solutions are the fixed-effect estimates and the random-effect predictions, arising from maximizing the joint log-likelihood of the response and the random effects.	RCD estimation henderson
What are the two perspectives that give the same fixed effect estimates?	The Henderson equations take the conditional model perspective, while the GLS derivation takes the marginal model perspective; both yield the same beta.	RCD estimation
What is the model-based covariance of the fixed effects and its weakness?	It is the inverse of the weighted cross-product matrix. It is not robust against misspecification of the covariance structure and ignores the uncertainty in estimating the variance parameters.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}\big(\hat\beta\big)=\big(X^{\top}V^{-1}X\big)^{-1} \]	RCD inference covariance
What is the sandwich estimator?	A robust covariance estimator that keeps the model covariance in the outer factors but substitutes the empirical covariance in the middle. It is consistent even if the covariance structure is wrong, provided the mean is correctly specified.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}\big(\hat\beta\big)=\big(X^{\top}V^{-1}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}V^{-1}\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(Y)V^{-1}X\big(X^{\top}V^{-1}X\big)^{-1} \]	RCD inference sandwich
When is the sandwich estimator appropriate?	With a large number of subjects, since its robustness is an asymptotic property in N.	RCD inference sandwich
How is a general linear hypothesis about fixed effects written?	As a contrast matrix L times beta equal to zero, where L selects which coefficients or combinations are being tested.<br><br>\[ H_0:\ L\beta=0\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad H_1:\ L\beta\neq 0 \]	RCD inference testing
What is the weakness of the Wald test in a mixed model?	It ignores the uncertainty in the estimated variance parameters and underestimates the uncertainty of beta, so t and F tests are usually preferred.	RCD inference testing
Why can the likelihood ratio test not be used with REML for fixed effects?	Because REML transforms the data in a way that depends on the fixed-effect design, so likelihoods from models with different fixed effects are not comparable.	RCD inference reml
When should the likelihood ratio test be used with REML?	When testing the variance parameters rather than the fixed effects. For variance components REML-based LR tests are valid.	RCD inference reml
Why is testing a variance component at zero problematic?	Because zero lies on the boundary of the parameter space, violating the regularity condition for the usual chi-squared limit. The resulting p-values are too large.	RCD inference boundary
How is the boundary problem in variance testing handled?	By using a mixture of two chi-squared distributions with adjacent degrees of freedom as the reference distribution.<br><br>\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\chi^2_1+\tfrac{1}{2}\chi^2_2 \]	RCD inference boundary
How are penalized splines connected to mixed models?	The spline penalty term is equivalent to treating the spline coefficients as random effects, with the smoothing parameter equal to the error variance divided by the random effect variance.<br><br>\[ \min_{\beta,b}\ \|y-X\beta-Zb\|^2+\lambda\|b\|^2,\qquad \lambda=\frac{\sigma^2}{d^2} \]	RCD additive-models splines
What is the practical benefit of the spline-as-random-effect connection?	The smoothing parameter is estimated automatically from the data by ML or REML instead of being tuned by hand.	RCD additive-models splines
What is serial correlation in a longitudinal model?	Correlation among the errors beyond what the random effects generate, typically decaying with the time distance between measurements.	RCD serial-correlation
How is the error decomposed to model serial correlation?	Into an uncorrelated component and a correlated component, so the error covariance becomes a serial correlation matrix plus a scalar multiple of the identity.<br><br>\[ \varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_{(1)i}+\varepsilon_{(2)i},\qquad \Sigma_i=\tau^2H_i+\sigma^2I_{n_i} \]	RCD serial-correlation
What is the nugget effect?	The variance of the purely uncorrelated error component, which appears as a jump at zero distance in the covariance structure.	RCD serial-correlation nugget
What functional forms are used for the serial correlation?	Functions of the temporal distance such as the exponential or the squared exponential, both of which decay to zero as the distance grows.<br><br>\[ g(u)=\exp(-\phi u)\qquad\text{or}\qquad g(u)=\exp\big(-\phi u^2\big) \]	RCD serial-correlation
What is a semi-variogram?	Half the expected squared difference between two measurements separated by a time lag. It describes the covariance structure of a stationary process.<br><br>\[ v(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,E\Big[\big(Y(t)-Y(t-u)\big)^2\Big] \]	RCD serial-correlation variogram
How is a semi-variogram used as a diagnostic?	It is estimated from squared residual differences and inspected against the time lag; for correctly normalized residuals it should fluctuate randomly around 1.<br><br>\[ \hat v_{ijk}=\frac{\big(r_{ij}-r_{ik}\big)^2}{2}\ \longrightarrow\ 1\ \text{for normalized residuals} \]	RCD diagnostics variogram
What does variance heterogeneity mean in this context?	The error variance changes over time or with covariates rather than being constant, requiring an explicit variance function.	RCD variance-heterogeneity
Why should all random effect covariates also be included as fixed effects?	Because the random effects have mean zero, so the overall level of each such term must be carried by a corresponding fixed effect.	RCD model-building
What should residual plots against covariates or fitted values show?	No trend at all. A spread that widens systematically indicates that a random slope is missing from the model.	RCD diagnostics residuals
Why can raw residuals not be used directly in a mixed model?	Because they are correlated and heteroscedastic by construction, so patterns in them are hard to interpret.	RCD diagnostics residuals
What are normalized residuals?	Residuals pre-multiplied by the inverse Cholesky factor of the covariance matrix, which makes them uncorrelated with unit variance.<br><br>\[ r_i^{*}=L_i^{-1}r_i,\qquad V_i=L_iL_i^{\top} \]	RCD diagnostics residuals
What is the squared Mahalanobis distance in this setting?	The residual vector weighted by the inverse covariance matrix, measuring the distance between observed and fitted values in the correct metric.<br><br>\[ d_i=r_i^{*\top}r_i^{*}=\big(y_i-X_i\hat\beta\big)^{\top}V_i^{-1}\big(y_i-X_i\hat\beta\big) \]	RCD diagnostics mahalanobis
How are two mixed models compared?	If they have the same number of parameters, compare likelihoods directly; if they are nested, use a likelihood ratio test; otherwise use AIC or BIC.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{AIC}=-2\ell+2\,\mathrm{df},\qquad \mathrm{BIC}=-2\ell+\log(n)\,\mathrm{df} \]	RCD model-choice
What does minimizing AIC achieve?	It minimizes the estimated Kullback-Leibler distance to the true model.	RCD model-choice aic
What is marginal AIC and when is it appropriate?	AIC based on the marginal log-likelihood. It is appropriate when the focus is on population-level fixed effects, but it induces a preference for models with fewer random effects and must not be used with REML.	RCD model-choice aic
What is conditional AIC and when is it appropriate?	AIC based on the conditional log-likelihood given the random effects. It is appropriate when the focus is on the random effects and is available for both ML and REML.	RCD model-choice aic
What are the two central assumptions of the LMM?	The errors have zero mean conditional on the covariates and random effects, and the random effects have zero mean conditional on the covariates.<br><br>\[ E\big(\varepsilon_{ij}\mid x_{ij},b_i\big)=0,\qquad E\big(b_i\mid x_{ij}\big)=0 \]	RCD assumptions
What is the between-within decomposition of a covariate?	Splitting the covariate into the subject's own mean and the deviation from that mean, which gives separate between-subject and within-subject coefficients that can be confounded differently.<br><br>\[ Y_{ij}=\beta_0+\beta_B\bar x_i+\beta_W\big(x_{ij}-\bar x_i\big)+b_i+\varepsilon_{ij} \]	RCD confounding
When would you model subject effects as fixed rather than random?	When the random effect assumptions are doubtful. It is less efficient when those assumptions do hold, uses extra degrees of freedom, and may be infeasible when subjects have few observations.	RCD fixed-vs-random
Why can a GLM's mean and variance not be modelled separately?	Because every exponential family distribution fixes a specific relationship between the expected value and the variance. Only the Gaussian case decouples them.	RCD glm
What is the essential difference between a GLM and a marginal longitudinal model?	In the longitudinal setting the repeated measurements of one subject are conditionally associated given the covariates, whereas a GLM assumes independence.	RCD glm marginal-model
What are the three model types for binary longitudinal data?	The marginal model giving population-level inference with separate mean and correlation modelling, the mixed model giving individual-level inference and handling unequally spaced times, and the transition model where the current state depends on the previous observation.	RCD binary-longitudinal
What is a transition model?	A model where the linear predictor for the current response includes the previous observation as a covariate, so the current state depends on the last one.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{logit}\,E\big(Y_{ij}\mid Y_{ij-1},\dots\big)=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{ij}+\gamma\,Y_{ij-1} \]	RCD transition-model
Why is the marginal effect smaller than the conditional effect in a logistic mixed model?	Because averaging the non-linear logistic curves over subjects flattens the resulting population curve. The individual effect is always at least as large in absolute value as the population effect.<br><br>\[ \big|\beta_1\big|\ \le\ \big|\beta_1^{*}\big| \]	RCD glmm attenuation
What does the random intercept do to individual logistic curves?	It simply shifts each subject's curve left or right, while the fixed slope controls the steepness of every curve.	RCD glmm
What is the GLMM in one sentence?	Conditional on the random effects, the responses are independent and follow an exponential family distribution, with a link function connecting the conditional mean to a predictor containing fixed and random effects.<br><br>\[ g\big(\mu_{ij}\big)=x_{ij}^{\top}\beta+z_{ij}^{\top}b_i,\qquad \mu_{ij}=E\big(Y_{ij}\mid b_i\big) \]	RCD glmm
Why can you not switch freely between conditional and marginal interpretations in a GLMM?	Because with a non-linear link, the expectation of the inverse link is not the inverse link of the expectation, so the population mean is not obtained by simply setting the random effect to zero.<br><br>\[ E\Big[h\big(x^{\top}\beta+z^{\top}b\big)\Big]\ \neq\ h\Big(E\big[x^{\top}\beta+z^{\top}b\big]\Big) \]	RCD glmm interpretation
What is the conditional variance for logistic and Poisson GLMMs?	For logistic regression it is the conditional mean times one minus the conditional mean; for Poisson regression it equals the conditional mean itself.<br><br>\[ \text{logistic: }\mu_{ij}\big(1-\mu_{ij}\big)\qquad\text{Poisson: }\mu_{ij} \]	RCD glmm
Why is GLMM estimation computationally hard? (Correlated Data)	The marginal likelihood requires integrating the random effects out of a product over observations, which is a high-dimensional integral with no closed form.<br><br>\[ f\big(y_i\mid\beta,D,\phi\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^q}\prod_{j=1}^{n_i}f\big(y_{ij}\mid b_i\big)\,f\big(b_i\mid D\big)\,db_i \]	RCD glmm estimation
What are the three strategies for approximating the GLMM likelihood?	Approximate the data with penalized quasi-likelihood, approximate the integrand with a Laplace approximation, or approximate the integral itself with Gaussian quadrature.	RCD glmm estimation
What is penalized quasi-likelihood?	A method that uses a Taylor expansion to approximate the data and then applies ordinary linear mixed model machinery. It is only accurate near the linear case.	RCD glmm pql
What is the Laplace approximation in this context?	A second-order Taylor expansion of the integrand so that the integral becomes analytically solvable.	RCD glmm estimation
Why is no likelihood ratio test available after PQL estimation?	Because PQL estimates on pseudo-data rather than the actual likelihood, so the resulting likelihood values are not comparable.	RCD glmm pql
What is the practical recommendation for random effects with binary data?	Use only a random intercept, since binary responses carry too little information to identify more complex random effect structures.	RCD glmm binary
What are the three components of a marginal model for non-normal responses?	A mean model through a link function, a variance that depends on the marginal mean via a dispersion parameter, and a correlation modelled as a function of the marginal means and an association parameter.<br><br>\[ E(Y_{ij})=h\big(x_{ij}^{\top}\beta\big),\quad \operatorname{Var}(Y_{ij})=\phi\,v(\mu_{ij}),\quad \operatorname{Corr}\big(Y_{ij},Y_{ik}\big)=\rho\big(\mu_{ij},\mu_{ik};\alpha\big) \]	RCD gee marginal-model
Why are marginal models for non-normal data difficult to specify fully?	Because outside the multivariate normal case the first two moments do not determine the distribution, so additional association parameters would be needed.	RCD gee
What is the GEE solution to that difficulty?	Estimate only the first two moments of the response distribution rather than specifying the whole distribution.	RCD gee
What does GEE minimize?	The Mahalanobis distance between the observed responses and the modelled means, weighted by a working covariance matrix.<br><br>\[ \big[Y-\mu(\beta)\big]^{\top}V^{-1}\big[Y-\mu(\beta)\big] \]	RCD gee
What is the GEE score equation?	The sum over subjects of the derivative of the mean with respect to beta, transposed, times the inverse working covariance, times the residual, set equal to zero.<br><br>\[ S(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\partial\mu_i}{\partial\beta}\right)^{\!\top}V_i^{-1}\big(y_i-\mu_i(\beta)\big)=0 \]	RCD gee
How does the GEE score equation relate to GLS and GLM?	GLS is the special case where the mean is linear in beta, and GLM is the special case with independent observations. GEE generalizes both.	RCD gee
What is the key consistency property of the GEE estimator?	It is consistent for beta as the number of subjects grows, even if the covariance assumption is wrong.	RCD gee
What are the advantages of GEE?	It is almost as precise as the ML estimator, matches GLS in the multivariate normal case, and is robust against misspecification of the variance structure.	RCD gee
What are the disadvantages of GEE?	It is less efficient than ML when the covariance is correctly specified, its robustness is only asymptotic, and it does not produce a fully specified distribution.	RCD gee
Why is model selection harder with GEE?	Because there is no likelihood, so likelihood-based criteria and tests are unavailable.	RCD gee model-selection
What are the four key contrasts between GEE and GLMM?	GEE models mean and covariance separately and interprets on the population level; GLMM assumes conditional independence given the random effects and interprets on the individual level.	RCD gee glmm
What is the notation for missingness in longitudinal data?	An indicator that is 1 if the measurement was taken and 0 otherwise, splitting the response into an observed and a missing part.	RCD missing-data
What is the difference between a dropout and intermittent missingness?	A dropout leaves the study permanently from some time point onward; intermittent missingness means gaps appear somewhere in the middle with later observations still present.	RCD missing-data
What is missing completely at random (MCAR)?	The probability of being missing is unrelated to the responses or even to the covariates.<br><br>\[ P\big(R_{ij}=1\mid Y_i^{o},Y_i^{m},X_i\big)=P\big(R_{ij}=1\big) \]	RCD missing-data mcar
What is missing at random (MAR)?	The probability of being missing does not depend on the value that would have been observed, only on the observed data and covariates. Such values can be imputed.<br><br>\[ P\big(R_{ij}=1\mid Y_i^{o},Y_i^{m},X_i\big)=P\big(R_{ij}=1\mid Y_i^{o},X_i\big) \]	RCD missing-data mar
What is not missing at random (NMAR)?	The probability of being missing depends on the unobserved value itself, for example wealthy people being less likely to report their income.	RCD missing-data nmar
Why is dropout especially problematic?	Because it is usually impossible to tell whether it is MCAR or MAR, since the subject is never observed again.	RCD missing-data dropout
What is ignorable missingness?	Under MCAR or MAR the missing-data mechanism factorizes out of the likelihood, so likelihood-based inference can simply ignore it.	RCD missing-data
Under what missingness mechanism is plain GEE consistent?	Only under MCAR. Under MAR, GEE must be weighted by the inverse probability of being observed.<br><br>\[ w_{ij}=\frac{1}{P\big(R_{ij}=1\big)}\qquad\text{(IPW for MAR)} \]	RCD missing-data gee
What is complete case analysis and what is wrong with it?	Using only subjects with no missing values. It induces bias under both MAR and NMAR.	RCD missing-data
What is last value carried forward?	An imputation replacing missing values with the last observed one, ideally adjusted for the observed time trend.	RCD missing-data imputation
What is propensity-based imputation?	Imputing missing values using subjects with a similar estimated dropout probability.	RCD missing-data imputation
What is predictive mean matching?	Predicting a missing observation using other subjects for whom that same observation is not missing.	RCD missing-data imputation
What is multiple imputation?	Imputing several complete datasets, fitting the model to each, and comparing or pooling the resulting coefficient estimates to reflect the imputation uncertainty.	RCD missing-data imputation
What is a selection model for missingness?	A factorization of the joint density into the response distribution times the missingness probability conditional on the responses.<br><br>\[ f\big(y_i,r_i\mid X_i,\theta,\psi\big)=f\big(y_i\mid X_i,\theta\big)\,f\big(r_i\mid y_i,X_i,\psi\big) \]	RCD missing-data selection-model
What is a pattern mixture model?	A factorization of the joint density into the missingness distribution times the response distribution conditional on the missingness pattern.<br><br>\[ f\big(y_i,r_i\mid X_i,\theta,\psi\big)=f\big(r_i\mid X_i,\psi\big)\,f\big(y_i\mid r_i,X_i,\theta\big) \]	RCD missing-data pattern-mixture
What does the R function gls fit?	A marginal model, optionally with an unstructured correlation matrix. Unlike a mixed model it permits negative correlations.	RCD software r
What is the difference between gls and lme in R?	gls fits a marginal model while lme fits a mixed, that is conditional, model. Negative correlations are possible in the marginal formulation but not through random effects.	RCD software r
How is a random intercept specified in mgcv?	As a smooth term on the subject factor with basis type re. Penalized splines use basis type ps, where effective degrees of freedom of 1 means the effect is linear.	RCD software r
What does the default REML setting mean in lmer?	REML is used by default, which gives less biased variance component estimates but makes fixed-effect likelihood comparisons invalid.	RCD software r
Why does anova refit mixed models with ML?	Because a likelihood ratio test for fixed effects requires ML rather than REML likelihoods to be comparable.	RCD software r
What is the covariance identity used throughout for linear transformations?	The covariance of A times a vector equals A times the covariance times A transposed. If the mean is zero the covariance is just the expectation of the outer product.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(Av)=A\operatorname{Cov}(v)A^{\top},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(v)=E\big(vv^{\top}\big)\ \text{if}\ E(v)=0 \]	RCD linear-algebra
Why is the covariance between a random effect term and the fixed part zero?	Because the fixed part is deterministic given the covariates, so it contributes no covariance.	RCD lmm derivation
What is the marginal correlation in a random intercept model?	The random intercept variance divided by the sum of the random intercept variance and the error variance, identical for every pair of measurements of a subject.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Corr}\big(Y_{ij},Y_{ik}\big)=\frac{d_{11}}{d_{11}+\sigma^2} \]	RCD lmm random-intercept
What are the three steps of ML estimation in a mixed model?	Find the GLS estimator of beta as a function of the variance parameters, maximize the profile log-likelihood to obtain those variance parameters, then plug them back in.	RCD estimation
What are the three steps of REML estimation?	The same beta formula as ML, then estimate the variance parameters from the transformed data in which the global trend has been eliminated, then plug them back in.	RCD estimation reml
