What is an embedding in NLP?	A dense vector representation of a word. Unlike one-hot encoding it is low-dimensional and captures similarity between words.	NLP embeddings
Why is one-hot encoding unsuitable for words?	It is extremely high-dimensional and every pair of distinct words is equally far apart, so no notion of similarity is captured.	NLP embeddings
What is pre-training in NLP?	Training on large amounts of unlabelled text with a self-supervised objective such as masked word prediction or next word prediction.	NLP pretraining
What is fine-tuning?	Adapting a pre-trained model to a specific task using a labelled dataset. It changes the model weights.	NLP fine-tuning
What is transductive transfer learning?	Same task, but labelled data only in the source domain. Sub-cases are domain adaptation (different domain) and cross-lingual learning (different language).	NLP transfer-learning
What is inductive transfer learning?	A different task, with labelled data available in the target domain. Multi-task learning does this simultaneously, sequential transfer learning one after the other.	NLP transfer-learning
What is zero-shot learning?	Using a pre-trained model without any fine-tuning, so the weights remain unchanged.	NLP zero-shot
What is prompting?	Describing the task the model should perform in natural language, with the model weights frozen. No gradient updates occur.	NLP prompting
How is the probability of a sequence factorized in a language model?	As a product of conditional probabilities, each token given all previous tokens, by the chain rule of probability.<br><br>\[ P(w_1,\dots,w_n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}P\big(w_i\mid w_1,\dots,w_{i-1}\big) \]	NLP language-model
What are typical low-level NLP tasks?	Token classification such as part-of-speech tagging and named entity recognition, and modelling relationships between tokens.	NLP tasks
What are typical high-level NLP tasks?	Sequence classification like spam or sentiment detection, retrieval, text generation, writing assistance, information extraction, and machine translation.	NLP tasks
What is the k-th order Markov assumption in a language model?	The next word depends only on the k previous words rather than the entire history.<br><br>\[ P\big(w_i\mid w_1,\dots,w_{i-1}\big)\approx P\big(w_i\mid w_{i-k},\dots,w_{i-1}\big) \]	NLP language-model
What are the three problems of classical n-gram language models?	The parameter count grows exponentially with context size, the data are sparse so even bigrams go unobserved, and the context window must be fixed manually.	NLP language-model
How do neural networks solve the n-gram problems?	They introduce non-linearity and achieve only a linear increase in parameters as context grows, with a softmax output giving the probability distribution.	NLP language-model
What is cosine similarity and why is it used for embeddings?	The inner product of two vectors divided by the product of their norms. It measures the angle rather than the magnitude, so it captures semantic similarity independently of vector length.<br><br>\[ \cos\big(w^{(i)},v^{(j)}\big)=\frac{w^{(i)\top}v^{(j)}}{\|w^{(i)}\|_2\,\|v^{(j)}\|_2} \]	NLP embeddings
Why do unseen words hurt embedding models?	Their vectors remain random, so a large amount of training data is needed for every word to be seen enough times.	NLP embeddings
What is Word2Vec at its core?	A bigram (word-pair) language model that learns two vectors per word: one for the word as a prediction target and one for it as a context word.	NLP word2vec
Why is the plain softmax over the vocabulary impractical in Word2Vec?	It requires a dot product against every word in the vocabulary at each step, which is far too slow.<br><br>\[ P(i\mid j)=\frac{\exp\big(w^{(i)\top}v^{(j)}\big)}{\sum_{k=1}^{|V|}\exp\big(w^{(k)\top}v^{(j)}\big)} \]	NLP word2vec
What is negative sampling?	Instead of a softmax over the whole vocabulary, the model only predicts whether a given word pair is real or fake, using observed pairs as positives and random pairs as negatives.	NLP word2vec negative-sampling
Why does negative sampling still cover the vocabulary?	Because over many training iterations the randomly drawn negative words eventually span the entire vocabulary.	NLP word2vec negative-sampling
What does the negative sampling likelihood maximize?	The probability of positive pairs being classified as 1 and negative pairs as 0. The model learns whether word pairs look realistic.<br><br>\[ L=\prod_{(i,j)\in\text{pos}}\sigma\big(w^{(i)\top}v^{(j)}\big)\prod_{(i,j)\in\text{neg}}\Big(1-\sigma\big(w^{(i)\top}v^{(j)}\big)\Big) \]	NLP word2vec negative-sampling
What is the skip-gram task?	Given a centre word, predict the words inside a context window around it. A window size of 2 means two words before and two after.<br><br>\[ \prod_{\substack{i=-c\\ i\neq 0}}^{c}P\big(w_{t+i}\mid w_t\big) \]	NLP word2vec skip-gram
What is continuous bag of words?	The opposite of skip-gram: given the surrounding context, predict the centre word.	NLP word2vec cbow
What problem does FastText solve and how?	Rare and unseen words. It splits words into character n-grams, so a known word is represented by the average of its word and n-gram embeddings, and an unknown word by the average of its n-grams alone.	NLP fasttext
What is analogy mining with embeddings?	Vector arithmetic on embeddings, for example Berlin minus Germany plus UK giving approximately London.	NLP embeddings
Is it better to fine-tune or freeze embeddings for a task?	It is a design choice with no universal answer; both are legitimate options.	NLP embeddings
What is the basic idea of an RNN for text?	The model reads the text sequentially, updating an internal hidden state at each time step as a function of the current input and the previous state.<br><br>\[ h^{(j)}=\tanh\big(Wx^{(j)}+Uh^{(j-1)}+b\big)\in[-1,1]^{d} \]	NLP rnn
Why do RNNs suffer from vanishing gradients?	The derivative of tanh lies between 0 and 1, so the repeated multiplication across time steps causes exponential decay. Words at the start barely influence the end.<br><br>\[ \frac{\partial h^{(n)}}{\partial h^{(j)}}=\prod_{k=j+1}^{n}\frac{\partial h^{(k)}}{\partial h^{(k-1)}},\qquad |\tanh'|\le 1 \]	NLP rnn vanishing-gradients
What are the two hidden states of an LSTM?	The hidden state h for short-term memory and the cell state c for long-term memory.	NLP lstm
What do the three LSTM gates control?	The forget gate decides what to discard from the cell state, the input gate what from the hidden state gets added to it, and the output gate what from the cell state is exposed as the hidden state.<br><br>\[ c^{(j)}=f\odot c^{(j-1)}+i\odot\tilde c^{(j)},\qquad h^{(j)}=o\odot\tanh\big(c^{(j)}\big) \]	NLP lstm
Why do LSTMs avoid vanishing gradients?	Because the gradient path through the cell state is additive rather than purely multiplicative. If the forget gate stays near 1, the product of forget gates stays near 1.	NLP lstm
What does an LSTM effectively learn?	When to forget, when to memorize, and when to output from memory.	NLP lstm
What is a multi-layer RNN?	Several RNNs stacked so that the hidden states of layer i-1 serve as the input to layer i, with the raw sequence as input to the first layer.	NLP rnn
What is a bidirectional RNN? (NLP)	Two RNNs with separate parameters, one reading the sequence forward and one backward, with no connection between their hidden states.	NLP rnn bidirectional
What is the fundamental limitation of RNN encoder-decoder models?	The whole input sequence has to be compressed into a single fixed vector, which fails for long inputs.	NLP rnn limitations
What is Bahdanau attention?	An attention mechanism giving the decoder access to a weighted sum of all encoder hidden states rather than only the last one.	NLP attention
What are the three components of an attention mechanism?	A query vector, a set of key vectors, and a set of value vectors, together with a scoring function mapping a query-key pair to a scalar.	NLP attention
What are the three steps of computing attention?	Score the query against every key, softmax the scores into a probability distribution, then form the weighted sum of the value vectors.<br><br>\[ e_j=a(q,k_j),\qquad \alpha_j=\frac{\exp(e_j)}{\sum_i\exp(e_i)},\qquad o=\sum_j\alpha_j\,v_j \]	NLP attention
What do queries, keys and values represent intuitively?	The query asks what is relevant, the key measures how relevant each token is to that question, and the value carries the actual content or meaning of the token.	NLP attention
Why is Bahdanau attention not parallelizable?	Because the query at step i+1 depends on the output of step i, so the steps must run sequentially.	NLP attention
What is tokenization?	Breaking text into smaller units with unique IDs. Units can be sentences, words, subwords, or characters.	NLP tokenization
What is the trade-off in choosing token granularity?	Character-level tokens give a short vocabulary but little information per token; longer tokens carry more meaning but require a much larger vocabulary. It is a trade-off between accuracy and complexity.	NLP tokenization
What is byte pair encoding?	A data compression method applied to tokenization: repeatedly find the most frequent token pair and replace it with a new unused token.	NLP tokenization bpe
How does a fixed vocabulary size work with BPE?	Frequent words become individual tokens while less frequent words are split into subword tokens.	NLP tokenization bpe
How does WordPiece differ from byte pair encoding?	It merges not the most frequent pair, but the pair that increases the likelihood of the training data the most.	NLP tokenization wordpiece
What are the requirements for machine translation that motivated the Transformer?	It is a sequence-to-sequence task, the decoder needs access to the whole input via cross-attention, and the encoder needs good bidirectional contextualization, which stacked RNNs cannot provide.	NLP transformer
What is cross-attention?	Attention where the queries come from one sequence and the keys and values from another, letting a decoder attend to the encoder output.	NLP transformer cross-attention
What is self-attention?	Attention where queries, keys and values are all derived from the same sequence.	NLP transformer self-attention
What are the four steps of scaled dot-product attention?	Project the input into queries, keys and values; compute scaled dot products of queries with keys; softmax them into weights; then form the weighted sum of the values.<br><br>\[ E=\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}},\qquad A=\mathrm{softmax}(E),\qquad O=AV \]	NLP transformer self-attention
Why is the dot product divided by the square root of the key dimension?	To keep the score variance stable as dimension grows, preventing the softmax from saturating and killing the gradient.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}\big(q^{\top}k\big)\propto d_k\ \Rightarrow\ \text{scale by }\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \]	NLP transformer self-attention
Why does the Transformer need positional embeddings?	Attention is permutation invariant, so word order would be lost in the very first attention block. Position information must be added explicitly.	NLP transformer positional-encoding
What are the types of positional embeddings?	Deterministic ones built from sinusoidal functions, trainable ones stored in a position embedding matrix, and relative ones encoding positions with respect to each other.	NLP transformer positional-encoding
How is attention parallelized?	The query-key products become one matrix multiplication, the softmax normalization is applied along the key axis, and the weighted sum of values is another matrix multiplication.	NLP transformer efficiency
What is the drawback of parallelized attention?	Very high memory requirements, because the full attention matrix of size sequence length squared must be materialized.	NLP transformer efficiency
What is multi-head attention? (NLP)	Several attention blocks running in parallel, typically with each head dimension set to the model dimension divided by the number of heads, so different heads capture different kinds of relations.<br><br>\[ d_k=d_v=\frac{d_{\text{model}}}{n_{\text{heads}}},\qquad \mathrm{MHA}=\mathrm{Concat}(\text{heads})\,W^{O} \]	NLP transformer multi-head
What is masked attention in the decoder?	Setting the attention scores for future positions to negative infinity so their weights become zero, which prevents the model from cheating by looking ahead.<br><br>\[ e_{j,j'}=-\infty\quad\text{for}\quad j'\gt j\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \alpha_{j,j'}=0 \]	NLP transformer masking
Why is masking only needed at training time?	At inference the future tokens do not exist yet, so there is nothing to mask. Inference is also not parallelizable for the same reason.	NLP transformer masking
How are the encoder and decoder connected in a Transformer?	The masked self-attention output of the decoder provides the queries, while the encoder output provides the keys and values for a cross-attention block.	NLP transformer architecture
What are the components of a Transformer encoder block?	Multi-head attention, layer normalization, a feed-forward network, and another layer normalization.	NLP transformer architecture
What are the components of a Transformer decoder block?	Masked multi-head attention, layer norm, cross multi-head attention, layer norm, feed-forward network, and a final layer norm.	NLP transformer architecture
What does the feed-forward network in a Transformer block look like?	Two fully connected layers with input and output size equal to the model dimension and a hidden layer typically four times as large, with ReLU in between.	NLP transformer architecture
What does layer normalization compute?	The mean and standard deviation across the layer, with two learnable parameters per neuron.<br><br>\[ N_{\mathrm{LN}}=2\,d_{\text{model}}\qquad(\gamma,\beta\ \text{per neuron}) \]	NLP transformer layernorm
Roughly how many parameters does an attention block have?	About four times the squared model dimension, when the per-head dimension is the model dimension divided by the number of heads.<br><br>\[ N_{\text{attn}}=3\big(d_{\text{model}}d_{qkv}+d_{qkv}\big)n_{\text{heads}}+d_{\text{model}}^2+d_{\text{model}}\ \approx\ 4\,d_{\text{model}}^2 \]	NLP transformer parameters
Roughly how many parameters does the feed-forward network have?	About eight times the squared model dimension, when the hidden size is four times the model dimension.<br><br>\[ N_{\mathrm{FFN}}=2\,d_{\text{model}}\,d_{\mathrm{FFN}}\ \approx\ 8\,d_{\text{model}}^2\quad(d_{\mathrm{FFN}}=4d_{\text{model}}) \]	NLP transformer parameters
How many embedding parameters does a Transformer have?	Vocabulary size times model dimension.<br><br>\[ N_{\text{emb}}=|V|\cdot d_{\text{model}} \]	NLP transformer parameters
What is the computational complexity of a Transformer versus an RNN?	The Transformer is order n squared times d, while the RNN is order n times d squared, where n is sequence length and d the embedding dimension.<br><br>\[ \text{Transformer: }\mathcal{O}\big(n^2d\big)\qquad\text{RNN: }\mathcal{O}\big(nd^2\big) \]	NLP transformer complexity
What is an autoregressive language model?	A model predicting the next word, so the sentence probability factorizes by the chain rule. It is used in decoders and is better for text generation.	NLP language-model arlm
What is a masked language model?	A model predicting masked words in the middle of a sentence. It gives no estimate of the sentence probability but produces better contextualized representations because it sees the whole sentence.	NLP language-model mlm
What is self-supervised learning?	Learning without external labels, where the labels are generated from the data itself, for example predicting the next word from context.	NLP self-supervised
What is perplexity?	A measure of a probabilistic model's uncertainty, computed as the exponentiated negative average log-probability. It quantifies the model's surprisal.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{PPL}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\log p_\theta\big(w_i\mid w_{\lt i}\big)\right) \]	NLP evaluation perplexity
What is the range of perplexity?	Its upper bound is the vocabulary size. A perplexity of 1 is not actually desirable since it would mean no variety at all.<br><br>\[ 1\ \le\ \mathrm{PPL}\ \le\ |V| \]	NLP evaluation perplexity
What are the drawbacks of perplexity?	It depends on a fixed context size, and it is not clear what value should be considered good.	NLP evaluation perplexity
What is mean average precision?	The mean over all queries of the average precision at k, where each item's precision is weighted by its relevance.	NLP evaluation ranking
What is mean reciprocal rank?	The mean over queries of the reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant item.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{MRR}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{q}\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}_q} \]	NLP evaluation ranking
What is the BLEU score?	A translation metric measuring how many n-grams of the candidate appear in the reference, combined geometrically with weights, plus a brevity penalty for short outputs.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{BLEU}=\mathrm{BP}\cdot\exp\!\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_n\log p_n\Big),\qquad p_n=\frac{\#\text{correct }n\text{-grams}}{\#\text{all }n\text{-grams}} \]	NLP evaluation bleu
What is the criticism of BLEU?	It requires exact overlap, so synonyms and paraphrases are not credited. It measures only word overlap.	NLP evaluation bleu
What are BERTScore and BLEURT?	Metrics based on a pre-trained BERT model that can capture contextual similarity rather than just literal word overlap.	NLP evaluation
What are the ROUGE variants?	ROUGE-N for n-gram overlap, ROUGE-L for the longest common subsequence, and ROUGE-W for weighted n-gram overlap. They are mainly used for summarization.	NLP evaluation rouge
What is the diversity metric for generated text?	A product over n-gram orders of one minus the repetition rate, equivalently the fraction of unique n-grams among all n-grams.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{DIV}=\prod_{n=2}^{4}\Big(1-\tfrac{\mathrm{rep}\text{-}n}{100}\Big)=\prod_{n=2}^{4}\frac{\#\text{unique }n\text{-grams}}{\#\text{all }n\text{-grams}} \]	NLP evaluation diversity
What is the coherence metric?	The cosine similarity between the embedding of the prompt and the embedding of the generated text, measuring how well the output fits the prompt.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{COH}\big(x_{\text{prompt}},x_{\text{gen}}\big)=\frac{\mathrm{Emb}(x_{\text{prompt}})^{\top}\mathrm{Emb}(x_{\text{gen}})}{\|\mathrm{Emb}(x_{\text{prompt}})\|\cdot\|\mathrm{Emb}(x_{\text{gen}})\|} \]	NLP evaluation coherence
What is the MAUVE metric?	A metric comparing the language model's output distribution to the human generation distribution, capturing both overestimation of unlikely words and underestimation of plausible ones. It lies between 0 and 1 with higher being better.	NLP evaluation mauve
What are the pros and cons of human evaluation?	It is the gold standard and works where simple metrics fail, but it is manual, costly and subjective.	NLP evaluation
What is BERT?	Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers: an encoder-only stack of bidirectional Transformer blocks trained with a masked language modelling objective, parallelizable thanks to self-attention.	NLP bert
What are the special tokens in BERT?	The CLS token representing the whole sequence for classification or clustering, the SEP token separating two sequences, and the UNK token for unknown words.	NLP bert
What is the masked language modelling objective in BERT?	Randomly mask about 15 percent of tokens and predict them, which couples self-attention with bidirectionality without violating causality.	NLP bert mlm
What is the 80-10-10 rule in BERT masking?	Of the selected tokens, 80 percent are replaced by MASK, 10 percent by a random token, and 10 percent left unchanged. The latter two increase overlap with fine-tuning data where MASK never appears.	NLP bert mlm
What is next sentence prediction?	A BERT pre-training objective: given two sentences, predict whether the second actually follows the first.	NLP bert nsp
What can BERT be fine-tuned for and what not?	It can do sentence classification, sentence tagging and extractive question answering. It cannot do next token prediction.	NLP bert fine-tuning
Why does BERT have a maximum of 512 tokens?	Because Transformer complexity scales quadratically with sequence length, so longer inputs become computationally prohibitive.	NLP bert limitations
What is a subtle problem caused by BERT's bidirectionality?	A word can effectively see itself through earlier layers, which leaks information about the token being predicted.	NLP bert limitations
Why can BERT not compute joint probabilities?	Because MASK tokens are predicted independently of each other, which imposes an unwanted independence assumption. XLNet removes this by conditioning on previously predicted tokens.	NLP bert limitations
How is bias in BERT tested?	With intra-sentence tests offering bias, neutral and meaningless options for masked token prediction, and inter-sentence tests offering the same three options for next sentence prediction.	NLP bert bias
What did BERTology find about attention heads?	Different heads attend to different token types, for example noun modifiers attending adjectives and articles to their nouns. Some heads are redundant and removing them can even improve accuracy.	NLP bert bertology
What is RoBERTa?	A BERT variant with tuned hyperparameters, no next sentence prediction, a much larger corpus, dynamic masking so the same sentence is masked differently across epochs, and a larger vocabulary.	NLP bert roberta
What is dynamic masking?	Masking the same sentence differently each time it is seen, rather than fixing the mask once. It effectively multiplies the training signal.	NLP bert roberta
What is ALBERT?	A BERT variant that decouples the token embedding dimension from the hidden layer dimension, shares parameters across layers to reduce model size, replaces NSP with sentence order prediction, and masks up to three consecutive tokens.	NLP bert albert
What is sentence order prediction?	An objective where the model is given two sentences and must predict whether their order is correct. It is a harder and more useful task than next sentence prediction.	NLP bert albert
What is knowledge distillation?	Training a fast compact student model to approximate the function of a bigger teacher model, which generalizes better than training a small model from scratch.	NLP distillation
What is the difference between hard and soft targets in distillation?	Hard targets mark one word as correct and everything else wrong; soft targets are a temperature-scaled softmax over the whole vocabulary, carrying much finer-grained information about the teacher's behaviour.<br><br>\[ q_i=\frac{\exp\big(z_i/T\big)}{\sum_j\exp\big(z_j/T\big)} \]	NLP distillation
What does the temperature parameter do in distillation?	It smooths or sharpens the output distribution. A higher temperature makes the soft targets flatter and transfers more information about the teacher's relative preferences.	NLP distillation
What are the key facts about DistilBERT?	Half the layers and roughly half the parameters of BERT with about 97 percent of its performance, initialized from BERT's own weights rather than randomly, and trained with losses on soft targets, hard targets and embedding deviation.	NLP distillation distilbert
What are quantization and pruning?	Quantization reduces the numeric precision of the weights; pruning removes model parts with low importance scores such as neurons with little influence on the loss.	NLP compression
What is the core limitation of BERT-style models that motivated text-to-text?	They are trained for one task at a time, need a lot of labelled fine-tuning data, cannot learn from a task description, and can only classify rather than generate.	NLP t5
What is the text-to-text idea?	Include a task description in the training data so the model learns to associate task descriptions with class names, allowing a single model to handle many tasks.	NLP t5
What is T5?	The Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer: an encoder-decoder model with relative positional embeddings and WordPiece tokens, where all tasks are reformulated as text-to-text with a fixed task-description prefix.	NLP t5
How does T5's pre-training objective differ from BERT's?	It masks whole spans of tokens rather than single ones, with span length and the percentage of corrupted tokens as hyperparameters.	NLP t5
Which architecture worked best in the T5 experiments?	Encoder-decoder, tested against encoder-only and decoder-only variants.	NLP t5
What are the three attention patterns by architecture?	The encoder-decoder is fully visible, a plain language model is causal so it cannot see the future, and a prefix LM is fully visible over the prefix and causal afterwards.	NLP transformer architecture
What is GPT?	The Generative Pre-Trained Transformer: a 12-layer transformer decoder with masked self-attention, residual connections and learned positional embeddings, pre-trained on causal next-word prediction.	NLP gpt
Why does GPT have no cross-attention?	Because it is decoder-only with no encoder to cross-attend to.	NLP gpt
What is GPT's fine-tuning objective?	Supervised label prediction, taking the embedding of the last token through a linear classification layer and a softmax.<br><br>\[ P\big(y\mid x_1,\dots,x_m\big)=\mathrm{softmax}\big(h^{m}W_y\big) \]	NLP gpt
What was the breakthrough of GPT-2?	It was the first model to produce coherent text, and having been trained on a web corpus it could understand task descriptions and solve tasks it was not explicitly trained on, that is, zero-shot.	NLP gpt
What are the three key differences between GPT and BERT?	GPTs are autoregressive and trained to predict the next word, GPTs are decoders while BERT is an encoder unsuited to generation, and GPT is a true multi-task model via task descriptions.	NLP gpt bert
What is in-context learning?	The model develops a broad set of skills during pre-training and then uses them at inference to adapt quickly to a task, with no gradient updates at all.	NLP prompting in-context-learning
When is in-context learning effective?	Only with a high parameter count. It is an emergent capability of large models.	NLP prompting in-context-learning
What is greedy decoding?	At each step select the single most probable next word, until the end-of-sequence token becomes most probable.<br><br>\[ y_t=\arg\max_{y\in V}\ P\big(y\mid y_1,\dots,y_{t-1},x\big) \]	NLP decoding greedy
What are the drawbacks of greedy decoding?	It may miss the overall highest-probability sequence, produces repetitive and predictable degenerate text, can lose track of context over long sequences, and favours common words giving generic output.	NLP decoding greedy
What is beam search?	Maintain a set of k candidate sequences, extending each with its top k tokens at every step and keeping the k most likely sequences overall by cumulative probability.	NLP decoding beam-search
What are the drawbacks of beam search?	It is still deterministic so it can get stuck in loops and remains repetitive, and humans do sometimes use unlikely words.	NLP decoding beam-search
What is temperature sampling?	Applying a softmax with a temperature to the conditional probability distribution before sampling. High temperature approaches uniform random selection while temperature toward zero approaches greedy search.<br><br>\[ \sigma(z_i)=\frac{\exp\big(z_i/T\big)}{\sum_{j}\exp\big(z_j/T\big)} \]	NLP decoding sampling
What is top-k sampling?	Keep only the k most likely tokens, redistribute the probability mass among them, and sample. With k equal to 1 it reduces to greedy search.	NLP decoding sampling
What is the drawback of top-k sampling?	A fixed k can be counterproductive, since the appropriate number of plausible continuations varies by context.	NLP decoding sampling
What is nucleus (top-p) sampling?	Sample from the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds p, so the candidate set adapts to the shape of the distribution.<br><br>\[ V^{(p)}=\text{smallest set with}\ \sum_{y\in V^{(p)}}P(y)\ \ge\ p \]	NLP decoding sampling
What is contrastive search?	Decoding that combines model confidence with a degeneration penalty, defined as the maximum cosine similarity between the current token and all context tokens. Without the penalty it reduces to greedy search.	NLP decoding contrastive-search
What is old-style fine-tuning and what are its drawbacks?	Fine-tuning on a task-specific dataset, outputting either an arbitrary category or a meaningful verbalizer. Only one task benefits and a lot of training data is needed.	NLP fine-tuning
What is a verbalizer?	A meaningful text label such as positive or negative used as the model's output instead of an arbitrary class index. It can differ by task pattern.	NLP fine-tuning
What is continued pre-training?	Adapting a generic pre-trained model to a new domain, for example company-specific data, typically with around a thousand examples.	NLP fine-tuning
What is multi-task prompted training?	Learning many tasks in a unified prompt format so the model performs well on unseen tasks, evaluated on held-out tasks rather than merely held-out examples.	NLP fine-tuning multi-task
What is FLAN and what is instruction tuning?	FLAN is a model tuned on around 1.8 thousand tasks and tested on unseen ones. Instruction tuning replaces the pre-train-then-fine-tune and pure prompting paradigms and outperformed GPT-3.	NLP instruction-tuning
What is chain-of-thought prompting?	Few-shot prompting where each training example has the form input, chain of thought, output, so the model reasons before producing an answer.	NLP reasoning chain-of-thought
What are the benefits of chain of thought?	It decomposes multi-step problems, increases interpretability by exposing the reasoning so errors can be located, and is closer to how humans solve tasks.	NLP reasoning chain-of-thought
What is tree of thought?	A method addressing the limited exploration of autoregressive models by maintaining multiple alternative reasoning paths, discarding unproductive ones and making global decisions.	NLP reasoning tree-of-thought
What was the motivation behind DeepSeek-style reasoning models?	LLMs should become native reasoners rather than needing prompting tricks. High data requirements were met with synthetic data generation, with the goal of discovering new reasoning patterns.	NLP reasoning
What behaviours do reinforcement-learned reasoning models acquire?	Dynamic strategy adaptation, self-verification, reflection, and backtracking to try a different approach.	NLP reasoning
Why do reasoning models need a large context?	Because the whole reasoning trace has to fit in context for the model to work through and revisit its own steps.	NLP reasoning
What are emergent abilities?	Capabilities that appear only in larger models, arriving very sharply: at some model size the ability suddenly starts working.	NLP scaling emergent-abilities
What is the motivation for instruction tuning and alignment?	The LLM should align with human values even where the pre-training data does not, should not share harmful knowledge it possesses, should hallucinate less, and needs a dialog format that pre-training data barely contains.	NLP alignment
What are the three H's of alignment?	Helpful, harmless, and honest.	NLP alignment
What is RLHF in one sentence?	Have the model produce several options, let a human pick the better one, and use that feedback to build a reward predictor that infers what humans want.	NLP rlhf
Why is RLHF easier than supervised fine-tuning for dialog?	Writing high-quality answers from scratch is hard, whereas simply choosing the better of two replies is easy.	NLP rlhf
What are the three steps of the InstructGPT pipeline?	Collect labelled data for supervised fine-tuning, collect comparison data to train a reward model, then use the reward model to optimize the policy with PPO.	NLP rlhf ppo
What trade-off does PPO optimization balance?	Staying close to the supervised fine-tuned model versus maximizing the reward, so the next-word-prediction ability from pre-training is not lost.	NLP rlhf ppo
How does PPO compare with supervised fine-tuning?	PPO explores rather than merely imitating humans and has far more training data, but it hallucinates more because the reward model is imperfect.	NLP rlhf ppo
What are the limitations of InstructGPT?	It cannot make assumptions, does not follow constraints perfectly, can still produce harmful answers with the right prompt, and hallucinates because the pre-training data contains false facts.	NLP alignment limitations
What is hallucination?	False or misleading statements presented as facts by the model.	NLP alignment hallucination
What do scaling laws say about LLM performance?	Performance depends strongly on scale (parameters, data and compute measured in FLOPs) and only weakly on model shape, following a power law.<br><br>\[ L(N,D)=1.61+\frac{406.4}{N^{0.34}}+\frac{410.7}{D^{0.28}} \]	NLP scaling scaling-laws
What is the practical conclusion from modern scaling law analysis?	Current models should be smaller and trained on more or higher-quality data. This also reduces inference cost.	NLP scaling scaling-laws
How does loss scale with parameters and data?	Roughly with the cube root of the parameter count and the fourth root of the data size in the denominator, added to an irreducible constant.<br><br>\[ L\ \sim\ \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{N}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{D}} \]	NLP scaling scaling-laws
What are fp32, fp16 and int8?	Single precision floating point using 32 bits, half precision using 16 bits, and 8-bit integers covering minus 128 to 127. Lower precision reduces memory at the cost of numeric range.	NLP efficiency precision
What is the structure of a floating point number?	One sign bit, a number of exponent bits, and the remaining significand bits, combined as the sign times two to the biased exponent times the mantissa.<br><br>\[ (-1)^{s}\cdot 2^{\,e-\text{bias}}\cdot 1.m \]	NLP efficiency precision
What are gradient accumulation steps?	The number of forward-backward steps taken before a parameter update. More steps allow a smaller per-device batch size and hence less memory.	NLP training efficiency
What is gradient checkpointing?	Not storing the forward-pass activations but recomputing them during backpropagation, trading compute for memory.	NLP training efficiency
What are the UNK and PAD tokens for?	UNK is the default for unknown words, and PAD is used to pad sequences so all sequences within a batch have the same length.	NLP tokenization
What is a source mask versus a target mask?	The source mask ignores padding tokens in the encoder, while the target mask hides both padding and all future tokens in the decoder.	NLP transformer masking
What do zero_grad, backward and step do in a training loop?	zero_grad resets the gradients so they do not accumulate across batches, backward computes the loss derivative with respect to every parameter, and step updates the parameters along the gradient.	NLP training pytorch
Why must gradients be zeroed each step?	Because otherwise they accumulate across batches, so each update would be based on a mixture of the current and all previous batches.	NLP training pytorch
What is the intuition of query, key and value in self-attention, per token?	Each token asks a question through its query, each token offers an answer through its key indicating relevance to that question, and each token provides context information through its value.	NLP transformer self-attention
What does an attention score e between token j and j-prime mean?	How relevant token j-prime is to answering the question posed by token j.	NLP transformer self-attention
Along which axis is the attention softmax taken and why?	Row-wise, since rows correspond to queries. Each row becomes a probability distribution over the keys for that one query.	NLP transformer self-attention
What is each output vector of self-attention?	A weighted average of the value vectors of all tokens, weighted by their relevance to the query token for the relation modelled by that attention head.<br><br>\[ o_j=\sum_{i=1}^{J_x}\alpha_{j,i}\,v_i \]	NLP transformer self-attention
