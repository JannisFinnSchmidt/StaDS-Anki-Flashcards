What are the four assumptions of the classical linear model?	Errors have zero mean, constant variance, are mutually independent, and (for exact inference) are normally distributed.	StatMod linear-model assumptions
What does the OLS estimator minimize?	The sum of squared vertical deviations between observed responses and fitted values.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta=\big(X^{\top}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}y=\arg\min_\beta\|y-X\beta\|_2^2 \]	StatMod linear-model ols
What is the sampling distribution of the OLS estimator under normality?	Multivariate normal, centered at the true coefficient vector with covariance sigma squared times the inverse cross-product matrix.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta\ \sim\ N\!\Big(\beta,\ \sigma^2\big(X^{\top}X\big)^{-1}\Big) \]	StatMod linear-model inference
What is the Wald test in a linear model?	A test of a general linear hypothesis based on the quadratic form of the estimated deviation from the hypothesis, standardized by its covariance matrix.<br><br>\[ W=\big(A\hat\beta-c\big)^{\top}\big[A\,\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\hat\beta)\,A^{\top}\big]^{-1}\big(A\hat\beta-c\big) \]	StatMod linear-model testing wald
What is the sum of squares describing deviation from a hypothesis (SSH)?	The quadratic form measuring how far the estimated coefficients are from satisfying the hypothesis, in the metric of their covariance.	StatMod linear-model testing
What is the test statistic of the Wald F test?	The mean square of the hypothesis divided by the mean squared error. Under the null it follows an F distribution.<br><br>\[ F=\frac{SSH/\operatorname{rank}(A)}{SSE/(n-p)}\ \sim\ F_{\operatorname{rank}(A),\,n-p} \]	StatMod linear-model testing wald
What is the likelihood ratio test principle?	Prefer the alternative when the ratio of the maximized likelihood under the alternative to that under the null exceeds a critical value.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{lq}=2\big(\log L(\hat\theta)-\log L(\tilde\theta)\big)\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ \chi^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)} \]	StatMod testing likelihood-ratio
What is the sum of squares decomposition SST = SSE + SSM?	Total variation of the responses around their mean splits into the unexplained error part plus the part explained by the model.<br><br>\[ \sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2=\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2+\sum_i(\hat y_i-\bar y)^2 \]	StatMod linear-model r-squared
How are the three sums of squares interpreted?	SST is the dispersion of the observations around the mean, SSE is the error between observed and predicted, and SSM measures how well the fitted line captures the data.	StatMod linear-model r-squared
What does R squared measure?	The proportion of the variance in the data that the model is able to explain, computed as SSM divided by SST.<br><br>\[ R^2=\frac{SSM}{SST}=1-\frac{SSE}{SST} \]	StatMod linear-model r-squared
How are interactions specified for different variable types?	For discrete variables use all products of the dummy variables, for continuous variables the product of the variables, and for a mix all products of the two.	StatMod linear-model interactions
What does an interaction term mean substantively?	The effect of one variable depends on the level of another. Different groups get different slopes rather than merely different intercepts.	StatMod linear-model interactions
What is the fundamental structural difference between a GLM and a linear model?	A GLM has no additive residual term; instead it makes a distributional assumption about the response itself, and links the conditional mean to the linear predictor.	StatMod glm
What is the exponential family form used in GLM theory?	A density written as the exponential of the response times the natural parameter minus a normalizing function, divided by a dispersion parameter, plus a data-only term.<br><br>\[ f(y\mid\theta,\phi)=\exp\!\left\{\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{\phi}+c(y,\phi)\right\} \]	StatMod glm exponential-family
What do the derivatives of the normalizing function b give?	The first derivative gives the mean of the response and the second, multiplied by the dispersion, gives its variance.<br><br>\[ E(Y)=b'(\theta),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y)=\phi\,b''(\theta) \]	StatMod glm exponential-family
What is the canonical link function?	The link that sets the natural parameter equal to the linear predictor. It simplifies the score equations considerably.<br><br>\[ g(\mu)=\theta=\eta=x^{\top}\beta \]	StatMod glm link-function
What is the score function of a GLM in matrix form?	The design matrix transposed, times a weight matrix of link derivatives, times the inverse variance matrix, times the residual vector of observed minus fitted means.<br><br>\[ S(\beta)=X^{\top}D\,V^{-1}\big(y-\mu\big) \]	StatMod glm estimation
How does the GLM score function simplify under the canonical link?	It reduces to the design matrix transposed times the raw residuals divided by the dispersion, exactly like the normal equations.	StatMod glm estimation
What is the expected Fisher information in a GLM?	A weighted cross-product of the design matrix, where the weight for each observation is the squared link derivative divided by its variance.<br><br>\[ F(\beta)=X^{\top}WX,\qquad w_i=\frac{h'(\eta_i)^2}{\phi\,v(\mu_i)} \]	StatMod glm estimation
What is the asymptotic distribution of the GLM maximum likelihood estimator?	Approximately normal around the true coefficients with covariance the inverse Fisher information evaluated at the estimate.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ N\!\big(\beta,\ F(\hat\beta)^{-1}\big) \]	StatMod glm inference
What is the GLM likelihood ratio test statistic?	Twice the difference between the maximized log-likelihoods of the unrestricted and restricted models, asymptotically chi-squared with degrees of freedom the rank of the hypothesis matrix.	StatMod glm testing
What is the deviance of a GLM? (Statistical Modelling)	Twice the difference between the log-likelihood of the saturated model and that of the fitted model, scaled by the dispersion. It generalizes the residual sum of squares.<br><br>\[ D=2\phi\big(\ell_{\text{sat}}-\ell(\hat\beta)\big) \]	StatMod glm deviance
What is the saturated model in the deviance definition?	The model that pretends the estimated mean is exactly the observed data, giving the maximum attainable likelihood.	StatMod glm deviance
What is quasi-likelihood?	An approach specifying only the mean and the variance function of the response without assuming a full distribution. There is no genuine likelihood or score function.	StatMod glm quasi-likelihood
Why is quasi-likelihood useful for overdispersion?	It lets you multiply the variance by an extra dispersion factor, for example modelling a Poisson-type mean with a variance larger than the mean.	StatMod glm overdispersion
What is the logistic regression model?	A GLM for a binary response where the probability is the logistic transform of the linear predictor, so the log-odds are modelled linearly.<br><br>\[ \pi(x)=\frac{\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big)}{1+\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big)},\qquad \mathrm{logit}(\pi)=x^{\top}\beta \]	StatMod glm logistic
How is a logistic regression coefficient interpreted?	Its exponential is an odds ratio. A positive coefficient means the odds increase with the covariate, a negative one that they decrease.<br><br>\[ \frac{\pi}{1-\pi}=\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big),\qquad \mathrm{OR}=e^{\beta_j} \]	StatMod glm logistic interpretation
What is the multiplicative structure of the odds in logistic regression?	The odds factorize into a product of exponentials of each covariate contribution, so covariates act multiplicatively on the odds.	StatMod glm logistic
What is the log-linear Poisson model?	A GLM for count data where the log of the expected count is the linear predictor, so the mean is a product of exponential factors.<br><br>\[ \log\mu=x^{\top}\beta,\qquad \mu=e^{\beta_0}e^{\beta_1x_1}\cdots e^{\beta_px_p} \]	StatMod glm poisson
How is a Poisson regression coefficient interpreted?	The expected log count increases by the coefficient, equivalently the expected count is multiplied by its exponential.	StatMod glm poisson interpretation
What is an offset in a Poisson model?	The log of a known exposure quantity entered with coefficient fixed at 1, so the model describes a rate per unit of exposure rather than a raw count.<br><br>\[ \log\mu_i=\log t_i+x_i^{\top}\beta \]	StatMod glm offset
What is the negative binomial distribution used for?	Count data that is more variable than the Poisson allows. Its variance exceeds the mean by an additional quadratic term, giving extra flexibility.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mu+\frac{\mu^2}{\nu}\ \gt\ \mu \]	StatMod glm overdispersion
What is beta regression and when is it used?	Regression for responses restricted to the open unit interval, such as rates or proportions. The beta distribution is reparameterized into a mean and a precision parameter.	StatMod glm beta-regression
What is the natural link for each of the main GLM families?	Identity for Gaussian, log for Poisson, logit for binomial, inverse for gamma, and inverse squared for inverse Gaussian.	StatMod glm link-function
What does a gamma GLM coefficient mean under the natural link?	The inverse of the expected value increases by the coefficient for a one-unit covariate increase.	StatMod glm interpretation
What is the probit link?	The inverse standard normal CDF used as a link for binary responses. It is an alternative to the logit.	StatMod glm link-function
Why do all GLM interpretations end with keeping all other variables constant?	Because a coefficient describes the partial effect of one covariate. Without holding the others fixed the effect is not identified.	StatMod glm interpretation
What problem do linear mixed models solve?	Grouped or longitudinal data where observations within a group are not independent. Removing individual intercepts and slopes restores independence.	StatMod lmm
What is a random intercept model?	A model giving each group its own intercept deviation, treated as a random variable with mean zero rather than as a fixed parameter.<br><br>\[ y_{ij}=x_{ij}^{\top}\beta+b_i+\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad b_i\sim N(0,\tau^2) \]	StatMod lmm random-intercept
What is the difference between the conditional and the marginal view of a mixed model?	The conditional view treats the random effects as given and describes individual groups; the marginal view integrates them out and describes the population average with an induced correlation structure.	StatMod lmm conditional-marginal
What is the marginal covariance structure induced by a random intercept?	Observations in the same group have covariance equal to the random intercept variance, while observations in different groups are uncorrelated.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(y_{ij},y_{ik})=\tau^2\ (j\neq k),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(y_{ij})=\tau^2+\sigma^2 \]	StatMod lmm covariance
What is the general linear mixed model?	A model with fixed effects through a design matrix X and random effects through a design matrix Z, where the random effects and errors are independent normals.<br><br>\[ y_i=X_i\beta+Z_ib_i+\varepsilon_i,\qquad b_i\sim N_q(0,G),\ \varepsilon_i\sim N(0,R_i) \]	StatMod lmm
What is the marginal distribution of the response in a linear mixed model?	Normal with mean the fixed part and covariance Z G Z-transpose plus the error covariance. The random effects contribute an extra covariance term.<br><br>\[ y_i\ \sim\ N\!\big(X_i\beta,\ Z_iGZ_i^{\top}+R_i\big) \]	StatMod lmm
What do the matrices G and R denote in a mixed model?	G is the covariance matrix of the random effects and R is the covariance matrix of the error terms.	StatMod lmm
Why can random effects not be estimated as parameters?	Because they are random variables, not fixed quantities. Only their variance components can be estimated.	StatMod lmm estimation
What is the generalized least squares estimator of the fixed effects in a mixed model?	The usual formula with the marginal covariance matrix V inserted as a weighting matrix in both the cross-product and the right-hand side.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta=\big(X^{\top}V^{-1}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}V^{-1}y \]	StatMod lmm estimation
What is the profile log-likelihood in mixed model estimation?	The likelihood with the fixed effects replaced by their conditional maximizer, leaving a function of the variance parameters alone.	StatMod lmm estimation
Why is REML used instead of plain maximum likelihood?	Because ML variance component estimates are biased downward: they ignore the degrees of freedom used to estimate the fixed effects. REML corrects for this.	StatMod lmm reml
Why can models fitted by REML not be compared across different fixed effect structures?	Because REML depends on the design matrix, which differs between models, so the likelihood values are not comparable.	StatMod lmm reml
What is shrinkage in a mixed model?	Random effect predictions are pulled toward zero, more so for groups with little data. Small groups borrow strength from the overall population.	StatMod lmm shrinkage
What is a generalized linear mixed model?	A model where the conditional response given the random effects is from an exponential family and the conditional mean is linked to a predictor containing both fixed and random effects.	StatMod glmm
Why do GLMM coefficients only have a conditional interpretation?	Because with a non-identity link, averaging over the random effects does not commute with the inverse link. The marginal mean is not the inverse link of the fixed part.	StatMod glmm interpretation
What is the difference between the conditional and marginal odds ratio in a GLMM?	The conditional odds ratio equals the exponential of the coefficient for a given random effect; the marginal odds ratio, obtained by averaging over random effects, does not.	StatMod glmm interpretation
Why is GLMM estimation computationally hard?	The likelihood requires integrating the random effects out, and that integral has no closed form for non-normal responses.	StatMod glmm estimation
What are GEE models?	Generalized estimating equations: a marginal approach analogous to quasi-likelihood that specifies only the mean and the working correlation structure, not the full distribution.	StatMod gee
What is the advantage of GEE over a GLMM?	It gives population-averaged (marginal) coefficient interpretations and allows flexible dependence structures without a full distributional assumption.	StatMod gee
What is a piecewise polynomial (polynomial spline)?	A function built from separate polynomials fitted on the intervals between knots, joined so the result is smooth at the knots.<br><br>\[ f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\beta_jx^{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{q}b_k\big(x-\kappa_k\big)_{+}^{p} \]	StatMod splines
How smooth is a polynomial spline of degree l at its knots?	It is l minus 1 times continuously differentiable there, so the derivatives up to that order match on both sides of each knot.	StatMod splines
What are the advantages and disadvantages of piecewise polynomials?	They offer local smoothness and high flexibility, but they need many parameters and can produce jumps if continuity is not enforced.	StatMod splines
What is the truncated power series basis?	A basis consisting of a global polynomial plus truncated power terms that switch on only beyond each knot, correcting the global polynomial locally.<br><br>\[ \big(x-\kappa_k\big)_{+}^{p}=\begin{cases}(x-\kappa_k)^p,&amp; x\gt \kappa_k\\ 0,&amp; \text{else}\end{cases} \]	StatMod splines basis
How does the truncated power basis turn spline fitting into a linear model?	The basis functions become the columns of a new design matrix, so the coefficients can be obtained by ordinary least squares.	StatMod splines basis
What is penalized least squares for splines?	The residual sum of squares plus a penalty scaled by a smoothing parameter, which controls the flexibility of the fitted curve.<br><br>\[ \min_{\beta,b}\ \|y-X\beta-Zb\|_2^2+\lambda\,\|b\|_2^2 \]	StatMod splines penalization
Which coefficients are penalized in a truncated power basis fit?	Only the coefficients of the truncated terms, not those of the global polynomial. The global trend should not be shrunk.	StatMod splines penalization
What is the mixed model connection to spline penalization?	The penalized spline coefficients can be treated as random effects with variance equal to the error variance divided by the smoothing parameter, so the smoothing parameter is estimated from data.<br><br>\[ \lambda=\frac{\sigma^2}{\tau^2},\qquad b_k\sim N(0,\tau^2) \]	StatMod splines mixed-model
What happens as the smoothing parameter goes to infinity?	The random effect variance goes to zero, so the truncated coefficients vanish and only the global polynomial remains.	StatMod splines penalization
What are B-splines and how are they defined?	A basis defined recursively, starting from indicator functions on knot intervals and building higher orders by weighted combinations of two lower-order splines.	StatMod splines b-splines
What is the local support property of B-splines?	Each basis function is non-zero only over a span of a few adjacent knots, which makes the basis numerically well behaved and locally interpretable.	StatMod splines b-splines
What is the partition of unity property of B-splines?	At every point the unweighted basis functions sum to 1, since multiple functions overlap at each location.	StatMod splines b-splines
What is the dimension of a B-spline basis?	The number of interior knots plus the degree minus 1. The number and placement of the knots matter a great deal.	StatMod splines b-splines
How is a B-spline fit penalized?	By penalizing the curvature, that is the integral of the squared second derivative, which for equidistant knots is approximated by squared second differences of adjacent coefficients.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{pen}=\int f''(x)^2dx\ \approx\ \sum_k\big(\Delta^2\beta_k\big)^2 \]	StatMod splines penalization
What is a smoothing spline?	The function minimizing penalized least squares over all functions; the solution is a cubic spline with a knot at every data point and natural boundary conditions.	StatMod splines smoothing
What is a linear smoother?	A fit expressible as a weighted average of the observed responses, so the fitted vector is a fixed smoother matrix times the data.	StatMod smoothing
How is the variance of a linear smoother obtained?	As the error variance times the squared norm of the weight vector at that point. This gives pointwise confidence intervals.	StatMod smoothing inference
How are degrees of freedom defined for a smoother?	As the trace of the smoother matrix. For penalized splines it is the trace of the hat matrix with the penalty included.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{df}=\operatorname{tr}(S),\qquad \hat y=Sy \]	StatMod smoothing degrees-of-freedom
What is the AIC for a smoother?	n times the log of the estimated error variance plus twice the effective degrees of freedom plus 1, minimized over the smoothing parameter.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{AIC}=n\log\hat\sigma^2+2\,\mathrm{df}+1 \]	StatMod smoothing model-selection
What is leave-one-out cross-validation for smoothing?	The average squared error where each prediction is made by a model refitted without that observation.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{CV}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(y_i-\hat f^{(-i)}(x_i)\big)^2 \]	StatMod smoothing cross-validation
What is an additive model?	A model with a separate smooth function for each covariate, added together. Each function has its own smoothing parameter.<br><br>\[ E(y\mid x)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{p}f_j(x_j) \]	StatMod additive-models
Why must the smooth functions in an additive model be centered?	Because otherwise each function could absorb an arbitrary constant, making the decomposition non-identifiable relative to the intercept.	StatMod additive-models identifiability
What is a generalized additive model?	An additive model combined with a GLM: an exponential family response whose mean is linked to a predictor containing both linear terms and smooth functions.	StatMod gam
What components can a GAM include?	Linear effects, effects with known transformations, smooth unknown effects in basis representation, categorical effects, all their interactions, and random effects.	StatMod gam
What is the objective function for GAM estimation?	The penalized log-likelihood: the log-likelihood minus a sum of quadratic penalties, one per smooth function with its own smoothing parameter.<br><br>\[ \ell(\beta)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_j\lambda_j\,\beta_j^{\top}K_j\beta_j \]	StatMod gam estimation
What are the four kinds of interactions available in a GAM?	Linear by linear (a product), smooth by linear, smooth by categorical (a separate curve per group), and smooth by smooth (a two-dimensional surface).	StatMod gam interactions
What are the data-rich and data-poor situations in model selection?	Data-rich allows an explicit split into training, validation and test sets; data-poor requires analytic approximation with AIC or BIC, resampling by cross-validation or bootstrap, or theoretical arguments.	StatMod model-selection
What are the three components of the prediction error decomposition?	The irreducible error from the noise around the true mean, the squared bias from model misspecification, and the variance of the estimate around its own mean.	StatMod model-selection bias-variance
What is the general form of an information criterion?	Minus twice the maximized log-likelihood plus a penalty depending on the number of parameters. AIC uses twice the parameter count, BIC uses log n times it.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{IC}=-2\,\ell(\hat\theta)+\mathrm{pen}\cdot p \]	StatMod model-selection
What does AIC estimate?	The expected KL divergence between the true density and the fitted model, evaluated on average over new data after parameter estimation.	StatMod model-selection aic
What does AIC not tell you?	How close the model comes to the true model in absolute terms. Only differences between AIC values of competing models are meaningful.	StatMod model-selection aic
When is bias-corrected AIC recommended?	When the ratio of sample size to number of parameters is below about 40.	StatMod model-selection aic
Why is selecting by AIC and then reading p-values overly optimistic?	Because the selection step already used the data, so the reported p-values do not account for the search and are anti-conservative.	StatMod model-selection inference
Why is regularization needed instead of exhaustive AIC search?	Because searching over all possible models is combinatorially infeasible for even a moderate number of covariates.	StatMod regularization
Why must covariates be standardized before penalized regression?	Because the penalty is not scale invariant: rescaling a covariate changes its coefficient magnitude and therefore how heavily it is penalized.	StatMod regularization standardization
What is the closed-form ridge estimator?	The usual least squares formula with the penalty times the identity added to the cross-product matrix before inverting. It is biased but has lower variance.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_{\text{ridge}}=\big(X^{\top}X+\lambda I\big)^{-1}X^{\top}y \]	StatMod regularization ridge
What does ridge do to the OLS estimate when the design is orthonormal?	It shrinks every coefficient by the same factor of one over one plus the penalty. Pure proportional shrinkage.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_{\text{ridge},j}=\frac{\hat\beta_{j}}{1+\lambda} \]	StatMod regularization ridge
How do bias and variance behave as the ridge penalty varies?	At zero penalty there is no bias; as the penalty tends to infinity the variance tends to zero. The optimum trades one against the other.	StatMod regularization ridge
What is the lasso solution when the design is orthonormal?	The soft-threshold rule: shift each OLS coefficient toward zero by the penalty amount and set it to zero if it would cross over.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_j=\mathrm{sign}\big(\hat\beta_j^{OLS}\big)\max\big(|\hat\beta_j^{OLS}|-\lambda,\ 0\big) \]	StatMod regularization lasso
Why does lasso produce exact zeros geometrically?	Because the L1 constraint region is a diamond with corners on the axes, so the contour of the loss typically first touches it at a corner where a coefficient is exactly zero.	StatMod regularization lasso
Why were SCAD and MCP developed?	To avoid the large bias that L1 and L2 penalties impose on genuinely large coefficients. They behave like L1 for small coefficients and like L0 for large ones.	StatMod regularization
What is the one-standard-error rule for choosing the penalty?	Instead of the cross-validation minimizer, take the largest penalty whose error is still within one standard error of the minimum. This gives a simpler model.	StatMod regularization cross-validation
What is group lasso?	A penalty applied to the Euclidean norm of coefficient groups, so an entire group such as all dummy levels of one categorical variable is included or excluded together.<br><br>\[ J(\beta)=\lambda\sum_{g}\sqrt{p_g}\,\big\|\beta_{g}\big\|_2 \]	StatMod regularization group-lasso
What is fused lasso?	A penalty on the absolute differences between successive coefficients, which shrinks neighbouring coefficients toward each other and creates piecewise constant profiles.<br><br>\[ J(\beta)=\lambda\sum_{j=2}^{p}\big|\beta_j-\beta_{j-1}\big| \]	StatMod regularization fused-lasso
How is lasso applied to a GLM?	By adding the L1 penalty to the negative log-likelihood instead of the residual sum of squares.	StatMod regularization glm
What is distributional regression?	Regression modelling every parameter of the response distribution as a function of covariates, not just the mean.	StatMod distributional-regression
What is a heteroscedastic regression model?	A model where both the mean and the standard deviation depend on covariates, with the scale using a positivity-preserving link such as the exponential.<br><br>\[ y_i\sim N\big(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2\big),\qquad \mu_i=x_i^{\top}\beta,\ \ \log\sigma_i=z_i^{\top}\gamma \]	StatMod distributional-regression
What is GAMLSS?	Generalized additive models for location, scale and shape: separate additive predictors, each with its own link, for the mean, the scale, the skewness and the kurtosis.	StatMod gamlss
What is the key conceptual advantage of distributional regression?	Covariates shape the entire conditional distribution rather than only shifting its centre.	StatMod distributional-regression
What is a zero-one inflated model?	A mixture placing point masses at 0 and 1 alongside a continuous beta component, for data concentrated at the boundaries of the unit interval.	StatMod gamlss
What is quantile regression?	Directly modelling a conditional quantile as a function of covariates, without going through a parametric distribution.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_\tau=\arg\min_\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_\tau\big(y_i-x_i^{\top}\beta\big) \]	StatMod quantile-regression
What is the pinball (check) loss?	An asymmetric absolute loss weighting positive residuals by tau and negative residuals by tau minus 1. Minimizing it estimates the tau-quantile.<br><br>\[ \rho_\tau(r)=\begin{cases}\tau\,r,&amp; r\ge 0\\ (\tau-1)\,r,&amp; r\lt 0\end{cases} \]	StatMod quantile-regression
What is the quantile crossing problem?	Because each quantile is estimated separately, the fitted curves for different quantile levels can cross, which is logically impossible for true quantiles.	StatMod quantile-regression
What is the advantage of quantile regression over distributional regression?	It requires no distributional assumption at all; the quantiles are modelled directly rather than derived from a fitted distribution.	StatMod quantile-regression
What is a transformation model?	A model learning a monotone transformation of the response such that the transformed variable follows a known reference distribution.	StatMod transformation-models
Why must the transformation function be monotone?	To preserve the ordering of the response, so the CDF relationship carries over and the transformation is invertible.	StatMod transformation-models
How is the density obtained in a transformation model?	Via the density transformation formula: the reference density evaluated at the transformed value, multiplied by the absolute derivative of the transformation.<br><br>\[ f_Y(y)=f_Z\big(h(y)\big)\cdot\big|h'(y)\big| \]	StatMod transformation-models
What is the simplest case of a transformation model?	A linear model in the log of the response. The general case replaces the log with a flexible monotone spline.	StatMod transformation-models
What are the defining properties of functional data?	Each observation is an entire curve rather than a scalar, the underlying process is assumed smooth, and the curve is observed at discrete time points with noise.	StatMod functional-data
How are the functional mean, variance and covariance defined?	As the ordinary mean, variance and covariance computed separately at each time point, with the covariance defined between pairs of time points.	StatMod functional-data
What are the two ways to center functional data?	Subtract the cross-sectional mean at each time point, or subtract each curve's own average level over time.	StatMod functional-data
What is functional PCA?	Decomposition of centered curves into a series of eigenfunctions weighted by scalar scores, where each successive eigenfunction explains the maximum remaining variance.<br><br>\[ x_i(t)=\bar x(t)+\sum_{k\ge 1}\xi_{ik}\,\phi_k(t) \]	StatMod functional-data fpca
What are FPC scores and how are they used?	The scalar coefficients of each curve on the eigenfunctions. They reduce a curve to a few numbers that can be used as ordinary covariates in a GAM.	StatMod functional-data fpca
What are the three types of functional regression models?	Scalar-on-function, function-on-scalar, and function-on-function, named by which of the response and covariates are curves.	StatMod functional-data regression
What is a scalar-on-function linear model?	A model where a functional covariate contributes through the integral of the curve times a coefficient function, so every time point has its own linear effect.<br><br>\[ y_i=\beta_0+\int_{\mathcal{T}}x_i(t)\,\beta(t)\,dt+\varepsilon_i \]	StatMod functional-data regression
Why must the integral in a functional model be approximated?	Because the curve is only observed at finitely many discrete time points, so the integral becomes a weighted sum over those points.	StatMod functional-data regression
What is function-on-scalar regression?	A model where the response is a curve and the coefficients are functions of time, estimated by minimizing residuals over all time points.	StatMod functional-data regression
What is a concurrent effect in function-on-function regression?	An effect where only the current value of the covariate influences the current value of the response, with no contribution from other time points.	StatMod functional-data regression
What is a historical linear effect?	An effect where only past covariate values, up to some lag before the current time, influence the current response. It enforces causality in time.	StatMod functional-data regression
What is a copula? (Statistical Modelling)	A model of the dependence structure alone, which can be combined with arbitrary marginal distributions.	StatMod copulas
What does Sklar's theorem state? (Statistical Modelling)	Every joint CDF can be written as a copula evaluated at the marginal CDFs, and the copula is the joint distribution of the probability-integral-transformed variables.<br><br>\[ F(y_1,\dots,y_d)=C\big(F_1(y_1),\dots,F_d(y_d)\big) \]	StatMod copulas sklar
What is Sklar's theorem for densities?	Any multivariate density factorizes into the copula density evaluated at the marginal CDFs times the product of the marginal densities. The copula density supplies all the dependence.<br><br>\[ f(y_1,\dots,y_d)=c\big(F_1(y_1),\dots,F_d(y_d)\big)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}f_j(y_j) \]	StatMod copulas sklar
How is a copula constructed by inverting Sklar's theorem?	Evaluate the joint CDF at the inverse marginal CDFs of its arguments. Applying this to a multivariate normal gives the Gaussian copula.	StatMod copulas
What is an Archimedean generator construction?	Build a copula from a single decreasing convex generator by applying it to each argument, summing, and applying the pseudo-inverse.	StatMod copulas archimedean
What are the copula rotations and what are they for?	Reflecting one or both arguments by replacing u with 1 minus u. Rotations turn a positively dependent copula into a negatively dependent one.	StatMod copulas
What are the three steps of copula estimation?	Estimate the marginals parametrically or non-parametrically, compute pseudo-observations by applying the estimated marginal CDFs, and then maximize the copula pseudo-likelihood.	StatMod copulas estimation
How is a sample drawn from a copula model?	Sample uniforms from the copula, then apply the inverse marginal CDFs component by component.	StatMod copulas simulation
Why is Pearson correlation a poor dependence measure?	It only captures linear dependence and it depends on the marginal distributions rather than on the copula alone.	StatMod copulas dependence
What is Spearman's rho in copula terms?	The Pearson correlation of the probability-integral-transformed variables, expressible as an integral against the copula density.	StatMod copulas dependence
What is Kendall's tau in copula terms?	The probability of concordance minus that of discordance between independent copies, expressible as an integral of the copula against its density.	StatMod copulas dependence
What is the crucial warning about zero rank correlation?	Zero Kendall's tau or Spearman's rho does not imply independence. Only zero mutual information characterizes independence.	StatMod copulas dependence
What is mutual information in copula terms?	The KL divergence between the joint density and the product of the marginals, which equals the negative entropy of the copula density. It is zero exactly under independence.<br><br>\[ I=\mathrm{KL}\big(f\,\|\,\textstyle\prod_j f_j\big)=-H(c) \]	StatMod copulas dependence
What is upper tail dependence? (Statistical Modelling)	The limiting conditional probability that one variable is extreme high given the other is, expressible directly through the copula near the upper corner.<br><br>\[ \lambda_U=\lim_{q\to 1^-}\frac{1-2q+C(q,q)}{1-q} \]	StatMod copulas tail-dependence
What are the tail dependence properties of the standard copulas?	Gaussian has none in either tail, Student t has both symmetrically, Clayton has lower tail dependence only, and Gumbel upper tail dependence only.	StatMod copulas tail-dependence
What is pair copula decomposition?	Factorizing a high-dimensional copula density into a product of bivariate copulas, some applied to conditional distributions.	StatMod copulas vine
What is a vine copula?	A hierarchical structure of trees specifying which pairs of variables are modelled by which bivariate copula at each conditioning level.	StatMod copulas vine
What are the three steps of Dissmann's algorithm?	At each tree level, select the structure via a maximum spanning tree on empirical Kendall's tau, estimate the pair-copula parameters, and choose the family with the best AIC or BIC.	StatMod copulas vine
What is the trade-off between experimental and observational studies?	Experiments minimize confounding through randomization but may suffer selection bias and small samples; observational studies give larger, more representative samples but face confounding.	StatMod causality
What are the key steps of the causal roadmap?	Pose a well-defined question, specify the target causal quantity and a causal model, link the observed data to it, assess identifiability, commit to an estimand, estimate, and interpret.	StatMod causality
What is confounding?	Bias arising when one or more variables affect both the treatment and the outcome, so the observed association mixes the causal effect with the common cause.	StatMod causality confounding
What is exogeneity in a linear model?	The covariate is uncorrelated with the error term, equivalently the conditional expectation of the error given the covariate is zero.	StatMod causality endogeneity
What is endogeneity and what does it cause?	An omitted variable that affects both the covariate and the outcome ends up in the error, making the covariate correlated with it. The estimated coefficient is then biased.	StatMod causality endogeneity
What is a directed acyclic graph in causal inference?	A graph whose arrows represent causal relationships, used to decide which variables to include in a regression to identify a causal effect.	StatMod causality dag
What is a back-door path?	A path from the treatment to the outcome that begins with an arrow pointing into the treatment. Such paths generate confounding.	StatMod causality dag
What is the difference between a confounder, a mediator and a collider?	A confounder causes both treatment and outcome, a mediator lies on the causal path between them, and a collider is caused by both.	StatMod causality dag
What are the d-separation rules for blocking a path?	A chain or fork is blocked by conditioning on the middle variable; an inverted fork (collider) is blocked by NOT conditioning on the collider or its descendants.	StatMod causality dag
Why is conditioning on a collider harmful?	It opens a path that was previously closed, creating spurious association between the two causes of the collider.	StatMod causality dag
What is the back-door criterion?	A set of variables suffices if it blocks all back-door paths from treatment to outcome and contains no descendant of the treatment.	StatMod causality dag
What are the main methods for drawing causal conclusions?	Randomized experiments, natural experiments, multiple regression adjusting for confounders identified from a DAG, instrumental variables, and propensity score matching.	StatMod causality
What are the two required properties of an instrumental variable?	It must be uncorrelated with the error (affecting the outcome only through the treatment), and it must be correlated with the treatment.	StatMod causality instrumental-variables
What is the intuition behind an instrumental variable?	Changing the instrument changes the treatment but not the unobserved confounder, so the induced variation in the outcome isolates the causal effect.	StatMod causality instrumental-variables
What is the simple IV estimator?	The ratio of the regression coefficient of the outcome on the instrument to that of the treatment on the instrument.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_{IV}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)}{\operatorname{Cov}(Z,D)} \]	StatMod causality instrumental-variables
What is two-stage least squares?	First regress the treatment on the instrument to obtain fitted values, then regress the outcome on those fitted values. It is algebraically identical to the ratio estimator.	StatMod causality instrumental-variables
What is the average treatment effect on the treated?	The expected difference between the outcome under treatment and under control, among those who actually received the treatment.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{ATT}=E\big[Y(1)-Y(0)\ \big|\ D=1\big] \]	StatMod causality ate
Why can the ATT not be computed directly from observed data?	Because the counterfactual outcome of the treated under no treatment is never observed. The naive difference of group means includes a selection bias term.	StatMod causality ate
What is the key identifying assumption of propensity score matching?	Given the observed covariates, the potential outcomes are conditionally independent of the treatment assignment: treatment depends only on the covariates.	StatMod causality propensity-score
What is a propensity score?	The probability of receiving treatment given the covariates, typically estimated by logistic regression.<br><br>\[ e(x)=P\big(D=1\mid X=x\big) \]	StatMod causality propensity-score
What are the four steps of propensity score matching?	Estimate the propensity score, match treated to untreated units with similar scores, check that covariate distributions are balanced, and then estimate the effect as in a randomized experiment.	StatMod causality propensity-score
What are the three assumptions of propensity score matching?	Positivity (everyone has a non-zero chance of either treatment), SUTVA (no interference between subjects), and exchangeability (all relevant confounders are observed).	StatMod causality propensity-score
What makes survival analysis special compared with ordinary regression?	Covariates and their effects can change over the observation period, and subjects can drop out before the event, so only a lower bound on the time is known.	StatMod survival
What are the key relations between hazard, survival and density?	The hazard is the density divided by the survival function, the survival function is the exponential of the negative cumulative hazard, and the density is the negative derivative of the survival function.<br><br>\[ h=\frac{f}{S},\qquad S(t)=e^{-H(t)},\qquad f=-S' \]	StatMod survival
What is the Kaplan-Meier estimator built from?	The product over observed event times of one minus the discrete hazard, estimated as events divided by the number at risk.<br><br>\[ \hat S(t)=\prod_{t_{(j)}\le t}\Big(1-\frac{d_j}{n_j}\Big) \]	StatMod survival kaplan-meier
What is the likelihood contribution under right censoring?	The density raised to the event indicator times the survival function raised to one minus the indicator: events contribute a density, censored cases a survival probability.<br><br>\[ L=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(y_i)^{d_i}\,S(y_i)^{1-d_i} \]	StatMod survival likelihood
Why does the censoring distribution drop out of the survival likelihood?	Because under independent censoring it contributes only factors free of the parameters of interest, so it becomes a multiplicative constant.	StatMod survival likelihood
What is a parametric transformation model in survival analysis?	A model for the log of the survival time as a linear predictor plus a scaled error, which makes covariate effects multiplicative on the duration time.<br><br>\[ \log T=x^{\top}\beta+\sigma\varepsilon\ \Rightarrow\ T=e^{x^{\top}\beta}\cdot e^{\sigma\varepsilon} \]	StatMod survival aft
Which error distributions correspond to which survival distributions?	A standard normal error gives lognormal times, a generalized extreme value error gives Weibull, a logistic error gives log-logistic, and log-gamma gives gamma.	StatMod survival aft
What is the Cox proportional hazards model? (Statistical Modelling)	A semi-parametric model where the hazard factorizes into an unspecified baseline hazard, treated as a nuisance parameter, times the exponential of a linear predictor without an intercept.<br><br>\[ h(t\mid x)=h_0(t)\exp\big(x^{\top}\beta\big) \]	StatMod survival cox
Why does the Cox model have no intercept?	Because a constant would be absorbed into the unspecified baseline hazard and hence not identifiable.	StatMod survival cox
What is the conditional probability underlying the Cox partial likelihood?	The probability that a particular individual is the one experiencing the event at a given time, given that someone in the risk set does. The baseline hazard cancels.<br><br>\[ PL(\beta)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\exp\big(x_{(i)}^{\top}\beta\big)}{\sum_{j\in R_i}\exp\big(x_j^{\top}\beta\big)} \]	StatMod survival cox partial-likelihood
What is the Breslow estimator?	An estimator of the cumulative baseline hazard that sums, over event times, the reciprocal of the total exponentiated linear predictor in the risk set.<br><br>\[ \hat H_0(t)=\sum_{t_{(i)}\le t}\frac{1}{\sum_{j\in R_i}\exp\big(x_j^{\top}\hat\beta\big)} \]	StatMod survival cox breslow
How is the conditional survival function estimated in a Cox model?	As the baseline survival function raised to the power of the exponentiated linear predictor.<br><br>\[ \hat S(t\mid x)=\hat S_0(t)^{\exp\left(x^{\top}\hat\beta\right)} \]	StatMod survival cox
What is the piecewise exponential model likelihood assumption?	The event indicators per interval are treated as Poisson with mean the piecewise constant hazard times the time at risk, which gives a likelihood proportional to the proportional hazards likelihood.<br><br>\[ d_{ij}\sim\mathrm{Poi}\big(\mu_{ij}=h_{ij}\,t_{ij}\big) \]	StatMod survival pem
What does the piecewise exponential model do with time?	It treats time as just another covariate, so a smooth function of time can serve as the baseline hazard and interactions with time give time-varying effects.	StatMod survival pem
What is the trade-off in the number of cut points in a PEM?	Few intervals give a crude but stable and cheap approximation of the baseline hazard; many intervals give more flexibility at higher computational cost and greater instability.	StatMod survival pem
Why are time-dependent covariates easy to include in a PEM?	Because the data are already split into intervals, so a covariate value can simply be updated in each interval's row.	StatMod survival pem
