What are the three response and covariate combinations that define regression, ANOVA and ANCOVA?	Regression has a metric response and metric covariates; analysis of variance has a metric response and nominal factors; analysis of covariance has both metric regressors and nominal factors in the same model.	Stat2 linear-models overview
What distinguishes linear, non-linear and non-parametric regression?	In linear regression the mean function is a prescribed linear form; in non-linear regression the functional form is given but not linear; in non-parametric regression the mean function itself is unknown and estimated from the data.	Stat2 regression overview
What is the simple linear regression model?	The response equals an intercept plus a slope times the covariate plus an error term, where the errors are uncorrelated with mean zero and common variance.<br><br>\[ y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i+\varepsilon_i,\qquad E(\varepsilon_i)=0,\ \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i)=\sigma^2 \]	Stat2 regression
What is homoscedasticity?	All error terms have the same variance. It is the assumption that the scatter around the regression line is constant across the range of the covariate.	Stat2 regression assumptions
Why are the responses in a regression not identically distributed?	Because each has a different mean determined by its own covariate value, even though all share the same error variance.	Stat2 regression assumptions
What is the least squares criterion?	Minimize the sum of squared residuals, that is the squared vertical distances between observed responses and the fitted line.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta=\arg\min_{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(y_i-\beta_0-\beta_1x_i\big)^2 \]	Stat2 regression least-squares
What are the normal equations?	The system obtained by setting the gradient of the sum of squared residuals to zero. Their solution is the least squares estimator.	Stat2 regression least-squares
Why is it unnecessary to check the Hessian in least squares estimation?	Because the criterion is a convex quadratic function that is bounded below, so any stationary point is automatically the global minimum.	Stat2 regression least-squares
What is the least squares slope estimator in simple linear regression?	The empirical covariance of the covariate and the response divided by the empirical variance of the covariate. It is the standardized co-movement of the two variables.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_1=\frac{\sum_i (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum_i (x_i-\bar x)^2}=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}} \]	Stat2 regression least-squares
What is the least squares intercept estimator?	The mean of the response minus the slope times the mean of the covariate. The fitted line always passes through the point of the two sample means.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_0=\bar y-\hat\beta_1\bar x \]	Stat2 regression least-squares
What happens to the slope estimate when the empirical covariance is zero?	The slope is zero, meaning the covariate has no linear influence on the response in the fitted model.	Stat2 regression
What key advantage does least squares have over maximum likelihood?	It requires no distributional assumption at all, only the mean and variance structure. Maximum likelihood needs the full distribution to be specified.	Stat2 regression least-squares
Under normal errors, what is the relation between least squares and maximum likelihood?	They coincide. Maximizing a normal likelihood is equivalent to minimizing the sum of squared deviations, since the exponent contains exactly that sum.	Stat2 regression likelihood
Are least squares estimators unbiased?	Yes, under the standard assumptions of zero-mean uncorrelated errors, without needing any normality assumption.	Stat2 regression unbiasedness
What is a residual in regression?	The difference between the observed response and the fitted value. It is the empirical counterpart of the unobservable true error.	Stat2 regression residuals
Why are residuals not the same as the true errors?	Because residuals are computed with estimated coefficients. They are correlated with each other and have smaller variance than the true errors, whereas the true errors are uncorrelated.	Stat2 regression residuals
What is the unbiased estimator of the error variance in simple linear regression?	The residual sum of squares divided by n-2. Two degrees of freedom are consumed by estimating the intercept and slope.<br><br>\[ \hat\sigma^2=\frac{1}{n-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat\varepsilon_i^{\,2} \]	Stat2 regression variance-estimation
What is the sum of squares decomposition (SQT = SQE + SQR)?	Total variation of the response splits into the variation explained by the regression (fitted values around the mean) plus the unexplained residual variation.<br><br>\[ \underbrace{\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2}_{SQT}=\underbrace{\sum_i(\hat y_i-\bar y)^2}_{SQE}+\underbrace{\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}_{SQR} \]	Stat2 regression anova
How is the sum of squares decomposition proved?	By adding and subtracting the fitted value inside the total sum of squares; the resulting cross term vanishes.	Stat2 regression anova
What is the coefficient of determination R squared?	The explained sum of squares divided by the total sum of squares, equivalently one minus the residual share. It is the proportion of response variation explained by the model.<br><br>\[ R^2=\frac{SQE}{SQT}=1-\frac{SQR}{SQT}\in[0,1] \]	Stat2 regression r-squared
What does R squared equal 1 mean?	The residual sum of squares is zero: every data point lies exactly on the fitted line. The model fits perfectly.	Stat2 regression r-squared
What is the relation between R squared and the correlation coefficient in simple regression?	R squared equals the squared Pearson correlation between the covariate and the response. With one regressor the two measures are equivalent.<br><br>\[ R^2=r_{xy}^2 \]	Stat2 regression r-squared
What is adjusted R squared and why is it needed?	A version of R squared penalized by the number of covariates. Plain R squared never decreases when variables are added, so it cannot be used for model comparison.<br><br>\[ R^2_{adj}=1-\frac{SQR/(n-p-1)}{SQT/(n-1)} \]	Stat2 regression r-squared model-selection
Under the normality assumption, what is the distribution of the least squares estimator?	Normal, because it is a linear combination of the normally distributed responses. Its variance involves the inverse of the covariate cross-product.	Stat2 regression inference
Why is a large spread of covariate values desirable in regression?	Because the variance of the slope estimator is inversely proportional to the spread of the covariate. Spread-out covariate values pin down the slope more precisely.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_1)=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2} \]	Stat2 regression design
What is the test statistic for a regression coefficient?	The estimated coefficient minus its hypothesized value divided by its standard error, which follows a t distribution with n-2 degrees of freedom in simple regression.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j^{0}}{\widehat{\mathrm{se}}(\hat\beta_j)}\ \sim\ t_{n-2} \]	Stat2 regression testing
What does the standard error of a coefficient measure?	The estimated standard deviation of the coefficient estimator. It is the estimated version of the true sampling variability of the estimate.	Stat2 regression inference
Why is the confidence interval for a regression coefficient symmetric?	Because it is built from the t distribution, which is symmetric, so the interval is the estimate plus and minus the same margin.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_j\ \pm\ t_{n-p-1;\,1-\alpha/2}\;\widehat{\mathrm{se}}(\hat\beta_j) \]	Stat2 regression confidence-intervals
Where is the confidence interval for the regression line narrowest?	At the mean of the covariate. Uncertainty grows as you move away from the center of the data.	Stat2 regression confidence-intervals
What is the difference between a confidence band for the regression line and a prediction interval?	A confidence band covers the mean response, while a prediction interval covers a new individual observation and is therefore wider because it must also absorb the error term.	Stat2 regression prediction
Why is a simultaneous confidence band for the whole line wider than pointwise intervals?	Because it must hold at all covariate values at once, which requires a larger critical value drawn from an F distribution rather than a t distribution.	Stat2 regression confidence-intervals
What is the setup of one-way analysis of variance?	Observations fall into k groups, each with its own group mean, and the goal is to estimate those means and the common error variance, then test whether they are all equal.	Stat2 anova
What is the least squares estimator of a group mean in one-way ANOVA?	The arithmetic mean of the observations in that group. Each group is estimated purely from its own data.<br><br>\[ \hat\mu_k=\bar y_{k\cdot}=\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{i=1}^{n_k}y_{ki} \]	Stat2 anova
What is the ANOVA sum of squares decomposition?	Total variation splits into within-group variation (residual) plus between-group variation. Between-group variation compares group means to the overall mean.<br><br>\[ \sum_{k}\sum_i (y_{ki}-\bar y)^2=\underbrace{\sum_k n_k(\bar y_{k\cdot}-\bar y)^2}_{\text{between}}+\underbrace{\sum_k\sum_i(y_{ki}-\bar y_{k\cdot})^2}_{\text{within}} \]	Stat2 anova decomposition
What is a mean square in ANOVA?	A sum of squares divided by its degrees of freedom. It converts a raw sum of squares into a variance-like quantity that can be compared.	Stat2 anova
What is the F statistic in one-way ANOVA?	The between-group mean square divided by the within-group mean square. It compares the spread of the group means against the noise level inside the groups.<br><br>\[ F=\frac{SQE/(k-1)}{SQR/(n-k)}\ \sim\ F_{k-1,\,n-k} \]	Stat2 anova f-test
What are the degrees of freedom of the one-way ANOVA F statistic?	k-1 in the numerator and n-k in the denominator, where k is the number of groups and n the total sample size.	Stat2 anova f-test
What is the null hypothesis of one-way ANOVA?	That all group means are equal, so the factor has no influence on the response. The alternative is that at least two group means differ.	Stat2 anova
What critical assumption underlies the ANOVA F test?	Equal variance in all groups. The test compares means, but it assumes a single shared error variance across groups.	Stat2 anova assumptions
What does one-way ANOVA reduce to when there are exactly two groups?	The two-sample t-test. The F statistic is then the square of the t statistic.	Stat2 anova
What is the effect coding (alternative) representation of ANOVA?	Each group mean is written as an overall level plus a group-specific effect, with the constraint that the weighted effects sum to zero to make the parameterization identifiable.<br><br>\[ \mu_k=\mu+\alpha_k,\qquad \textstyle\sum_k n_k\alpha_k=0 \]	Stat2 anova parameterization
Why is a side constraint needed in the effect representation?	Because otherwise the parameters are not identifiable: adding a constant to the overall level and subtracting it from every effect gives the same model.	Stat2 anova identifiability
What is two-way ANOVA (cross-classification)?	A design with two factors, where each combination of levels forms a cell. Combining the factors reduces it conceptually to a one-way analysis over cells.	Stat2 anova
What are the components of the general linear model?	A response vector, a design matrix of covariates, an unknown parameter vector, and an error vector with zero mean and a scalar covariance matrix.<br><br>\[ y=X\beta+\varepsilon,\qquad E(\varepsilon)=0,\ \operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon)=\sigma^2 I_n \]	Stat2 linear-models
What is the design matrix and what does its rank mean?	The matrix of covariate values, with one row per observation. Its rank tells how many linearly independent covariate directions there are; full rank means the columns are not redundant.	Stat2 linear-models design-matrix
What is the normal linear model?	A linear model whose error vector is multivariate normal with a scalar covariance matrix. Normality is what enables exact tests and confidence intervals.	Stat2 linear-models
What is the column space L of the design matrix, geometrically?	The set of all vectors that can be produced as linear combinations of the covariates. It is the subspace in which the fitted mean vector must lie.	Stat2 linear-models geometry
What is the geometric interpretation of least squares estimation?	Orthogonal projection of the response vector onto the column space of the design matrix. The fitted values are the closest point in that subspace to the data.	Stat2 linear-models geometry projection
What are the two defining properties of a projection matrix?	Idempotence (applying it twice equals applying it once) and symmetry. Together they characterize orthogonal projection.<br><br>\[ H^2=H,\qquad H^{\top}=H \]	Stat2 linear-models projection
What is the projection matrix (hat matrix) when the design matrix has full rank?	X times the inverse of X-transpose-X times X-transpose. It maps the response directly to the fitted values.<br><br>\[ H=X\big(X^{\top}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top},\qquad \hat y=Hy \]	Stat2 linear-models projection
What is the complementary projection matrix?	The identity minus the hat matrix. It projects onto the orthogonal complement and maps the response directly to the residual vector.<br><br>\[ I-H,\qquad \hat\varepsilon=(I-H)\,y \]	Stat2 linear-models projection residuals
Are projection matrices invertible?	No, except for the identity. They collapse the orthogonal complement to zero, so they are singular.	Stat2 linear-models projection
What is the least squares estimator in the general linear model?	The inverse of X-transpose-X multiplied by X-transpose times the response, valid when the design matrix has full rank.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta=\big(X^{\top}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}y \]	Stat2 linear-models least-squares
What is the covariance matrix of the least squares estimator?	Sigma squared times the inverse of X-transpose-X. Collinearity among covariates inflates this inverse and hence the variances.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(\hat\beta)=\sigma^2\big(X^{\top}X\big)^{-1} \]	Stat2 linear-models inference
Why does full rank of the design matrix matter?	It makes X-transpose-X invertible, which gives a unique least squares solution. Without full rank there are infinitely many solutions.	Stat2 linear-models identifiability
What is the unbiased variance estimator in the general linear model?	The residual sum of squares divided by n-r, where r is the rank of the design matrix. The subtraction accounts for the dimensions used up by fitting.<br><br>\[ \hat\sigma^2=\frac{\hat\varepsilon^{\top}\hat\varepsilon}{n-r},\qquad r=\operatorname{rank}(X) \]	Stat2 linear-models variance-estimation
What is the distribution of the residual sum of squares under normality?	Sigma squared times a chi-squared distribution with n-r degrees of freedom. This is what makes exact F tests possible.<br><br>\[ \frac{\hat\varepsilon^{\top}\hat\varepsilon}{\sigma^2}\ \sim\ \chi^2_{n-r} \]	Stat2 linear-models inference
What is the independence result between the projection and its complement?	The fitted value vector and the residual vector are independent under normality, because the two projections are orthogonal and jointly normal.	Stat2 linear-models inference
What is an estimable function?	A linear combination of the parameters for which some linear function of the response is an unbiased estimator. Not every combination is estimable when the design lacks full rank.	Stat2 linear-models estimability
When is every linear combination of parameters estimable?	When the design matrix has full rank. Then the parameter vector itself is identified and all its linear functions can be estimated unbiasedly.	Stat2 linear-models estimability
What does the Gauss-Markov theorem state?	Among all linear unbiased estimators, the least squares estimator has the smallest variance. It is the best linear unbiased estimator, or BLUE.	Stat2 linear-models gauss-markov
What assumptions does the Gauss-Markov theorem require?	Only that errors have zero mean, common variance and are uncorrelated. Normality is not needed.	Stat2 linear-models gauss-markov
Why is the Gauss-Markov result restricted to linear estimators?	Because outside that class a non-linear or biased estimator may achieve lower variance or lower mean squared error. Optimality is relative to the class considered.	Stat2 linear-models gauss-markov
What is the variance of the residual vector?	Sigma squared times the identity minus the hat matrix. Residual variances are therefore smaller than the true error variance.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(\hat\varepsilon)=\sigma^2\,(I-H) \]	Stat2 linear-models residuals
Why must the diagonal entries of the hat matrix lie between 0 and 1?	Because otherwise the residual variance formula would give a negative variance, which is impossible.	Stat2 linear-models residuals leverage
What is leverage in regression?	The diagonal element of the hat matrix for an observation. It measures how strongly that observation pulls its own fitted value, that is, its potential influence.<br><br>\[ h_{ii}=\big[H\big]_{ii}\in[0,1],\qquad \textstyle\sum_i h_{ii}=r \]	Stat2 linear-models leverage
What is a standardized residual?	The residual divided by the estimated standard deviation times the square root of one minus its leverage. This rescales all residuals to unit variance.<br><br>\[ r_i=\frac{\hat\varepsilon_i}{\hat\sigma\sqrt{1-h_{ii}}} \]	Stat2 linear-models residuals
What is a studentized residual and why is it preferred?	A residual standardized using a variance estimate computed with that observation deleted. Removing the point makes the residual independent of its own variance estimate, so it follows an exact t distribution.	Stat2 linear-models residuals
What is a Tukey-Anscombe plot?	A plot of residuals against fitted values. Under a correct model it should show unstructured scatter, because residuals and fitted values are uncorrelated.	Stat2 linear-models diagnostics
What patterns in a Tukey-Anscombe plot indicate problems?	A funnel shape indicates heteroscedasticity, and a curved pattern indicates a missing non-linear term in the mean structure.	Stat2 linear-models diagnostics
What is the general form of a linear hypothesis?	A hypothesis matrix H multiplied by the parameter vector equals zero. The number of rows of H is the number of restrictions, that is, the dimension reduction.<br><br>\[ H_0:\ H\beta=0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(H)=q \]	Stat2 linear-models testing
What is the idea behind the F test for a linear hypothesis?	Compare the projection of the response onto the full space with its projection onto the restricted subspace. If the two are far apart relative to noise, the restriction is rejected.	Stat2 linear-models testing f-test
What is the general F statistic for a linear hypothesis?	The difference in residual sums of squares between the restricted and full models, scaled by the number of restrictions, divided by the estimated error variance of the full model.<br><br>\[ F=\frac{\big(SQR_{H_0}-SQR\big)/q}{SQR/(n-r)}\ \sim\ F_{q,\,n-r} \]	Stat2 linear-models testing f-test
How is the dimension reduction q determined for common hypotheses?	For a single regressor coefficient it is 1; for a factor with s levels it is s-1, because one level is absorbed by the reference.	Stat2 linear-models testing
Why is the F test measured in units of the estimated variance?	Because the raw distance between projections is scale dependent. Dividing by the estimated error variance makes the comparison scale free.	Stat2 linear-models testing
What is weak consistency of the least squares estimator and what does it require?	Convergence in probability to the true parameter. It requires that the design keeps full rank and that the inverse cross-product matrix tends to zero as n grows.	Stat2 linear-models asymptotics
Why must the inverse of X-transpose-X tend to zero for consistency?	Because that inverse is proportional to the estimator variance. Only if it vanishes does the estimator variance shrink to zero.	Stat2 linear-models asymptotics
What is an exponential family in one parameter?	A family of densities of the form exp of a parameter function times a sufficient statistic, plus a function of the data, minus a normalizing function.<br><br>\[ f(y\mid\theta)=\exp\!\big\{\theta\,T(y)-b(\theta)+c(y)\big\} \]	Stat2 exponential-family
What is the canonical form of an exponential family?	The form in which the parameter function is the identity and the sufficient statistic is the observation itself. The parameter is then called the natural parameter.	Stat2 exponential-family
What is the natural parameter space?	The set of parameter values for which the integral defining the family converges, so that the density is well defined and normalizable.	Stat2 exponential-family
What is the fundamental derivative property of an exponential family?	The first derivative of the normalizing function b gives the mean of the sufficient statistic, and its second derivative gives the variance.<br><br>\[ b'(\theta)=E\big(T(Y)\big),\qquad b''(\theta)=\operatorname{Var}\big(T(Y)\big) \]	Stat2 exponential-family
Why must the second derivative of b be positive?	Because it equals a variance, and variances cannot be negative. This also makes b convex and its first derivative strictly monotone.	Stat2 exponential-family
Why is the strict monotonicity of the first derivative of b important?	Because it guarantees an invertible link between the natural parameter and the mean, which is exactly the response function of a GLM.	Stat2 exponential-family glm
Why is the uniform distribution not an exponential family?	Because the support of its density depends on the parameter. Exponential family members must have a support that is the same for all parameter values.	Stat2 exponential-family
Which common distributions belong to the exponential family?	The normal, binomial, Poisson, gamma, exponential, negative binomial, geometric and beta distributions all do.	Stat2 exponential-family
What is the natural parameter of the binomial distribution?	The log-odds, the logarithm of p divided by 1 minus p. Inverting it gives the logistic response function.<br><br>\[ \theta=\log\frac{p}{1-p},\qquad p=\frac{e^{\theta}}{1+e^{\theta}} \]	Stat2 exponential-family binomial
What is the natural parameter of the Poisson distribution?	The logarithm of the intensity. This is why the log link is the natural link for Poisson regression.<br><br>\[ \theta=\log\lambda,\qquad \lambda=e^{\theta} \]	Stat2 exponential-family poisson
What is the joint density of independent exponential family observations?	Again an exponential family, with the sufficient statistics simply summed across observations. Independence keeps you inside the family.	Stat2 exponential-family
What is a generalized linear model?	A model where the response follows some exponential family distribution and a link function connects a linear predictor to the mean of the response.	Stat2 glm
What are the three components of a GLM?	A random component (the response distribution from the exponential family), a systematic component (the linear predictor), and a link function connecting the two.	Stat2 glm
How is the normal linear model a special case of a GLM?	It is the GLM with a normal response distribution and the identity link function.	Stat2 glm
What is the linear predictor?	The linear combination of covariates and coefficients. It is the part of the model that is linear in the parameters.<br><br>\[ \eta_i=x_i^{\top}\beta=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{i1}+\dots+\beta_px_{ip} \]	Stat2 glm
What is the link function?	A twice continuously differentiable, strictly monotone function that maps the mean of the response onto the linear predictor. Its inverse is the response function.<br><br>\[ g(\mu_i)=\eta_i=x_i^{\top}\beta \]	Stat2 glm link-function
What is the response function?	The inverse of the link function, mapping the linear predictor back to the mean of the response. It is used to convert model output back to the original scale.<br><br>\[ \mu_i=h(\eta_i)=g^{-1}\big(x_i^{\top}\beta\big) \]	Stat2 glm link-function
Why is a link function needed at all?	Because the mean of the response is often restricted, for example to positive values or to the interval from 0 to 1, while the linear predictor ranges over all real numbers.	Stat2 glm link-function
What is the natural (canonical) link function?	The link that sets the natural parameter equal to the linear predictor. It makes the score equations especially simple and the Hessian deterministic.	Stat2 glm link-function
What is the natural link for logistic regression?	The logit, the log-odds. Its inverse is the logistic response function that maps any real number into the interval from 0 to 1.<br><br>\[ g(\mu)=\log\frac{\mu}{1-\mu},\qquad h(\eta)=\frac{e^{\eta}}{1+e^{\eta}} \]	Stat2 glm logistic
What is the natural link for Poisson regression?	The logarithm. Its inverse, the exponential, guarantees a positive fitted intensity.<br><br>\[ g(\mu)=\log\mu,\qquad h(\eta)=e^{\eta} \]	Stat2 glm poisson
What is the dispersion parameter in a GLM?	An additional nuisance parameter scaling the variance, such as the variance in the normal case. It equals 1 for the binomial and Poisson families.	Stat2 glm dispersion
Why is there no error term in a GLM?	Because the randomness is fully specified by the response distribution itself. An additive error term would not be identically distributed and is not needed.	Stat2 glm
What is the variance structure in a GLM?	The variance is a function of the mean, multiplied by the dispersion parameter. Unlike in the linear model, the variance is not constant across observations.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(y_i)=\phi\,v(\mu_i) \]	Stat2 glm variance
What is the score vector in a GLM?	The gradient of the log-likelihood with respect to the coefficients. Setting it to zero gives the maximum likelihood equations.	Stat2 glm estimation
What is the special form of the GLM score equations under a natural link?	The design matrix transpose times the difference between observed responses and fitted means equals zero. It is the direct analogue of the normal equations.<br><br>\[ X^{\top}\big(y-\mu(\beta)\big)=0 \]	Stat2 glm estimation
What is the Fisher information matrix in a GLM?	The design matrix transpose times a weight matrix depending on the variances times the design matrix. It generalizes the cross-product matrix of the linear model.<br><br>\[ F(\beta)=X^{\top}WX,\qquad W=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{h'(\eta_i)^2}{\phi\,v(\mu_i)}\right) \]	Stat2 glm estimation
What makes the natural link special for GLM estimation?	The Hessian of the log-likelihood is deterministic and equals the negative Fisher information, so it is negative definite and the ML estimator is unique.	Stat2 glm estimation
How are GLM parameters estimated in practice?	Iteratively, typically by Fisher scoring or iteratively reweighted least squares, because the score equations are non-linear and have no closed-form solution.	Stat2 glm estimation
What can and cannot be done exactly in a GLM compared to a linear model?	The GLM has no closed-form solution and no exact tests or confidence intervals. All inference is asymptotic.	Stat2 glm inference
What are the three asymptotic tests in a GLM?	The likelihood ratio test, the Wald test, and the score test. All three are asymptotically chi-squared under the null.	Stat2 glm testing
What does the likelihood ratio test statistic compare?	Twice the difference between the maximized log-likelihoods of the full and restricted models. It requires fitting both models.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{lq}=2\big(\ell(\hat\theta)-\ell(\tilde\theta)\big)\ \stackrel{a}{\sim}\ \chi^2_q \]	Stat2 glm testing
What does the Wald test require?	Only the full model fit. It measures how far the estimate is from the hypothesized value in units of its estimated standard error.<br><br>\[ W=\big(H\hat\beta-c\big)^{\top}\big[H\,\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\hat\beta)\,H^{\top}\big]^{-1}\big(H\hat\beta-c\big) \]	Stat2 glm testing
What does the score test require?	Only the restricted model fit. It checks whether the score of the full model, evaluated at the restricted estimate, is close to zero.	Stat2 glm testing
Why is the Wald test called a to-remove test?	Because it needs only the fitted full model, so it is convenient for deciding whether an already included variable can be dropped.	Stat2 glm model-selection
Why is the score test called a to-enter test?	Because it needs only the restricted model, so it can screen candidate variables for inclusion without fitting each expanded model.	Stat2 glm model-selection
What is the deviance of a GLM?	Twice the difference between the log-likelihood of the saturated model and that of the fitted model. It generalizes the residual sum of squares.<br><br>\[ D=2\phi\big(\ell_{\text{sat}}-\ell(\hat\beta)\big) \]	Stat2 glm deviance
What is the saturated model?	The model with one parameter per observation, which fits the data perfectly. It provides the upper reference point for the achievable likelihood.	Stat2 glm deviance
What is the null deviance in R output?	The deviance of the model containing only an intercept, with n-1 degrees of freedom. It is the baseline against which the fitted model is compared.	Stat2 glm deviance
What is the AIC and how is it used?	A model selection criterion balancing likelihood against the number of parameters. Lower values indicate a better trade-off between fit and complexity.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{AIC}=-2\ell(\hat\beta)+2p \]	Stat2 model-selection aic
What is backward elimination?	Start from the full model and successively remove the variable with the weakest evidence until all remaining variables are needed.	Stat2 model-selection
What is forward selection?	Start from the intercept-only model and successively add the variable that improves the model most, until no further addition helps.	Stat2 model-selection
What is best subset selection and what is its drawback?	Fitting all possible combinations of covariates and choosing the best. It is optimal in principle but the number of models grows exponentially.	Stat2 model-selection
How is an asymptotic confidence interval for a GLM coefficient built?	From the estimate plus and minus a normal quantile times the standard error taken from the corresponding diagonal element of the inverse observed information.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_j\ \pm\ z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\big[F(\hat\beta)^{-1}\big]_{jj}} \]	Stat2 glm confidence-intervals
Why is a joint confidence region for several parameters larger than the individual intervals?	Because it must cover all parameters simultaneously; it is an ellipse based on a chi-squared quantile rather than a rectangle of independent intervals.	Stat2 glm confidence-intervals
What is an offset in a regression model?	A covariate whose coefficient is fixed at 1 rather than estimated. It is used to account for known exposure, such as modelling counts per unit of population.<br><br>\[ \eta_i=\log(t_i)+x_i^{\top}\beta\ \Rightarrow\ \mu_i=t_i\,e^{x_i^{\top}\beta} \]	Stat2 glm offset
What is weighted least squares?	Minimizing a weighted sum of squared residuals rather than the plain sum. It is the correct approach when the errors have unequal variances.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta=\arg\min_\beta\sum_i w_i\big(y_i-x_i^{\top}\beta\big)^2 \]	Stat2 linear-models weighted-least-squares
What are the optimal weights in weighted least squares?	The reciprocals of the error variances, so that precise observations count more. The practical problem is that the true variances are usually unknown.	Stat2 linear-models weighted-least-squares
What is the linear model with correlated errors?	A model where the error covariance is a general matrix V rather than a scalar multiple of the identity. It arises in time series and clustered data.	Stat2 linear-models correlated-errors
What is the generalized least squares estimator?	The estimator that pre-multiplies by the inverse error covariance matrix in both the cross-product and the right-hand side. It is unbiased and BLUE under correlated errors.<br><br>\[ \hat\beta_{GLS}=\big(X^{\top}V^{-1}X\big)^{-1}X^{\top}V^{-1}y \]	Stat2 linear-models gls
What is the basic idea of Bayesian estimation?	Treat the unknown parameter as a random variable with a prior distribution, and update that prior with the data via Bayes' rule to obtain a posterior distribution.<br><br>\[ p(\theta\mid y)=\frac{f(y\mid\theta)\,p(\theta)}{\int f(y\mid\theta)p(\theta)\,d\theta}\ \propto\ f(y\mid\theta)\,p(\theta) \]	Stat2 bayes
What is a prior distribution?	A distribution over the parameter space expressing subjective beliefs before seeing the data. Its parameters are called hyperparameters.	Stat2 bayes prior
What is a posterior distribution?	The conditional distribution of the parameter given the observed data. It is proportional to the likelihood times the prior.	Stat2 bayes posterior
Why can the denominator in Bayes' rule often be ignored?	Because it integrates the parameter out and is therefore a constant not depending on the parameter. It only serves as a normalizing factor.	Stat2 bayes posterior
What is the posterior mean estimator?	The expected value of the parameter under the posterior distribution. It is the Bayesian analogue of a point estimate under squared error loss.	Stat2 bayes estimation
What is the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimator?	The parameter value maximizing the posterior density, equivalently maximizing the sum of the log-likelihood and the log-prior. It is the Bayesian analogue of the ML estimator.<br><br>\[ \hat\theta_{MAP}=\arg\max_\theta\ \big[\log f(y\mid\theta)+\log p(\theta)\big] \]	Stat2 bayes estimation map
What is the difference between MAP and ML estimation?	MAP adds the log-prior to the log-likelihood. With a flat, uninformative prior the two coincide.	Stat2 bayes estimation
What is the posterior mean for a normal mean with a normal prior?	A weighted average of the sample mean and the prior mean, with weights determined by the sample size and the two variances. Data and prior are precision-weighted.<br><br>\[ E(\mu\mid y)=\frac{\tfrac{n}{\sigma^2}\bar y+\tfrac{1}{\tau^2}\mu_0}{\tfrac{n}{\sigma^2}+\tfrac{1}{\tau^2}} \]	Stat2 bayes normal
What happens to the posterior as the prior variance grows large?	The prior becomes uninformative and the posterior mean converges to the sample mean, recovering the classical estimate.	Stat2 bayes prior
What happens to the posterior as the sample size grows?	The data dominate and the influence of the prior vanishes. Bayesian and frequentist estimates converge.	Stat2 bayes asymptotics
What is a Lorenz curve?	A curve plotting the cumulative share of the response against the cumulative share of observations, after sorting observations by their fitted values. It visualizes concentration and discriminatory power.	Stat2 model-evaluation lorenz
What is the Gini coefficient?	A summary of the Lorenz curve: the area between the diagonal of equality and the curve, relative to the total area under the diagonal.<br><br>\[ G=\frac{A}{A+B}\in[0,1] \]	Stat2 model-evaluation gini
What is a normalized Gini coefficient?	The Gini coefficient divided by the Gini coefficient of the optimal curve, obtained by sorting on the true values. It measures how close the model comes to the best achievable ordering.	Stat2 model-evaluation gini
What is a lift plot?	A plot that sorts observations by fitted value, groups them into equal-sized bins, and compares mean observed to mean predicted values per bin. It checks calibration across the risk spectrum.	Stat2 model-evaluation lift
What does Chebyshev's inequality state?	The probability of deviating from the mean by at least k is bounded by the variance divided by k squared. It holds for any distribution with finite variance.<br><br>\[ P\big(|X-E(X)|\ge k\big)\ \le\ \frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{k^2} \]	Stat2 probability chebyshev
What is the sufficient condition for consistency used throughout?	Asymptotically unbiased with variance tending to zero implies consistency, via Chebyshev's inequality.	Stat2 estimation consistency
What should you always specify when fitting a GLM in R?	The family, which determines the response distribution and the default link function. Omitting it silently gives a Gaussian fit.	Stat2 glm software
