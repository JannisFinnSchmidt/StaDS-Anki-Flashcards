What is the inversion method for simulating a random variable?	If U ~ Uniform(0,1) and F is a CDF, then X = F-inverse(U) has distribution F. It turns uniform random numbers into draws from any distribution whose (generalized) inverse CDF you can write down.<br><br>\[ U\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1),\quad X=F^{-1}(U)\ \Rightarrow\ X\sim F \]	AKS simulation monte-carlo
What is the generalized inverse (quantile function) and why is it needed?	F-generalized-inverse(u) = min{x : F(x) >= u}. The ordinary inverse only exists if F is continuous and strictly increasing; the generalized inverse is defined for every right-continuous CDF, including those with jumps or flat parts.<br><br>\[ F^{-}(u)=\min\{x: F(x)\ge u\} \]	AKS simulation
If F is continuous and strictly increasing on an interval, what is the relation between the generalized inverse and the ordinary inverse?	They coincide: F-generalized-inverse = F-inverse on that interval. Strict monotonicity removes flat parts, continuity removes jumps.	AKS simulation
If U ~ Uniform(0,1), what is the distribution of Y = F-generalized-inverse(U)?	Y has exactly the distribution F, i.e. Y has the same law as any X ~ F. This is the theoretical justification of the inversion method.	AKS simulation
If F is continuous, what is the distribution of F(X)?	F(X) ~ Uniform(0,1). This is the probability integral transform, and it is the reverse direction of the inversion method.<br><br>\[ F(X)\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1) \]	AKS simulation probability-integral-transform
Why is the probability integral transform so important in copula theory?	Because applying its own continuous CDF to each margin turns any random vector into one with uniform margins, which is exactly the domain on which a copula is defined.	AKS copulas
What is the general Monte Carlo simulation strategy for a random variable X?	Generate independent uniform u values, then transform each one via x = F-generalized-inverse(u). Independent u values produce independent x values.	AKS simulation
How do you simulate an exponential random variable by inversion?	Set F(x) = 1 - exp(-lambda x) equal to u and solve: x = -log(1-u)/lambda. Since 1-U is also uniform, x = -log(u)/lambda works equally well.<br><br>\[ F(x)=1-e^{-\lambda x}\ \Rightarrow\ x=-\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-u) \]	AKS simulation
What is the Pareto distribution used for in insurance, and what makes it special?	It models large claims. It is heavy-tailed, so it produces occasional extremely large losses that a normal or exponential model would essentially never generate.	AKS insurance heavy-tails
What is the difference between a Pareto and a shifted (Pareto 0 / Lomax) distribution in claim modelling?	The plain Pareto simulates large claims above a threshold t. The shifted version subtracts the threshold, so everything above the large-claim boundary is treated as an excess over that boundary.	AKS insurance heavy-tails
What is the Box-Muller method?	A way to generate standard normal variates from uniforms: take two independent uniforms, and combine sqrt(-2 log u1) with cos(2 pi u2) and sin(2 pi u2) to obtain two independent N(0,1) values.<br><br>\[ Z_1=\sqrt{-2\log u_1}\cos(2\pi u_2),\qquad Z_2=\sqrt{-2\log u_1}\sin(2\pi u_2) \]	AKS simulation
How do you simulate a general normal N(mu, sigma squared) from a standard normal?	Take Z ~ N(0,1) and set X = mu + sigma Z. Location-scale families only need one standard generator.<br><br>\[ X=\mu+\sigma Z,\qquad Z\sim N(0,1) \]	AKS simulation
How do you simulate a lognormal random variable?	Simulate a normal X ~ N(mu, sigma squared) and exponentiate: exp(X). Equivalently, log of a lognormal is normal.	AKS simulation distributions
What is the defining property of the lognormal distribution?	X is lognormal exactly when log(X) is normally distributed. It is positive-valued and right-skewed, which makes it a natural claim-size model.	AKS distributions insurance
How do you simulate a multivariate normal vector with mean mu and covariance Sigma?	Find a matrix A with A times A-transpose = Sigma (Cholesky), simulate a vector Z of independent standard normals, and set X = A Z + mu.<br><br>\[ X=AZ+\mu,\qquad AA^{\top}=\Sigma,\qquad Z\sim N_d(0,I) \]	AKS simulation multivariate
Why does the transformation X = A Z + mu give the right covariance?	Because Var(A Z + mu) = A Var(Z) A-transpose = A I A-transpose = A A-transpose = Sigma. The mean shift does not affect the covariance.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(AZ+\mu)=A\,I\,A^{\top}=AA^{\top}=\Sigma \]	AKS simulation multivariate
What is the Cholesky decomposition and what does it require?	A factorization of a symmetric positive definite matrix Sigma into a lower triangular A with A A-transpose = Sigma. It requires Sigma to be symmetric and positive definite.	AKS linear-algebra simulation
Is any linear transformation of a multivariate normal still normal?	Yes. If X is multivariate normal, then A X + b is multivariate normal too, with mean A mu + b and covariance A Sigma A-transpose. The normal family is closed under affine maps.	AKS multivariate
How do you simulate a Bernoulli(p) random variable from a uniform?	Return 1 if u is at most p, and 0 otherwise. The probability of the event {u <= p} is exactly p.	AKS simulation
How do you simulate a geometric random variable from a uniform?	Use the closed form floor(log(u) / log(1-p)); the number of failures before the first success has a geometric law derived directly from the inversion method.	AKS simulation
How do you simulate a binomial or negative binomial variable?	Simulate the corresponding independent Bernoulli or geometric variables and sum them. Both are sums of simpler independent building blocks.	AKS simulation
How do you simulate a Poisson random variable using exponential waiting times?	Generate independent Exp(lambda) inter-arrival times and count how many fit into one unit of time before the cumulative sum exceeds 1. That count is Poisson(lambda).<br><br>\[ N=\max\Big\{n:\ \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_i\le 1\Big\},\qquad E_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda) \]	AKS simulation poisson-process
What is the collective risk model in insurance?	Total loss S is the sum of N individual claim amounts X_i, where the claim number N is random and the claim sizes X_i are independent and identically distributed and independent of N.<br><br>\[ S=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_i \]	AKS insurance collective-model
What are the Wald identities for the collective model?	The expected total loss is E(S) = E(X) E(N). The variance is Var(S) = E(N) Var(X) + Var(N) E(X) squared. They decompose total risk into claim-size risk and claim-count risk.<br><br>\[ E(S)=E(N)\,E(X),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(S)=E(N)\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(N)\,E(X)^2 \]	AKS insurance collective-model
Why do we need the Wald identities rather than just E(N) E(X) for variance?	Because both the number of claims and their sizes fluctuate. The variance formula adds the contribution of the random claim count, which a naive product would miss.	AKS insurance collective-model
What is Value at Risk (VaR) at level alpha?	The quantile of the loss distribution: the loss level that is not exceeded with probability alpha. Expressed on results rather than losses, it is the negative outcome that is not undercut.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{VaR}_\alpha(X)=F_X^{-}(\alpha)=\inf\{x: F_X(x)\ge\alpha\} \]	AKS risk-measures
What is Risk Based Capital (RBC)?	The capital needed to cover a bad outcome beyond what is already expected: RBC equals VaR minus the expected loss. It is the unexpected part of the risk, since the expected part should already be funded by premiums.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{RBC}=\mathrm{VaR}_{1-\varepsilon}(S)-E(S) \]	AKS risk-measures capital
Why does RBC subtract the expected loss from the VaR?	Because the expected loss is the net premium and is already financed through pricing. Capital only needs to absorb the deviation above expectation.	AKS risk-measures capital
For a normally distributed loss, how does RBC simplify?	It becomes the standard normal quantile at level 1-epsilon times sigma, because the mean cancels when subtracting the expected value from the quantile. Capital is proportional to the standard deviation.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{RBC}=z_{1-\varepsilon}\,\sigma \]	AKS risk-measures normal
Why does the same simplification fail for the lognormal distribution?	Because the lognormal is not a location family: the mean and the quantile do not shift together, so the mean does not cancel out and RBC must be computed explicitly.	AKS risk-measures lognormal
What is the diversification effect in risk capital?	The capital required for a combined portfolio is smaller than the sum of the capitals of the parts, because the risks are not perfectly correlated and bad outcomes rarely coincide.	AKS risk-measures diversification
How is diversified capital allocated proportionally back to sub-portfolios?	Each part receives the total diversified capital scaled by its share of the sum of stand-alone capitals. This keeps allocations additive while passing the diversification benefit down.	AKS risk-measures capital-allocation
What is the empirical distribution function and what does it estimate?	The fraction of observations less than or equal to x. It estimates the true CDF F(x) and converges to it as the sample grows.<br><br>\[ \hat F_n(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{X_i\le x\} \]	AKS statistics estimation
Why is the empirical CDF a consistent estimator of F?	Because it is unbiased with expectation F(x), and its variance shrinks to zero as the sample size grows, so it converges in probability to F(x) at every point.	AKS statistics estimation
How do you estimate a quantile from a sample?	Sort the sample and pick the order statistic at the position corresponding to the desired probability level. The empirical quantile inverts the empirical CDF.	AKS statistics quantiles
What is the key distributional fact behind non-parametric confidence intervals for quantiles?	The number of observations falling below a fixed value is binomially distributed with success probability F(x). This turns quantile coverage into a binomial probability statement.	AKS statistics confidence-intervals
How do you build a non-parametric confidence interval for a quantile?	Choose two order statistics as endpoints, and pick their indices using the binomial distribution so that the coverage probability is at least 1 minus alpha. No distributional assumption is needed.	AKS statistics confidence-intervals
What is the Pearson correlation coefficient measuring?	The strength of the linear relationship between two variables, standardized by their standard deviations to lie between -1 and 1.<br><br>\[ \rho(X,Y)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}\in[-1,1] \]	AKS dependence correlation
What are the key limitations of Pearson correlation?	It only captures linear association, it is not invariant under non-linear transformations, and zero correlation does not imply independence except in the joint normal case.	AKS dependence correlation
When does Pearson correlation equal exactly 1?	Only when one variable is an exactly increasing linear function of the other. Correlation 1 is a statement about a perfect straight-line relationship.	AKS dependence correlation
In which model does Pearson correlation fully describe the dependence?	In the multivariate normal model. For the bivariate normal, the correlation parameter captures the entire dependence structure between the components.	AKS dependence correlation normal
What is Kendall's tau in words?	The probability of concordance minus the probability of discordance between two independent copies of a pair. It asks how often the two variables move in the same direction.<br><br>\[ \tau=P\big((X_1-X_2)(Y_1-Y_2)\gt 0\big)-P\big((X_1-X_2)(Y_1-Y_2)\lt 0\big) \]	AKS dependence rank-correlation
What does it mean for a pair of observations to be concordant?	Both coordinates move the same way: if one observation has a larger x, it also has a larger y. Discordant pairs move in opposite directions.	AKS dependence rank-correlation
What is Spearman's rho?	Pearson correlation applied to the ranks, equivalently the Pearson correlation of the transformed variables F_X(X) and F_Y(Y). It measures monotone rather than linear association.<br><br>\[ \rho_S=\rho\big(F_X(X),\,F_Y(Y)\big) \]	AKS dependence rank-correlation
What is the crucial invariance property of Kendall's tau and Spearman's rho?	They are invariant under strictly increasing transformations of the margins. Rescaling or log-transforming the variables does not change them, unlike Pearson correlation.	AKS dependence rank-correlation
Why is invariance under monotone transformation the right property for a dependence measure?	Because dependence should be a property of the coupling between variables, not of the units or scale in which each is measured. Only the copula should matter.	AKS dependence copulas
What is a copula?	A joint distribution function on the unit cube whose margins are all uniform on (0,1). It encodes the dependence structure separately from the marginal distributions.	AKS copulas
What are the defining properties of a two-dimensional copula?	It is zero if either argument is zero, it reduces to the other argument when one argument equals 1 (uniform margins), and it is 2-increasing so that all rectangle probabilities are non-negative.<br><br>\[ C(u,0)=C(0,v)=0,\qquad C(u,1)=u,\qquad C(1,v)=v \]	AKS copulas
What does Sklar's theorem state?	Every joint CDF can be written as a copula applied to its marginal CDFs. If the margins are continuous, that copula is unique.<br><br>\[ F(x_1,\dots,x_d)=C\big(F_1(x_1),\dots,F_d(x_d)\big) \]	AKS copulas sklar
Why is Sklar's theorem the central result of copula theory?	Because it splits any multivariate model cleanly into two independent choices: the marginal behaviour of each variable, and the dependence structure linking them.	AKS copulas sklar
How do you recover the copula from a known joint distribution?	Determine the marginal CDFs by letting the other arguments go to infinity, invert them, and plug those inverses into the joint CDF.	AKS copulas
How do you build a joint distribution from given margins and a copula?	Insert the marginal CDFs into the copula. This is the standard construction for building dependent models with prescribed margins.	AKS copulas
What is the independence copula?	The product of its arguments. It corresponds exactly to independent components, since the joint CDF then factorizes into the marginals.<br><br>\[ \Pi(u,v)=uv \]	AKS copulas
What is the comonotonicity copula?	The minimum of its arguments. It represents perfect positive dependence, where all components are increasing deterministic functions of one another.<br><br>\[ M(u,v)=\min(u,v) \]	AKS copulas
What is the countermonotonicity copula (2 dimensions only)?	The maximum of 0 and the sum of the arguments minus 1. It represents perfect negative dependence and only exists in two dimensions.<br><br>\[ W(u,v)=\max(u+v-1,\,0) \]	AKS copulas
Why does countermonotonicity not generalize beyond two dimensions?	Because three or more variables cannot all be pairwise perfectly negatively dependent at once; the constraint is not simultaneously satisfiable.	AKS copulas
What is the copula invariance theorem under monotone transformations?	If each component is transformed by a continuous, strictly increasing function, the copula of the transformed vector is unchanged. The copula sees only the ranks.	AKS copulas
What is the Gauss copula?	The copula of a multivariate normal distribution, obtained by plugging standard normal quantiles into the multivariate normal CDF. It is parameterized only by the correlation matrix.<br><br>\[ C^{Ga}_{R}(u,v)=\Phi_R\big(\Phi^{-1}(u),\,\Phi^{-1}(v)\big) \]	AKS copulas gauss-copula
Why does the Gauss copula not depend on the means and variances of the underlying normals?	Because standardizing the margins removes location and scale. Only the correlation structure survives the transformation to uniform margins.	AKS copulas gauss-copula
What is a meta-Gauss distribution?	A distribution whose copula is a Gauss copula but whose margins are arbitrary, for example lognormal. Normal dependence is combined with non-normal marginal behaviour.	AKS copulas meta-distributions
How do you simulate from a Gauss copula in two dimensions?	Simulate correlated standard normals (or build the correlation from independent normals) and then apply the standard normal CDF to each component to obtain uniforms.	AKS copulas simulation
How do you simulate a meta-Gauss model with lognormal margins?	Simulate uniforms from the Gauss copula, then transform each uniform through the inverse lognormal CDF using its own mu and sigma.	AKS copulas simulation
What is the t-copula and how is it constructed?	The copula of the multivariate t distribution, obtained by dividing a normal vector by the square root of an independent chi-squared variable scaled by its degrees of freedom, then applying t quantiles.	AKS copulas t-copula
What is the key practical advantage of the t-copula over the Gauss copula?	The t-copula has non-zero tail dependence, so extreme events happen together. The Gauss copula has zero tail dependence and understates joint extremes.	AKS copulas tail-dependence
What is an Archimedean copula?	A copula built from a single decreasing convex generator function, combining arguments by applying the generator, adding, and applying the inverse generator.<br><br>\[ C(u,v)=\varphi^{[-1]}\big(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)\big) \]	AKS copulas archimedean
What conditions must an Archimedean generator satisfy?	It maps 1 to 0, tends to infinity at 0, is strictly decreasing, and is convex. These guarantee the construction really produces a copula.	AKS copulas archimedean
Name the three standard Archimedean copulas and their character.	Gumbel (upper tail dependence only), Clayton (lower tail dependence only), and Frank (symmetric, no tail dependence). Each captures a different pattern of joint extremes.	AKS copulas archimedean
What is upper tail dependence?	The limiting conditional probability that one variable is extreme high given the other is extreme high. It measures whether large values cluster in the upper right corner.<br><br>\[ \lambda_U=\lim_{q\to 1^-}P\big(Y\gt F_Y^{-}(q)\ \big|\ X\gt F_X^{-}(q)\big) \]	AKS copulas tail-dependence
What is lower tail dependence?	The limiting conditional probability that one variable is extreme low given the other is extreme low. It measures joint clustering of small values.<br><br>\[ \lambda_L=\lim_{q\to 0^+}P\big(Y\le F_Y^{-}(q)\ \big|\ X\le F_X^{-}(q)\big) \]	AKS copulas tail-dependence
What does zero tail dependence mean?	Asymptotic independence in that tail: no matter how extreme, the two variables eventually stop occurring together. The Gauss copula has this property in both tails.	AKS copulas tail-dependence
Why is tail dependence critical in insurance and finance?	Because capital is driven by simultaneous extreme losses. A model with zero tail dependence systematically underestimates the probability of everything going wrong at once.	AKS copulas tail-dependence risk
How can copula parameters be estimated in practice?	Via the relationship between the copula parameter and a rank correlation: estimate Spearman's rho or Kendall's tau from data and invert the known analytic link.	AKS copulas estimation
What is a pseudo-observation in copula estimation?	The scaled ranks of the data, used as substitutes for the unobservable uniform variables. Dividing ranks by n+1 keeps them strictly inside the unit interval.	AKS copulas estimation
What is a copula density and how is it obtained?	The mixed second partial derivative of the copula with respect to its arguments. It is needed for likelihood-based estimation.	AKS copulas
What is a stochastic process?	A family of random variables indexed by time, used to model dynamics over a time horizon. A single realized path is called a sample path.	AKS stochastic-processes
What is the state space of a stochastic process?	The set of all values the process can take, the union of the ranges of the random variables over all time points.	AKS stochastic-processes
What is a Markov chain?	A process with discrete time and discrete state space in which the future depends on the past only through the present state. History before the current state is irrelevant.	AKS markov-chains
State the Markov property in words.	Conditional on the present state, the future is independent of the past. Knowing more history adds no predictive information.	AKS markov-chains
What is a transition probability p_ij?	The probability of moving from state i to state j in one step. The transition matrix collects these for all pairs of states.<br><br>\[ p_{ij}=P(X_{n+1}=j\mid X_n=i),\qquad \textstyle\sum_j p_{ij}=1 \]	AKS markov-chains
What makes a Markov chain homogeneous?	Transition probabilities do not depend on the time index; the same transition matrix applies at every step.	AKS markov-chains
What is the Chapman-Kolmogorov idea for k-step transitions?	The k-step transition matrix is the one-step matrix raised to the power k. Multi-step behaviour follows from repeated matrix multiplication.<br><br>\[ P^{(k)}=P^{k} \]	AKS markov-chains
How does the state distribution evolve in a Markov chain?	Multiply the current distribution vector by the transition matrix. Iterating gives the distribution after any number of steps.<br><br>\[ \pi_{n}^{\top}=\pi_0^{\top}P^{n} \]	AKS markov-chains
What are the three structural facts about eigenvalues of a transition matrix?	Each row sums to 1, the all-ones vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 1, and every eigenvalue has modulus at most 1. These constrain long-run behaviour.	AKS markov-chains eigenvalues
What is a stationary distribution of a Markov chain?	A probability vector that is unchanged by the transition matrix. Once the chain reaches it, the distribution stays fixed forever.<br><br>\[ \pi^{\top}P=\pi^{\top},\qquad \textstyle\sum_i\pi_i=1 \]	AKS markov-chains stationarity
When does the chain converge to a unique stationary distribution?	When 1 is the only eigenvalue of modulus 1. Then the distribution converges to the corresponding left eigenvector regardless of the starting point.	AKS markov-chains stationarity
Why is the stationary distribution a left eigenvector of the transition matrix?	Because stationarity means the distribution vector times the transition matrix returns the same vector, which is exactly the left eigenvector equation for eigenvalue 1.	AKS markov-chains stationarity
How is a matrix power computed via diagonalization?	Write the matrix as B times a diagonal eigenvalue matrix times B-inverse; then the power only raises the diagonal entries to that power. Eigen-decomposition turns matrix powers into scalar powers.	AKS linear-algebra
What is a matrix exponential and how is it defined?	The power series sum of matrix powers divided by factorials, exactly mirroring the scalar exponential series. Via diagonalization it exponentiates the eigenvalues.	AKS linear-algebra
What is a Markov process (continuous time)?	The continuous-time analogue of a Markov chain: continuous time index, discrete state space, and the future depends on the past only through the present state.	AKS markov-processes
What is the intensity matrix (generator) Q of a Markov process?	The derivative of the transition matrix at time zero. It describes the instantaneous rates of moving between states, and it generates the transition matrix as a matrix exponential of Q times t.<br><br>\[ Q=\left.\tfrac{d}{dt}P(t)\right|_{t=0},\qquad \textstyle\sum_j q_{ij}=0 \]	AKS markov-processes generator
What are the structural constraints on the generator matrix Q?	Every row sums to zero, diagonal entries are non-positive, and off-diagonal entries are non-negative. Outflow from a state must equal the total inflow rates to other states.	AKS markov-processes generator
How is the transition matrix recovered from the generator?	As the matrix exponential of Q times t. The generator encodes the whole time evolution.<br><br>\[ P(t)=\exp(Qt)=\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{(Qt)^k}{k!} \]	AKS markov-processes generator
What is the holding time in a state of a Markov process?	The time until the process leaves the state. It is exponentially distributed with rate equal to the absolute value of that state's diagonal generator entry.<br><br>\[ T_i\sim\mathrm{Exp}(|q_{ii}|),\qquad E(T_i)=\frac{1}{|q_{ii}|} \]	AKS markov-processes
What is an absorbing state?	A state with zero exit rate, so the expected holding time is infinite and the process never leaves once it enters. Death or default states are modelled this way.	AKS markov-processes
Given that a Markov process jumps, what determines the destination?	The jump probability to another state is that state's transition rate divided by the total exit rate from the current state. Rates are normalized into probabilities.<br><br>\[ P(i\to j)=\frac{q_{ij}}{|q_{ii}|},\qquad j\neq i \]	AKS markov-processes
What is a Poisson process?	A counting process with continuous time and discrete states that starts at zero, is monotonically increasing, and has independent and stationary increments, with N(t) ~ Poisson(lambda t).<br><br>\[ N(t)\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda t),\qquad P(N(t)=k)=e^{-\lambda t}\frac{(\lambda t)^k}{k!} \]	AKS poisson-process
What are independent increments?	Changes of the process over disjoint time intervals are independent random variables. What happened earlier gives no information about the next interval's change.	AKS stochastic-processes
What are stationary increments?	The distribution of a change depends only on the length of the time interval, not on where the interval sits on the time axis.	AKS stochastic-processes
What is the distribution of inter-arrival times in a Poisson process?	Independent exponential with rate lambda. The memoryless exponential waiting time is what makes the counting process Poisson.	AKS poisson-process
What is the distribution of the time until the n-th event of a Poisson process?	Gamma (Erlang) with shape n and rate lambda, since it is the sum of n independent exponentials.<br><br>\[ T_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_i\sim\Gamma(n,\lambda) \]	AKS poisson-process
What happens when you superimpose two independent Poisson processes?	The sum is again a Poisson process, with intensity equal to the sum of the two intensities. Merging claim streams stays Poisson.<br><br>\[ N_1+N_2\sim\mathrm{PP}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2) \]	AKS poisson-process
What is the thinning (decomposition) property of the Poisson process?	If each event is independently marked a success with probability p, the successes form a Poisson process with intensity p times lambda, and the failures form an independent Poisson process with intensity (1-p) lambda.<br><br>\[ \mathrm{PP}(\lambda)\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{PP}(p\lambda)\ \perp\ \mathrm{PP}((1-p)\lambda) \]	AKS poisson-process thinning
Why is the thinning property useful in insurance?	It lets you split a claim arrival stream into independent sub-streams, for example large versus small claims, each of which remains a tractable Poisson process.	AKS poisson-process insurance
What defines Brownian motion (the Wiener process)?	It starts at zero, has almost surely continuous paths, has independent increments, and increments over a time span s are normally distributed with mean 0 and variance s.<br><br>\[ W(0)=0,\qquad W(t)-W(s)\sim N(0,\,t-s) \]	AKS brownian-motion
What is the covariance structure of Brownian motion?	Cov(W(t), W(s)) equals the minimum of t and s. Overlapping time spans share exactly the variance accumulated in their common interval.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}\big(W(t),W(s)\big)=\min(t,s) \]	AKS brownian-motion
What are the two paradoxical path properties of Brownian motion?	Its paths are everywhere continuous but nowhere differentiable. This is precisely why ordinary calculus fails and stochastic calculus is needed.	AKS brownian-motion
Is Brownian motion a Markov process?	Yes. Because increments are independent, the future depends on the past only through the current position.	AKS brownian-motion markov
Why is a linear combination of Brownian motion values at two times normal?	Because it can be rewritten as a combination of independent increments, and any linear combination of independent normals is normal.	AKS brownian-motion
What is Brownian motion with drift?	A process combining a deterministic linear trend mu times t with a scaled Brownian motion sigma times W(t). It models a value that trends while fluctuating randomly.<br><br>\[ X(t)=\mu t+\sigma W(t) \]	AKS brownian-motion
What is geometric Brownian motion and why is it used for stock prices?	The exponential of Brownian motion with drift. It stays positive and produces lognormally distributed values with independent relative (percentage) returns, matching how prices actually behave.<br><br>\[ S(t)=S(0)\exp\!\Big(\big(\mu-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\big)t+\sigma W(t)\Big) \]	AKS brownian-motion finance
What is the distribution of the ratio S(t)/S(0) under geometric Brownian motion?	Lognormal. Consequently log returns are normally distributed and non-overlapping relative increments are independent.	AKS brownian-motion finance
How do you simulate a Brownian motion path on a grid?	Add independent normal increments with variance equal to the step size at each step, that is, multiply standard normals by the square root of delta t.<br><br>\[ W(t_{k+1})=W(t_k)+\sqrt{\Delta t}\;Z_k,\qquad Z_k\sim N(0,1) \]	AKS simulation brownian-motion
Why does the increment scale with the square root of the time step?	Because variance grows linearly in time, so the standard deviation, which is what multiplies the standard normal, grows like the square root of time.	AKS brownian-motion simulation
What is the motivation for the Ito integral?	Brownian paths are nowhere differentiable, so classical Riemann-Stieltjes integration with respect to dW is impossible. A new integration concept is required.	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
How is the Ito integral defined for step processes?	As a sum of the process values on each interval multiplied by the corresponding Brownian increments. General integrands are then approximated by step processes and a limit is taken.	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is the expectation of an Ito integral?	Zero, because each Brownian increment has zero mean and is independent of the integrand evaluated before it.<br><br>\[ E\!\left[\int_0^T H_s\,dW_s\right]=0 \]	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is the Ito isometry?	The expectation of the square of an Ito integral equals the expectation of the integral of the squared integrand. It converts a stochastic second moment into an ordinary integral.<br><br>\[ E\!\left[\Big(\int_0^T H_s\,dW_s\Big)^{\!2}\right]=E\!\left[\int_0^T H_s^2\,ds\right] \]	AKS stochastic-calculus ito-isometry
What is the distribution of the Ito integral of a deterministic function against dW?	Normal with mean zero and variance equal to the ordinary integral of the squared function. Deterministic integrands preserve normality.	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is the classic non-classical result for the integral of W against dW?	It equals half of W(t) squared minus t/2. The extra minus t/2 term is precisely what distinguishes Ito calculus from ordinary calculus.<br><br>\[ \int_0^t W_s\,dW_s=\tfrac{1}{2}W_t^2-\tfrac{t}{2} \]	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is an Ito process?	A process written as an initial value plus a drift integral against dt plus a diffusion integral against dW, usually abbreviated in differential form as dX = D dt + V dW.<br><br>\[ dX_t=D_t\,dt+V_t\,dW_t \]	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is Ito's formula and what is it the analogue of?	The chain rule of stochastic calculus. When transforming an Ito process by a smooth function, an additional second-derivative term appears that is absent in ordinary calculus.<br><br>\[ df(t,X_t)=\Big(f_t+D_tf_x+\tfrac{1}{2}V_t^2f_{xx}\Big)dt+V_tf_x\,dW_t \]	AKS stochastic-calculus ito-formula
Why does Ito's formula contain a second derivative term?	Because the quadratic variation of Brownian motion is not negligible: squared increments accumulate proportionally to time rather than vanishing.	AKS stochastic-calculus ito-formula
What is the Ito product rule?	The differential of a product of two Ito processes includes the two usual terms plus a cross-variation correction term from the two diffusion parts.	AKS stochastic-calculus ito
What is a stochastic differential equation (SDE)?	An equation specifying the drift and diffusion coefficients of a process, dX = b(t,X) dt + sigma(t,X) dW. It describes the local dynamics of a random evolution.<br><br>\[ dX_t=b(t,X_t)\,dt+\sigma(t,X_t)\,dW_t \]	AKS stochastic-calculus sde
How is an SDE typically solved?	Guess a transformation, apply Ito's formula, and choose it so the resulting equation has constant or vanishing coefficients that can be integrated directly.	AKS stochastic-calculus sde
What is the Euler-Maruyama scheme for simulating an SDE?	Step forward by adding the drift times delta t plus the diffusion times a normal increment with standard deviation the square root of delta t. It is the stochastic analogue of the explicit Euler method.<br><br>\[ X_{k+1}=X_k+b(t_k,X_k)\Delta t+\sigma(t_k,X_k)\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_k \]	AKS simulation sde
What is the Black-Scholes model for a stock price?	A model where the relative price change has constant expected return mu and constant volatility sigma, leading to the SDE dS = mu S dt + sigma S dW and a geometric Brownian motion solution.<br><br>\[ dS_t=\mu S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t \]	AKS finance black-scholes
Which Black-Scholes assumption is most obviously unrealistic?	Constant volatility. Real markets show volatility clustering and time-varying, state-dependent volatility.	AKS finance black-scholes
What is a short-rate model?	A model of the instantaneous short-term interest rate as a diffusion process, used to build interest and bond dynamics.	AKS finance interest-rates
What is mean reversion in an interest rate model?	A drift that pulls the rate back toward a long-run level b at a speed a. When the rate is above b the drift is negative, and below b it is positive.<br><br>\[ dr_t=a(b-r_t)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t \]	AKS finance mean-reversion
What is the difference between the Vasicek and CIR models?	Both are mean reverting, but Vasicek has constant volatility and can produce negative rates, while CIR scales volatility by the square root of the rate, which keeps rates non-negative.<br><br>\[ \text{Vasicek: }\sigma\,dW_t\qquad\text{CIR: }\sigma\sqrt{r_t}\,dW_t \]	AKS finance interest-rates
What is the Black-Karasinski model idea?	Model the logarithm of the interest rate with a mean-reverting SDE. Exponentiating guarantees strictly positive rates.	AKS finance interest-rates
What are the three main risk categories of an insurance company?	Premium risk (premiums insufficient for the services rendered), reserve risk (reserves for past claims inadequate), and market risk (adverse price changes on financial markets).	AKS insurance risk-categories
What is premium risk?	The risk that the premiums charged this year do not cover the claims and costs actually incurred. It is a risk about the current underwriting year.	AKS insurance risk-categories
What is reserve risk?	The risk that the reserves set aside for claims from past years turn out to be insufficient as those claims develop. It concerns prior accident years.	AKS insurance risk-categories reserving
What is the return period (Wiederkehrperiode) of a loss level?	The average number of years between exceedances of that level. It is the reciprocal of the annual exceedance probability, so a 1 percent event has a 100-year return period.<br><br>\[ T=\frac{1}{P(S\gt s)} \]	AKS insurance risk-measures
What is a run-off triangle (Abwicklungsdreieck)?	A table of cumulative claim amounts by accident year and development year. Rows are accident years, and each row is observed only up to the present, which produces the triangular shape.	AKS insurance reserving
What is the reserve for an accident year?	The difference between the estimated ultimate claim amount and the amount already paid or reported. It is what still needs to be set aside.	AKS insurance reserving
What is the difference between paid, incurred and ultimate claims?	Paid is money already disbursed, incurred is paid plus case reserves, and ultimate is the final total claim amount once everything is settled.	AKS insurance reserving
What is a best estimate reserve?	The expected value of the outstanding claims, without safety loadings. It is the statistically unbiased central estimate.	AKS insurance reserving
How does an HGB reserve differ from a fair value reserve?	The HGB reserve is deliberately conservative and includes prudence margins, while a fair value reserve is the discounted present value of the expected future payments.	AKS insurance reserving accounting
What are the three assumptions of the stochastic Chain Ladder model?	Different accident years are independent, the expected development factor depends only on the development year, and the conditional variance is proportional to the current claim amount with a development-year-specific factor.	AKS insurance chain-ladder
What is a Chain Ladder development factor?	The ratio by which cumulative claims grow from one development year to the next, estimated as a weighted average across accident years using the claim amounts as weights.<br><br>\[ \hat f_j=\frac{\sum_{i} C_{i,j+1}}{\sum_{i} C_{i,j}} \]	AKS insurance chain-ladder
Under which Chain Ladder assumptions is the reserve estimator unbiased?	Under the first two assumptions (independence of accident years and constant development factors). The third variance assumption is only needed to quantify the estimation error.	AKS insurance chain-ladder
What does the mean squared error of the Chain Ladder reserve measure?	The total uncertainty of the reserve, combining the random fluctuation of future claims with the error in estimating the development factors.	AKS insurance chain-ladder
How is reserve risk simulated in practice?	Fit a distribution (typically lognormal) to the reserve using the best estimate as the mean and the mean squared error as the variance, then draw simulated ultimate outcomes.	AKS insurance reserving simulation
How do you convert a target mean and variance into lognormal parameters?	Solve the moment equations backwards: sigma squared comes from the log of one plus the squared coefficient of variation, and mu from the log of the mean minus half of sigma squared.<br><br>\[ \sigma^2=\log\!\Big(1+\frac{v}{m^2}\Big),\qquad \mu=\log m-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2} \]	AKS distributions lognormal
What is a run-off result (Abwicklungsergebnis)?	The difference between the reserve set at the start of the year and the sum of payments made plus the reserve at the end. A negative run-off gain means the reserves were insufficient.	AKS insurance reserving
How is the basic claim burden modelled in premium risk?	As a loss ratio: claims divided by exposure volume, typically modelled as lognormal with a variance that shrinks as volume grows. Larger portfolios have proportionally more stable ratios.	AKS insurance premium-risk
Why does the variance of the loss ratio decrease with portfolio volume?	Because of diversification across many independent policies: averaging more risks reduces relative fluctuation.	AKS insurance diversification
How is the large claim burden modelled?	With a collective model: a Poisson number of large claims combined with Pareto-distributed severities above a threshold. Frequency and severity are modelled separately.	AKS insurance large-claims
What is the maximum likelihood estimator for the Pareto tail index?	The number of exceedances divided by the sum of the log ratios of each claim to the threshold. It is the classic Hill-type estimator.<br><br>\[ \hat\alpha=\frac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(X_i/t)} \]	AKS insurance heavy-tails estimation
What is a Hill plot and how is it read?	A plot of the estimated tail index against the number of largest claims used. You choose a region where the estimate is stable, balancing bias from including too many claims against variance from too few.	AKS insurance heavy-tails estimation
What is an event (catastrophe) loss and why does it need separate modelling?	A loss caused by a single event affecting many policies at once, such as a storm. Individual claims are small but no longer independent, so the independence assumption of the collective model breaks down.	AKS insurance catastrophe
What are the two approaches to modelling catastrophe losses?	A mathematical approach applying a collective model per event, and a geophysical exposure-based approach that models the physical hazard and the portfolio exposed to it.	AKS insurance catastrophe
What is a time series?	A family of random variables indexed by a discrete time range. A single realization of that family is the observed data series.	AKS time-series
What is the component model of a time series?	Decomposition into a trend, a seasonal component and an irregular disturbance term. Each part is estimated and removed in turn.	AKS time-series decomposition
How is a polynomial trend removed without seasonality?	By differencing. First-order differencing removes a linear trend; a polynomial of degree k requires differencing k times.<br><br>\[ \Delta Y_t=Y_t-Y_{t-1} \]	AKS time-series detrending
What is the effect of the difference operator on a power of t?	Differencing k times reduces the degree by k, so it turns t to the power k into a constant and annihilates any lower power. This is why differencing kills polynomial trends.	AKS time-series detrending
Why does first-order differencing of a linear trend leave a well-behaved series?	The trend collapses to the constant slope, and the remaining term is the difference of two error terms, which has zero mean and twice the original variance.	AKS time-series detrending
How is a seasonal component estimated with moving averages?	Smooth the series with a moving average whose window is one full period, which averages out the seasonal effect, then subtract the smoothed series to isolate the seasonal pattern.	AKS time-series seasonality
Why does a moving average over a full period remove seasonality?	Because seasonal effects sum to zero over one complete period, so averaging over exactly one period cancels them out.	AKS time-series seasonality
What is the four-step procedure for monthly data with trend and season?	Compute moving averages, estimate the trend component, estimate and center the twelve seasonal figures, then take residuals as the observed value minus trend minus season.	AKS time-series decomposition
What is the alternative to sequential decomposition of a time series?	Fit trend and season simultaneously in one linear regression with a time variable plus dummy variables for the seasons, with the constraint that the seasonal coefficients sum to zero.	AKS time-series regression
What does it mean for a time series to be (weakly) stationary?	The mean is constant over time, and the covariance between two observations depends only on the lag between them, not on their absolute position in time.	AKS time-series stationarity
What is the autocorrelation function?	The correlation between observations separated by a given lag, standardized to lie between -1 and 1. It summarizes the memory structure of the series.<br><br>\[ \rho(\ell)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_t,Y_{t+\ell})}{\operatorname{Var}(Y_t)} \]	AKS time-series acf
Is a random walk stationary?	No. Although its mean is zero, its variance and covariance grow with t, violating the requirement that covariances depend only on the lag.	AKS time-series stationarity random-walk
What is the partial autocorrelation function?	The correlation between observations at a given lag after removing the influence of all intermediate observations. It isolates direct rather than indirect dependence.	AKS time-series pacf
What is an AR(p) process?	An autoregressive process where the current value is a linear combination of the p previous values plus white noise. The series predicts itself from its own past.<br><br>\[ Y_t=\alpha_1Y_{t-1}+\dots+\alpha_pY_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t \]	AKS time-series arma
When is an AR(1) process stationary?	When the absolute value of the autoregressive coefficient is strictly less than 1. Then shocks decay geometrically instead of accumulating.<br><br>\[ |\alpha|\lt 1 \]	AKS time-series arma stationarity
What is the autocorrelation structure of a stationary AR(1)?	The autocorrelation at lag l is the coefficient raised to the power l, so it decays geometrically but never reaches exactly zero.<br><br>\[ \rho(\ell)=\alpha^{\ell} \]	AKS time-series arma
What is an MA(q) process?	A moving average process where the current value is a weighted sum of the last q white-noise shocks plus the current shock.<br><br>\[ Y_t=\varepsilon_t+\beta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\dots+\beta_q\varepsilon_{t-q} \]	AKS time-series arma
Is every MA(q) process stationary?	Yes. It is a finite linear combination of white-noise terms, so its mean and its lag-dependent covariances are automatically constant in time.	AKS time-series arma stationarity
What is the signature autocorrelation pattern of an MA(q) process?	The autocorrelation is exactly zero for lags beyond q, while the partial autocorrelation decays gradually. This is the mirror image of the AR pattern.	AKS time-series arma identification
How do ACF and PACF distinguish AR from MA processes?	An AR(p) has PACF cutting off after lag p with gradually decaying ACF; an MA(q) has ACF cutting off after lag q with gradually decaying PACF.	AKS time-series identification
What is the maximum possible lag-1 autocorrelation of an MA(1) process?	One half. So if the observed lag-1 autocorrelation exceeds 0.5, an MA(1) model cannot be correct.<br><br>\[ |\rho(1)|=\frac{|\beta|}{1+\beta^2}\le\tfrac{1}{2} \]	AKS time-series arma
What is an ARMA(p,q) process?	A combination of an autoregressive part and a moving average part. Its stationarity is governed entirely by the AR component.	AKS time-series arma
What is an ARIMA(p,d,q) process?	A process that becomes ARMA(p,q) after differencing d times. It handles non-stationary series with a stochastic or polynomial trend.	AKS time-series arima
What is a SARIMA model?	An ARIMA model extended with a second seasonal set of autoregressive, differencing and moving average terms operating at the seasonal lag.	AKS time-series arima seasonality
What are the three steps of fitting an ARIMA model?	Identify the orders p, d and q from ACF and PACF, estimate the parameters, and then check the model by testing the residuals.	AKS time-series arima modelling
What do the Ljung-Box and Box-Pierce tests check?	Whether the residuals are uncorrelated (white noise). The null hypothesis is independence, so a large p-value indicates a well-specified model.	AKS time-series diagnostics
What does a small p-value in a Ljung-Box test tell you?	That residual autocorrelation remains, meaning the model has not captured all the structure and should be respecified.	AKS time-series diagnostics
What is the conditional distribution of one component of a bivariate normal given the other?	Normal, with a mean shifted by the correlation times the ratio of standard deviations times the deviation of the conditioning variable, and a variance reduced by a factor of 1 minus rho squared.<br><br>\[ X\mid Y=y\ \sim\ N\!\Big(\mu_X+\rho\tfrac{\sigma_X}{\sigma_Y}(y-\mu_Y),\ \sigma_X^2(1-\rho^2)\Big) \]	AKS multivariate normal
Why does conditioning in a bivariate normal always reduce variance?	Because the factor 1 minus rho squared is at most 1. Any correlation, positive or negative, provides information and shrinks the remaining uncertainty.	AKS multivariate normal
What is the mixture construction for combining copulas?	A convex combination of two copulas is again a copula, and its tail dependence coefficients are the same convex combination of the individual coefficients. This lets you dial in a chosen level of tail dependence.	AKS copulas
What is the relationship between covariance and correlation?	Covariance equals correlation times the product of the two standard deviations. Correlation is the scale-free standardized version of covariance.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\rho\,\sigma_X\sigma_Y \]	AKS statistics
What is the variance of a sum of two dependent variables?	The sum of the two variances plus twice their covariance. The covariance term is exactly what diversification exploits when it is negative or small.<br><br>\[ \operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) \]	AKS statistics diversification
What is a Brownian bridge in terms of drift and diffusion?	The special case of an Ito process with zero drift and unit diffusion in its differential form, which pins the process down at its endpoints.	AKS stochastic-processes
